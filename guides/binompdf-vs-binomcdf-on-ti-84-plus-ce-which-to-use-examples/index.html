binompdf vs binomcdf on TI-84 Plus CE: Which to Use (Examples)

How to choose binompdf or binomcdf on a TI-84 Plus CE, with a table for "at least," "at most," "more than" and "between." Includes key-by-key steps, worked answers and practice problems.

binompdf gives the probability of exactly one count. binomcdf gives the probability of that count or fewer, added up from 0.

Most wrong answers come from the wording of the question, not from the calculator. "At least 6" and "more than 6" need different numbers typed in. The table below covers every common phrase, followed by keystrokes and practice problems you can check against the answers.

Keys are written in brackets, like [2nd]. This is an independent guide, not affiliated with or endorsed by Texas Instruments.

First, check that the problem is binomial

Use these functions only if all four are true:

  • There is a fixed number of trials (n).
  • Each trial has two outcomes, success or failure.
  • The probability of success (p) is the same on every trial.
  • The trials are independent.

If you are counting trials until the first success, that is geometric, not binomial. If you sample without replacement from a small group, the trials aren't independent. Classes commonly accept binomial as an approximation when the sample is no more than 10% of the population.

The translation table

X is the number of successes, and k is the number in the question. The third column shows what to type.

| The question says | Probability | Type this | |---|---|---| | exactly k | P(X = k) | binompdf(n, p, k) | | at most k, no more than k, k or fewer | P(X ≤ k) | binomcdf(n, p, k) | | fewer than k, less than k | P(X < k) | binomcdf(n, p, k−1) | | at least k, no fewer than k, k or more | P(X ≥ k) | 1 − binomcdf(n, p, k−1) | | more than k, greater than k | P(X > k) | 1 − binomcdf(n, p, k) | | from a to b, including both | P(a ≤ X ≤ b) | binomcdf(n, p, b) − binomcdf(n, p, a−1) | | between a and b, not including either | P(a < X < b) | binomcdf(n, p, b−1) − binomcdf(n, p, a) |

Why the k−1 appears: binomcdf only adds from 0 up to a number. To get "at least 6," find everything you don't want, which is 0 through 5, and subtract it from 1. The excluded group stops at 5, so you type 5.

Quick check: write out the counts on a number line and circle the ones the question wants. If you are subtracting, the number you type is the last count that is not circled.

Step-by-step: binompdf with Stat Wizards on

Example: A player makes 30% of her three-point shots and takes 20 independent shots. What is the probability she makes exactly 6?

Here, n = 20, p = 0.3 and k = 6.

  1. Press [2nd] [VARS] to open the DISTR menu.
  2. Press [ALPHA] [MATH] to jump to A:binompdf(. You can also scroll to it and press [ENTER].
  3. Fill in the wizard, pressing [ENTER] or the down arrow after each entry:
  • trials: 20
  • p: .3
  • x value: 6
  1. Highlight Paste and press [ENTER]. The command appears on the home screen.
  2. Press [ENTER] again to calculate.

Answer: 0.1916 (your screen shows more digits).

Step-by-step: binomcdf

Same player: what is the probability she makes at most 6?

  1. Press [2nd] [VARS].
  2. Press [ALPHA] [APPS] to jump to B:binomcdf(.
  3. Enter trials 20, p .3, x value 6.
  4. Highlight Paste, then press [ENTER] twice.

Answer: 0.6080

The letter shortcuts work because the green letter above [MATH] is A and the one above [APPS] is B. Before pressing [ENTER], make sure the top of the wizard says the function you meant to choose.

With Stat Wizards off

If the menu drops a bare binompdf( on the home screen instead of opening a form, type the values in order, separated by commas:

binompdf(20,.3,6)

The order is always n, p, k. To switch the forms back on, press [MODE] and find STAT WIZARDS.

How to do "at least" and "more than"

Same player: what is the probability she makes at least 6?

From the table, P(X ≥ 6) = 1 − binomcdf(20, .3, 5).

  1. On the home screen, press [1], then the subtraction key [−]. Do not use the negative key [(-)].
  2. Press [2nd] [VARS] [ALPHA] [APPS].
  3. Enter trials 20, p .3, x value 5.
  4. Paste, then press [ENTER].

Answer: 0.5836

Backup method: calculate binomcdf(20,.3,5) by itself, then press [1] [−] [2nd] [(-)] [ENTER]. [2nd] [(-)] is Ans, so this works out 1 − Ans. Press the 1 first. If you start with [−], the calculator writes Ans − at the start and you'll get the wrong expression.

How to do a range

What is the probability she makes from 4 to 8 shots, including both?

Type: binomcdf(20,.3,8) − binomcdf(20,.3,3)

The first part covers 0 through 8. The second removes 0 through 3, which leaves 4 through 8. Use the wizard twice with [−] pressed between the two pastes, or type the whole line with the wizards off.

Answer: 0.7796

All the answers for this example

n = 20, p = 0.3:

  • P(X = 6) = 0.1916
  • P(X ≤ 6) = 0.6080
  • P(X < 6) = 0.4164
  • P(X ≥ 6) = 0.5836
  • P(X > 6) = 0.3920
  • P(4 ≤ X ≤ 8) = 0.7796

Two checks you can run. P(X < 6) plus P(X = 6) should equal P(X ≤ 6), and 0.4164 + 0.1916 = 0.6080. Also, P(X ≥ 6) and P(X < 6) should add up to 1.

Common mistakes and error messages

  • Used 6 instead of 5 for "at least 6." This drops 6 from the answer. It's the most common error with these functions, so use the table.
  • Typed the percent as a whole number. A p of 30 instead of .3 usually gives ERR:DOMAIN, because p has to be between 0 and 1.
  • Pressed [(-)] instead of [−]. "1 (-) binomcdf(" gives ERR:SYNTAX. The negative key is the small one next to [ENTER]. Subtraction is the blue-side key above [+].
  • Rounded a repeating p. If p is 1/3, type 1/3 in the p field rather than .33. Your answer will be more accurate.
  • Mixed up n and k. In a "20 shots, 6 makes" problem, 20 is trials and 6 is the x value.
  • Used the wrong success. If the question counts misses, p must be the probability of a miss, 0.7 here, not 0.3.
  • Got an answer with E in it, such as 1.2E-5. That's scientific notation for 0.000012, not an error. The event is just very unlikely.
  • Got the wrong number after menu scrolling. Scrolling past B lands on the Poisson functions. Read the title at the top of the wizard.

What to write on a test or AP free-response

Calculator syntax on its own is usually not enough. Your teacher or an AP Statistics reader needs the setup, not just "binomcdf(20,.3,5)." Name the distribution, its parameters and the event, then give the answer:

Let X = the number of shots made. X is binomial with n = 20 and p = 0.3. P(X ≥ 6) = 1 − P(X ≤ 5) = 0.5836.

Check your own teacher's rules, because expectations on showing work and rounding differ.

Practice problems

Choose the function and the number to type before you press any keys.

  1. n = 12, p = 0.25. Exactly 3 successes.
  2. n = 15, p = 0.6. At most 8 successes.
  3. n = 10, p = 0.9. Fewer than 8 successes.
  4. n = 25, p = 0.2. At least 7 successes.
  5. n = 18, p = 0.5. From 7 to 11 successes, inclusive.
  6. n = 8, p = 0.4. More than 5 successes.

Answers

  1. binompdf(12,.25,3) = 0.2581
  2. binomcdf(15,.6,8) = 0.3902
  3. binomcdf(10,.9,7) = 0.0702
  4. 1 − binomcdf(25,.2,6) = 0.2200
  5. binomcdf(18,.5,11) − binomcdf(18,.5,6) = 0.7621
  6. 1 − binomcdf(8,.4,5) = 0.0498
