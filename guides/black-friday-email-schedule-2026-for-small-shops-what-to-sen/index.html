Black Friday Email Schedule 2026 for Small Shops: What to Send When

A day-by-day Black Friday to Cyber Monday 2026 email calendar for solo and small online shops, with what each email should say, sample subject lines, a fill-in template and a pre-send checklist.

If you run a small shop on Shopify, Etsy, Squarespace or WooCommerce and send your own emails, the hard part of Black Friday usually isn't the discount. It's deciding how many emails to send, on which days, and what each one should say. This page gives you a full calendar for 2026 that you can copy into your email tool today.

The 2026 dates to know:

  • Thanksgiving (US): Thursday, November 26
  • Black Friday: Friday, November 27
  • Small Business Saturday: Saturday, November 28
  • Cyber Monday: Monday, November 30
  • Giving Tuesday: Tuesday, December 1

How many emails should a small shop send?

You don't need a big-brand sequence. For most small shops, 7 to 9 emails over about two weeks is plenty. It's enough to warn people the sale is coming, launch it, remind them, and close it, without sending something every day for a month.

If your list is small or doesn't hear from you often, start with the shorter "minimum" version below. You can add more emails next year once you've seen your own open and unsubscribe numbers.

The full 2026 calendar (9 emails)

Your email platform (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Flodesk, Shopify Email, Kit and others) will show you when your own subscribers tend to open. Use that if you have it. If you don't, mid-morning in your customers' main time zone is a reasonable place to start.

  1. Tue Nov 17: "Something's coming" teaser. Tell people a sale is on the way and give the date. Don't reveal the full offer yet. If you have a VIP or early-access list, invite people to join it here.
  2. Tue Nov 24: Gift guide or "what to get" email. Send a short, useful roundup of 3 to 6 products grouped by person or budget ("for the coffee lover", "under $30"). This one sells even without a discount.
  3. Wed Nov 25: Early access (optional). Open the sale a day early for VIPs or your whole list. People who are already loyal like getting a head start, and it spreads your orders out.
  4. Fri Nov 27, morning: Black Friday launch. This is your most important email. Put the offer, the code (or "no code needed") and the end date in the first two lines.
  5. Sat Nov 28: Small Business Saturday. Be personal. Mention who makes the products, packs the orders or answers the emails. Then remind people the sale is still on.
  6. Sun Nov 29: Quiet reminder (optional). A short "in case you missed it" note. Leave it out if your list is small or tends to unsubscribe when you send a lot.
  7. Mon Nov 30, morning: Cyber Monday. Either extend the same offer ("still on through Monday") or add a small extra, like free shipping or a bonus item.
  8. Mon Nov 30, evening: Last call. "Ends tonight at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]." Keep it very short. One line, one button.
  9. Tue Dec 1 or Wed Dec 2: Thank you / extended / ship-by reminder. Thank people, mention any extension honestly, and remind them of your last order date for holiday delivery.

The minimum version (5 emails)

If you're short on time, send just these:

  1. Tue Nov 24: Teaser plus gift guide combined
  2. Fri Nov 27: Black Friday launch
  3. Sat Nov 28: Small Business Saturday
  4. Mon Nov 30, morning: Cyber Monday
  5. Mon Nov 30, evening: Last call

What every sale email needs

Each email in the sequence should answer these five questions within a few seconds:

  • What's the offer? ("25% off everything", "Free gift over $50")
  • How do I get it? (The code, or "applied at checkout")
  • When does it end? (Day, time and time zone)
  • What's excluded? (Gift cards, sale items, custom orders and so on)
  • When do I need to order for holiday delivery? (Your ship-by date)

If someone has to scroll to find any of these, move them higher up.

Subject line ideas by day

Treat these as starting points and change the wording to sound like you:

  • Teaser: "Mark Nov 27 on your calendar" / "Our biggest sale of the year is almost here"
  • Gift guide: "Gifts for everyone on your list (under $40)" / "Stuck on what to give? Start here"
  • Early access: "You're in early: the sale starts now for you" / "VIP access is open"
  • Black Friday: "[OFFER] starts now" / "It's here: [OFFER] on everything"
  • Small Business Saturday: "From our workshop to your door" / "Thanks for shopping small"
  • Reminder: "In case you missed it" / "Still on: [OFFER]"
  • Cyber Monday: "Cyber Monday: [OFFER] + free shipping" / "One more day"
  • Last call: "Ends tonight at midnight" / "Last few hours"
  • Thank you: "Thank you (and one last date to know)" / "Order by [SHIP-BY DATE] for holiday delivery"

Keep the most important words in roughly the first 40 characters, because phones often cut off longer subject lines.

Fill-in template: the Black Friday launch email

Copy this into your email tool and replace everything in square brackets.

Subject: [OFFER] starts now Preview text: Through [END DAY] at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]. No code needed.

Hi [FIRST NAME, or "there"],

Our Black Friday sale is live: [OFFER] on [WHAT'S INCLUDED], now through [END DAY], [END DATE] at [TIME] [TIME ZONE].

[Use code [CODE] at checkout. / The discount is applied automatically.]

A few favorites to start with:

  • [PRODUCT 1]: [one short reason someone loves it]
  • [PRODUCT 2]: [one short reason]
  • [PRODUCT 3]: [one short reason]

[BUTTON: Shop the sale]

Good to know: [EXCLUSIONS, e.g. "Gift cards and custom orders aren't included."] To get your order in time for the holidays, please order by [SHIP-BY DATE].

Thanks for supporting a small shop, [YOUR NAME] [SHOP NAME]

Setting your ship-by date

Don't guess this. Check the published holiday shipping deadlines from the carriers you actually use, then work backwards from those dates, adding the days you need to make and pack orders. If you make to order, give yourself a generous buffer. It's better to say a date a few days early than to deal with late packages and unhappy messages in December.

Pre-send checklist

Go through this for every email before you hit send:

  • [ ] Search the email for "[" to catch any placeholder you forgot to fill in
  • [ ] The discount code actually works (test it in your own checkout)
  • [ ] The end date, time and time zone are correct and match your store settings
  • [ ] Every button and link goes to the right page, and sale prices show correctly there
  • [ ] Exclusions are stated clearly
  • [ ] Your ship-by date is included in the later emails
  • [ ] Preview text is filled in (otherwise some inboxes show random first-line text)
  • [ ] You sent yourself a test and read it on your phone
  • [ ] Your unsubscribe link and business address are present (most platforms add these automatically; check that yours did)
  • [ ] You're sending only to people who signed up for your emails

Schedule it now, not on the day

The most useful thing you can do this week is write and schedule all your emails before Thanksgiving. Draft them in one sitting, schedule each one in your platform, then send yourself tests. On the weekend you'll be busy packing orders and answering questions, not writing copy at 7 a.m. on Black Friday.

Put a reminder in your calendar for Monday Nov 30 at noon to check that your code is still working and your last-call email is queued. After that, the sequence runs by itself.

Quick recap

  • Black Friday 2026 is Nov 27, Small Business Saturday is Nov 28, and Cyber Monday is Nov 30.
  • Send 5 to 9 emails between Nov 17 and Dec 2.
  • Put the offer, how to get it, the end time and exclusions in the first lines of every sale email.
  • Use your carriers' published deadlines to set an honest ship-by date.
  • Schedule everything before Thanksgiving and test on your phone.
