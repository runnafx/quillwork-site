Jingle Bells in Middle C Position: Notes, Fingering + Duet Part

A complete beginner arrangement plan for the Jingle Bells chorus, played with five fingers in middle C position. It includes the note names bar by bar, counts, a simple left hand, a teacher duet part and a two-week practice plan.

The chorus of Jingle Bells is one of the easiest Christmas pieces a beginner can play. In C major the whole tune uses only five notes: C, D, E, F and G. That means it fits under one hand in middle C position, and the student never has to move. Most first-year students can learn it in December lessons.

James Lord Pierpont published the song in 1857 and died in 1893, so the melody is in the public domain. You can write it out for your students yourself. This page gives you everything you need to do that.

Hand position

Right hand: put the thumb on middle C. Each finger rests on the next white key:

  • C = finger 1 (thumb)
  • D = finger 2
  • E = finger 3
  • F = finger 4
  • G = finger 5

Left hand (optional): use the C position one octave lower. Finger 5 goes on the C below middle C, so you get C = 5, D = 4, E = 3, F = 2, G = 1. The simple left hand below uses only C (5), F (2) and G (1).

A quick tip for students: finger 3 starts the song. If the first note sounds wrong, check that the thumb is on middle C and not on D.

The melody, bar by bar

The piece is in 4/4 time, so there are four beats in every bar. Key: q = quarter note (1 beat), h = half note (2 beats), w = whole note (4 beats), dq = dotted quarter (1½ beats), e = eighth note (½ beat).

Line 1 ("Jingle bells, jingle bells, jingle all the way")

  1. E (q) E (q) E (h). Count: 1, 2, 3-4. Fingers: 3 3 3
  2. E (q) E (q) E (h). Count: 1, 2, 3-4. Fingers: 3 3 3
  3. E (q) G (q) C (dq) D (e). Count: 1, 2, 3-and, a. Fingers: 3 5 1 2
  4. E (w). Count: 1-2-3-4. Finger: 3

Line 2 ("Oh what fun it is to ride in a one-horse open sleigh, hey!")

  1. F (q) F (q) F (dq) F (e). Count: 1, 2, 3-and, a. Fingers: 4 4 4 4
  2. F (q) E (q) E (q) E (e) E (e). Count: 1, 2, 3, 4-and. Fingers: 4 3 3 3 3
  3. E (q) D (q) D (q) E (q). Count: 1, 2, 3, 4. Fingers: 3 2 2 3
  4. D (h) G (h). Count: 1-2, 3-4. Fingers: 2 5

Line 3 is the same as line 1

  1. E E E (q q h)
  2. E E E (q q h)
  3. E G C D (q q dq e)
  4. E (w)

Line 4 (the ending)

  1. F (q) F (q) F (q) F (q). Fingers: 4 4 4 4
  2. F (q) E (q) E (q) E (e) E (e). Fingers: 4 3 3 3 3
  3. G (q) G (q) F (q) D (q). Fingers: 5 5 4 2
  4. C (w). Finger: 1

Point out to the student: lines 1 and 3 are the same, and lines 2 and 4 differ only in their last two bars. Once they see that, the piece is really "two lines plus a new ending", which makes it much less scary.

Simplifying the rhythm for Primer students

If dotted rhythms are new to the student, you can straighten them out without changing the notes:

  • Bars 3 and 11: play E G C D as four even quarter notes.
  • Bar 5: play F F F F as four even quarter notes, as in bar 13.
  • Bars 6 and 14: play F E E E as four quarter notes, dropping one E.

Teach the simplified version first. Add the "real" rhythm once the notes are secure. Many students pick up the dotted rhythm by ear because they already know the song.

Easy left hand (one note per bar)

Hold one whole note in the left hand at the start of each bar while the right hand plays the melody.

  • Bars 1–4: C, C, C, C
  • Bar 5: F
  • Bar 6: C
  • Bars 7–8: G, G
  • Bars 9–12: C, C, C, C
  • Bar 13: F
  • Bar 14: C
  • Bar 15: G
  • Bar 16: C

For a student who isn't ready for hands together, have them play just C on beat 1 of bars 1–4 and again in the final bar, and leave the rest out. It still sounds complete.

Teacher duet part

A duet makes a beginner sound much fuller at a recital, and it helps the student keep a steady pulse. The student plays the melody one octave higher than written (thumb on the C above middle C) using the same fingering. The teacher sits to the left and plays chords in the middle of the keyboard.

Chords to use:

  • C major: C–E–G
  • F major: F–A–C (or C–F–A, so your hand barely moves from C major)
  • G7: B–F–G (or G–B–D–F if you want the full chord)

Chord per bar:

  • Bars 1–4: C
  • Bar 5: F
  • Bar 6: C
  • Bar 7: G7 (if you want the traditional harmony, play D7 here: D–F#–C)
  • Bar 8: G7
  • Bars 9–12: C
  • Bar 13: F
  • Bar 14: C
  • Bar 15: G7
  • Bar 16: C

Accompaniment patterns, easiest first:

  1. Whole notes: one held chord per bar. This is best for a nervous student.
  2. Steady quarter notes: play the chord on every beat. This works like a metronome.
  3. Sleigh bells: play the bass note on beats 1 and 3 and the chord on beats 2 and 4 ("oom-pah").

Introduction: play two bars of the C chord in your chosen pattern before the student comes in. Count out loud "1, 2, ready, go" during the second bar.

Ending: after the student's final C, play a soft C chord high up, or a quick glissando. Students love it.

Why not the verse?

The verse ("Dashing through the snow") starts on the G below middle C and also uses the A and B below it. Those notes are outside middle C position for the right hand. For a first Christmas piece, teach the chorus only. Students working at Level 1 or above who have learned to shift position can add the verse later.

Two-week practice plan

Lesson 1

  • Find middle C position and play C-D-E-F-G up and down with fingers 1-2-3-4-5.
  • Learn line 1 with the simplified rhythm.
  • At home: line 1, three times a day.

Days 2–4 at home

  • Line 1 from memory.
  • Clap and count bars 3 and 4 before playing them.

Lesson 2

  • Learn line 2 and line 4.
  • Show that line 3 repeats line 1.
  • Play the whole piece slowly with the teacher playing whole-note chords.

Days 5–9 at home

  • Whole piece once a day, slowly.
  • If the student makes a mistake, practise only that bar three times, then go on.

Lesson 3

  • Add the left-hand whole notes, or the dotted rhythm, but not both in the same week.
  • Play it through with the duet part at a slow, steady tempo.

Final days

  • Play it once a day as a "performance" for a family member, without stopping for mistakes.

Common mistakes and quick fixes

  • Rushing the half notes in bars 1–2. Have the student say "Jin-gle bells, hold" out loud while playing.
  • Using the wrong finger for G in bar 3. Remind them that G is always finger 5 in this position, so the little finger has to be ready.
  • Losing their place after bar 8. Write a large "1 again!" above line 3.
  • The ending in bar 15. G G F D is the only spot where the hand skips a finger (4 to 2). Practise just bars 15–16 as a "mini-piece".
  • Speeding up on the duet. The teacher's quarter-note chords set the pace. Start slower than you think you need to.

Recital-ready checklist

  • Student can find middle C position without help
  • Plays all 16 bars without stopping, even after a slip
  • Holds the whole notes in bars 4, 12 and 16 for the full four beats
  • Knows which version they are playing (simplified or dotted rhythm)
  • Has practised the duet intro and knows when to come in
  • Has practised walking up, bowing and sitting at the bench
  • Has a written copy of the music at the lesson, even if they play from memory

Once this piece is secure, the same five-finger approach works for other carols. Jolly Old St. Nicholas and Up on the Housetop are good next choices because they also stay close to one hand position in C major.
