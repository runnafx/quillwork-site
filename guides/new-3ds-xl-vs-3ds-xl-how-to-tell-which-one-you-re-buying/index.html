New 3DS XL vs 3DS XL: How to Tell Which One You're Buying

The New Nintendo 3DS XL and the original 3DS XL have nearly identical names but are different consoles. Use these photo checks, the differences that matter, and a message to send the seller before you pay.

"New Nintendo 3DS XL" is a product name, not a condition. A listing titled "New 3DS XL" might be the newer model, or it might be an original 3DS XL that the seller thinks is new or like new. Holiday gift buyers get caught by this, and so do people shopping estate auctions and lots.

The two consoles differ in controls, game compatibility and price. You can tell them apart from photos in about a minute.

The 60-second answer

Look at the front of the open console. If there is a small rubbery nub above the A/B/X/Y buttons, it is a New 3DS XL. If there is no nub, it is not.

Then confirm with the model number on the back:

  • RED-001 = New Nintendo 3DS XL
  • SPR-001 = original Nintendo 3DS XL
  • JAN-001 = New Nintendo 2DS XL (no 3D screen)
  • KTR-001 = small New Nintendo 3DS
  • CTR-001 = original small 3DS

If a listing has no photo of the open console or the back label, ask for one before buying.

Side-by-side: what to look for in photos

C-Stick (right-hand nub)

  • New 3DS XL: yes, above the A/B/X/Y buttons
  • Original XL: no

ZL and ZR shoulder buttons

  • New 3DS XL: yes, on the top edge, inside the L and R buttons
  • Original XL: no, only L and R

Start and Select

  • New 3DS XL: on the right, below the A/B/X/Y buttons
  • Original XL: in a row under the bottom screen, with Home

Cartridge slot

  • New 3DS XL: on the front bottom edge, next to the stylus
  • Original XL: on the back

Memory card

  • New 3DS XL: microSD, under the back plate (two small screws)
  • Original XL: full-size SD card, in a slot on the side

Wi-Fi switch

  • New 3DS XL: no physical switch
  • Original XL: a slider on the side

Amiibo (NFC) reader

  • New 3DS XL: built into the bottom screen
  • Original XL: none without a separate accessory

3D screen

  • New 3DS XL: uses the inside camera to track your face, which keeps the 3D stable when you move
  • Original XL: 3D only looks right from a narrow angle

The two are close in size and weight, so don't try to judge from a photo of the closed console. Don't rely on color either. Check the controls and the label.

What the difference means when you play

Most 3DS games run on both models, so the original XL is a real option if you only want the mainstream library. The New model matters in these cases:

  • A few games only run on the New models. Xenoblade Chronicles 3D and Minecraft: New Nintendo 3DS Edition are the best-known examples. If you plan to buy a specific game, check its box or listing for a "New Nintendo 3DS" only marking.
  • Some games use the extra controls. Majora's Mask 3D and Monster Hunter 4 Ultimate both support the C-Stick, though the original XL can still play them.
  • Performance is better. The New XL has a faster processor and more memory, which can shorten load times in some games.
  • Amiibo work without an adapter.
  • 3D is easier to use, because of the face tracking.

The compromises are small. The C-Stick is tiny and barely moves, because it senses pressure. L and R are shorter to make room for ZL and ZR, and some reviewers found the console a little less comfortable to hold.

If the buyer is a young child, note that Nintendo advises against 3D viewing for children 6 and under. You can turn off 3D with the console's Parental Controls. A 2DS XL (JAN-001) has the New model's controls and game compatibility with no 3D screen at all.

Which should you buy?

  • You want the most capable 3DS: New 3DS XL (RED-001).
  • You want a specific New-only game: a New model. RED-001, KTR-001 or JAN-001 will all run it.
  • You want no 3D, or a gift for a younger child: look at the New 2DS XL.
  • You just want Mario Kart 7, Pokémon or similar, on a budget: an original XL in good shape is fine, as long as it is priced as an original.

What each one should cost

Prices move, so check them yourself on the day you buy. On eBay, search your exact model, turn on the Sold items filter and look at the last 90 days of units that match yours.

When we checked on November 5, 2026, PriceCharting showed recent eBay sales of a loose black New 3DS XL between $230 and $310. A separate price tool showed a median of about $150 for "3DS XL," but that group mixes in the cheaper original model. This gap is the reason to compare only like with like.

When you read sold results:

  • Ignore any sale whose title or photos show SPR-001 if you are pricing a New model.
  • Separate boxed units from loose ones, since boxes sell for more.
  • Count chargers and games. A listing with a charger and two games is not the same as a bare console.
  • Be suspicious of listings far below the going rate. Check for the missing nub, a "for parts" note, a cracked hinge or a foreign region.

Red flags in listings

  • "New 3DS XL" with only stock photos. You can't confirm the model, and you can't see the actual condition.
  • "Powers on" with nothing else. That says nothing about the buttons, screens or cartridge slot.
  • "Untested" or "operating condition not established." Treat this as a parts unit and price it that way.
  • No charger mentioned. New 3DS XLs sold in North America came without an AC adapter, so plenty of used ones don't include one. Nintendo says adapters from the 2DS, 3DS, 3DS XL, DSi and DSi XL all work.
  • Region not stated. 3DS games are region-locked, so a Japanese or European console won't play most US cartridges. On a powered-on console, open System Settings and look at the version number in the corner of the top screen. It ends in U for the Americas, E for Europe and Australia, or J for Japan.
  • "Comes with lots of games installed." Digital games are tied to the previous owner's account and can disappear after a reset. Nintendo ended new 3DS eShop purchases in 2023, so you can't simply buy them again. Only count physical cartridges as included.
  • Mentions of "homebrew," "CFW," "Luma" or a "Dev Menu." These point to modified firmware. That is a dealbreaker for many buyers, especially for a gift.

Copy-and-paste message for the seller

Could you send a photo of the open console showing the right-hand buttons? > 3. Which region does it show? (System Settings, top screen, last letter of the version number: U, E or J.) > 4. Do all the buttons and the Circle Pad work, including ZL and ZR? > 5. Does it read cartridges, and does it charge? > 6. Does it include a charger, stylus and memory card? > Thanks!

Keep the seller's answers in the marketplace's own messages. If the item arrives different from the listing, you'll have a written record to point to when you open a return.

First 10 minutes after it arrives

  1. Match the back label to the listing.
  2. Charge it and confirm that the charge light comes on.
  3. Check System Settings for the region letter.
  4. Calibrate the Circle Pad and watch for drift.
  5. Press every button, including ZL, ZR, Start and Select, several times.
  6. Calibrate the touch screen and tap the corners.
  7. Launch a game cartridge.
  8. Try the 3D slider and both speakers.
  9. Check the hinge for cracks and looseness.

If you find a problem, contact the seller inside the return window. Check the listing's return terms before you buy, because estate auctions and many private sales are final.
