How to Price Used Items on eBay From Sold Listings (+ Example)

A step-by-step way to turn eBay sold listings into a Buy It Now price and a Best Offer floor. Includes a fill-in worksheet, the fee math and a fully worked example.

Asking prices only tell you what sellers hope to get. Sold listings show what buyers actually paid. This method takes about 10 minutes per item and gives you two numbers: a Buy It Now price and a lowest offer you will accept.

Fee figures come from eBay's US selling fees page, checked in November 2026. They apply to sellers without an eBay Store in most categories. Some categories have different rates, so confirm yours on that page or in the fee preview when you list.

What you need first

  • The exact model, part number or pattern name, taken from the item's label if possible
  • An honest condition: tested and working, untested, or not working
  • A list of what is included, such as case, cables, box or manual
  • The weight and rough box size after packing, so you can estimate postage
  • The least you would be happy to keep after all costs

Step 1: Pull up the sold listings

On a computer: search for the item, then scroll the left-hand filters to "Show only" and tick Sold Items.

In the eBay app: search for the item, tap Filter and turn on Sold items.

eBay moves these menus now and then, so look for the same words if yours differs. Search shows roughly the last 90 days. If you have a seller account, the Product Research tool in Seller Hub (formerly Terapeak) can reach further back, which helps with rare items.

Start specific ("Sony WM-F45 Walkman") and widen only if you get too few results.

Step 2: Throw out the comps that do not match

A comp only counts if a buyer would see it as the same deal as yours. Remove any sale that is:

  • A different model, size or version
  • In a different condition, especially "for parts" results mixed in with working ones
  • Serviced, refurbished or professionally restored, unless yours is too
  • Sold with extras yours does not have, or missing things yours includes
  • A lot of several items
  • A Best Offer sale showing a crossed-out price, because that figure is often the asking price and the accepted amount may be lower
  • An auction that ended with one bid, or a price far from all the others with no visible reason

Aim for 3 to 5 clean comps. Three good matches beat twenty loose ones.

Step 3: Add shipping to every comp

A $48 item with $9 shipping and a $57 item with free shipping cost the buyer the same. Write down item price + shipping charged for each comp. Then sort them low to high and take the middle number (the median). With an even count, average the two middle numbers. The median is better than the average because one lucky sale cannot drag it around.

Step 4: Work out your real costs

For most categories, a US seller without a Store pays:

  • 13.6% of the total sale, which includes the item price, shipping charged and sales tax
  • Plus $0.40 per order over $10, or $0.30 for orders of $10 or less

Because tax is part of the fee base, you pay a fee on money you never receive. Tax rates vary by buyer, so this guide assumes 8% as a working estimate. That makes the fee about 14.7% of your price.

Step 5: Set your price and your floor

Buy It Now price: if you will accept offers, start about 5 to 10% above the median delivered price. That leaves room to negotiate. It is a rule of thumb, so adjust it if your item is cleaner or rougher than the comps.

Best Offer floor, assuming you offer free shipping:

Floor = (least you will keep + postage + packing + $0.40) ÷ 0.853

The 0.853 comes from 1 − (0.136 × 1.08). If you use Promoted Listings, subtract that ad rate from 0.853 as well, so a 5% rate gives 0.803.

Worked example

The item and all prices below are made up to show the math. They are not real market data for this model.

The item is a used Sony cassette Walkman. It plays tape, has light scuffs and comes with no case or headphones.

Sold results found:

  • A: $62, free shipping. Working, no case. Keep, $62
  • B: $48 + $9 shipping. Working, scuffed. Keep, $57
  • C: $85, free shipping. New belts, serviced. Drop: better than ours
  • D: $24 + $8 shipping. "Not working." Drop: worse than ours
  • E: $70, free shipping. Includes case and headphones. Drop: extras
  • F: $55 + $7 shipping. Working, no case. Keep, $62
  • G: $59, free shipping. Working. Keep, $59
  • H: $75 crossed out, Best Offer accepted. Drop: real price unknown

Sorted delivered prices are $57, $59, $62 and $62. The median is ($59 + $62) ÷ 2 = $60.50.

Costs: $7 postage, $1 packing, and $35 is the least the seller wants to keep.

  • Buy It Now: $66 with free shipping, about 9% above the median
  • Floor: ($35 + $7 + $1 + $0.40) ÷ 0.853 = $50.88, rounded up to $51

What the seller would keep at each price, assuming 8% tax:

  • Sells at $66: total with tax $71.28, fees $10.09, shipping and packing $8, keeps $47.91
  • Sells at $60.50: total with tax $65.34, fees $9.29, shipping and packing $8, keeps $43.21
  • Sells at $51: total with tax $55.08, fees $7.89, shipping and packing $8, keeps $35.11

The check on the last line confirms the floor does what it should.

If the floor is higher than the median, see the last section below.

Setting the floor on eBay

When you turn on Best Offer in the listing form, look for the option to automatically decline offers below an amount, and enter your floor. Two things to know:

  • It is only available in categories that support auto-decline.
  • If you leave it blank, low offers simply come to you to review.

Setting a floor saves you from answering every lowball one by one. You can also set an auto-accept amount if you would happily take a certain price without being asked.

When there are too few comps

If you cannot find 3 clean sales:

  1. Remove words from the search one at a time: first color, then year, then the less important model details.
  2. Try the closest sibling model and note how it differs from yours.
  3. Check a specialist price guide if one exists, such as PriceCharting for video games.
  4. Look at active listings for the same item, but treat them only as a ceiling for your price.
  5. List toward the high end with Best Offer on. If there is no interest after about 30 days, lower the price and look again.

For anything that may be truly valuable, such as jewelry, fine art, rare coins or signed items, get a qualified appraisal before selling it based on eBay comps.

When the math says not to list it

Sometimes the floor comes out above the median. That means the item will not earn what you need after fees and postage. You have a few choices:

  • Lower the amount you need to keep, if clearing the space matters more.
  • Bundle it with related items so one postage cost covers several, then price the bundle using lot comps.
  • Sell it locally, where there is no postage to pay. This suits heavy or bulky items most.
  • Donate it. For low-value items in a downsizing or estate clear-out, this can be the sensible call.

Quick checklist before you publish

  • Comps match on model, condition and what is included
  • All comps are compared at delivered price
  • Fee math used the rate for your actual category
  • Floor is entered, if your category allows auto-decline
  • Every flaw appears in both the photos and the condition notes
  • "Untested" is stated plainly if you could not test it
  • Item is packed or weighed, so the postage figure is real
