Black Key Groups on Piano: Free Winter Keyboard Worksheet + Key

A printable winter keyboard geography lesson for Primer piano students. It covers finding groups of 2 and 3 black keys, then using them to find every C, D, E, F, G, A and B, and includes an answer key.

Before a beginner can read notes on the staff, they need to find notes on the keyboard without counting up from the bottom every time. The black keys make this possible. They come in groups of 2 and groups of 3, and every white key sits in a fixed place next to one of those groups.

This page gives you a short teaching sequence, a winter-themed worksheet you can copy straight into a lesson, and an answer key. Everything works on a real piano, a digital keyboard or a paper keyboard chart. You don't need to print anything special.

The facts students need

  • Black keys alternate in groups of 2 and groups of 3 across the whole keyboard.
  • A standard 88-key piano has 52 white keys and 36 black keys.
  • At the far left (bottom) there is one black key on its own, between the two lowest white keys (A and B). After that, the pattern of 2s and 3s repeats evenly up the keyboard.
  • An 88-key piano has 7 groups of 2 and 7 groups of 3, plus that single black key: 7×2 + 7×3 + 1 = 36.
  • The musical alphabet uses only A B C D E F G, then starts again.

Where each white key lives

Around the group of 2 black keys:

  • C is just to the left of the group of 2.
  • D is in the middle, between the 2 black keys.
  • E is just to the right of the group of 2.

Around the group of 3 black keys:

  • F is just to the left of the group of 3.
  • G and A are the two keys inside the group of 3. G comes first (left), then A.
  • B is just to the right of the group of 3.

A winter image that helps young students: D is a snowman standing between two trees (the two black keys). Students find a group of 2, put a finger on the snowman in the middle, and they've found D. Then C is one step to the left and E is one step to the right.

Middle C is the C closest to the middle of the piano. On an 88-key piano it is the 4th C from the left. Many acoustic pianos have the brand name printed roughly above it, but that's only a rough guide. Teach students to find it by counting groups of 2.

A 10-minute teaching sequence

  1. Black key hunt (2 minutes). Students play every group of 2 black keys from bottom to top using fingers 2 and 3 together. Then they play every group of 3 using fingers 2, 3 and 4. Ask: "Which group is lower, high or low?" Then ask them to play only the groups of 3 in the high half of the piano.
  2. Snowman D (2 minutes). Students find D in every group of 2, all the way up, saying "D" out loud each time.
  3. C and E neighbours (2 minutes). From each D, they step left to C and right to E. Then they play C D E together as a three-note "snowfall" going up the keyboard.
  4. F G A B (2 minutes). Do the same around the groups of 3: F to the left, G and A inside, B to the right.
  5. Random calls (2 minutes). You call a letter, they find it anywhere on the keyboard as fast as possible. Then you call a letter plus a direction, such as "a high G" or "a low E".

Spread this over two or three lessons for very young students. Older beginners can usually do all of it in one go.

Winter Keyboard Worksheet (copy or adapt)

Students use a keyboard chart printed or drawn on the page, or answer while sitting at the piano.

Part A: Snowball count Look at a full 88-key keyboard (or a chart of one).

  1. How many groups of 2 black keys are there? ______
  2. How many groups of 3 black keys are there? ______
  3. How many black keys are on their own, not in a group? ______
  4. Where is that single black key: far left or far right? ______

Part B: Mitten match Draw a line from each mitten to the right place.

  1. D → (a) left of the group of 3 (b) between the group of 2 (c) right of the group of 3
  2. F → (a) left of the group of 3 (b) left of the group of 2 (c) inside the group of 3
  3. B → (a) right of the group of 2 (b) right of the group of 3 (c) left of the group of 2
  4. C → (a) left of the group of 2 (b) right of the group of 2 (c) inside the group of 3
  5. E → (a) left of the group of 3 (b) right of the group of 2 (c) between the group of 2

Part C: Gingerbread gaps Fill in the missing letters of the musical alphabet.

  1. C D __ F G
  2. G A __ C D
  3. E F G __ B
  4. A B C __ E
  5. F __ A B __

Part D: Inside the group of 3

  1. Which two white keys are inside the group of 3 black keys? ______ and ______
  2. Which of those two is further left? ______

Part E: Gift tags (at the piano) Play each key and tick it off when your teacher hears it.

  1. Every C on the piano. How many did you find? ______
  2. Middle C. Which C is it, counting from the left? ______
  3. A high F (above middle C).
  4. A low A (below middle C).
  5. The very lowest white key. What letter is it? ______
  6. The very highest white key. What letter is it? ______

Part F: Snowman puzzle

  1. Start on D. Move one white key to the right. What note are you on? ______
  2. Start on F. Move one white key to the left. What note are you on? ______
  3. Start on B. Move one white key to the right. What note are you on? ______
  4. Start on C. Move two white keys to the left. What note are you on? ______

Common mix-ups and quick fixes

  • Confusing C and F. Both sit just to the left of a black key group. Ask "group of 2 or group of 3?" before the student names the key. C goes with 2, F goes with 3.
  • Mixing up G and A inside the group of 3. Remind them the alphabet always goes up to the right: F, then G, then A, then B.
  • Calling the note after G "H". Practise saying the alphabet backwards and forwards with the wrap (E F G A B, and A G F E D) until it comes automatically.
  • Using the brand name to find middle C. It works on some pianos but not on keyboards or other instruments. Counting groups of 2 works everywhere.
  • Smaller keyboards. 61- and 76-key keyboards start and end on different notes, so Part A and Part E answers will change. Count the groups together on the student's own instrument at home.

Making it fit your studio

  • Very young students: do Parts A, B and E at the piano and skip the writing. Let them put a small sticker or paper snowflake on every D.
  • Older beginners: time Part E. Can they find all eight Cs in under 15 seconds? Write down their time and try again next week.
  • Home practice: send home Parts C, D and F. They need no instrument and take about five minutes.
  • Group lessons: turn the random calls into a relay. Each student finds a called note, then passes the turn on.

Once students can find any white key without counting from the bottom, landmark notes on the staff and steps versus skips become much easier to teach. They already know where each note lives on the keyboard.
