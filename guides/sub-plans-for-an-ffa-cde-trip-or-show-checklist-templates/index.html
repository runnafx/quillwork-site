Sub Plans for an FFA CDE Trip or Show: Checklist + Templates

A checklist and fill-in templates for ag teachers who are leaving for a CDE, livestock show or convention. It covers what to leave for a substitute when half the class is gone and nobody left behind can run the barn, shop or greenhouse.

A trip day is harder to plan than a sick day. You know about it weeks ahead, but the night before is spent loading animals, checking paperwork and printing team rosters. The sub plans often get written last.

A trip day also brings problems a sick day doesn't have:

  • Half of some classes are gone. The substitute teaches whoever is left, and the travelling students will need to make up the work.
  • Animals and plants still need care, and the person who usually does it is on the bus with you.
  • Students know the routine better than the sub does. Expect to hear "we always go to the shop on Fridays."
  • You may be out more than one day, so the plans have to hold up for a 2- or 3-day show or convention.

This page gives you a checklist, a simple rule for choosing the work, and four fill-in templates you can copy into a document today. Use your district's policies on field trips, animal care and substitutes alongside it. They come first.

The Rule for Choosing Trip-Day Work

When part of the class is absent for a school activity, don't introduce new content that the rest of the unit depends on. If you do, the travelling students fall behind, and you end up re-teaching it on Monday.

Give the students who stay something that is:

  1. Paper-only and classroom-only. No tools, animals, chemicals, greenhouse, shop or outdoor work, because the sub can't supervise any of those safely.
  2. Self-contained. It's a complete lesson on its own, so missing it doesn't break the unit.
  3. Doable later by travellers. Travelling students get the same handout with a later due date, or a short alternative task about the event they attended.
  4. Easy to grade. Include an answer key, or grade it for completion.

Good fits include a reading with questions on a topic adjacent to your unit, ag careers work, a review of past vocabulary, an ag issues article with a written response, or a supply-chain or by-products mapping activity.

Poor fits include the first lesson of a new unit, anything that needs a video or a working computer lab, record book work that students usually need your help with, and any "finish your project in the shop" day.

The Checklist

Two weeks out

  • Confirm travel dates and which class periods each traveller will miss.
  • Ask the office to request a substitute for the full trip, and ask for the same person every day if you can.
  • Arrange animal care and greenhouse care with a qualified adult your school approves, such as another ag teacher, an approved volunteer or a farm manager. Don't count on the sub for this.
  • Choose the paper-only lessons for each day you'll be gone and print class sets.

Three days out

  • Fill in Template 1 (the trip-day sub note) and Template 2 (the class split).
  • Fill in Template 3 (the care handoff card) and walk the caretaker through the barn or greenhouse in person.
  • Put each day's work in its own labeled folder: "Trip Day 1," "Trip Day 2."
  • Seal the answer keys in an envelope marked "Teacher only."
  • Give travelling students their make-up handouts and Template 4 before you leave, so the work isn't waiting on Monday.

The day before

  • Lock the shop, greenhouse, chemical storage and animal areas, or tell the office in writing that they're off-limits.
  • Leave the sub folder in a place the office knows about, and tell the office where it is.
  • Leave a phone number for a teacher in the building, not your own number for routine questions. You'll be in a show ring or a contest room.
  • Check that the posted emergency map and evacuation route are by the door.

When you're back

  • Collect the folder and the log, and read the sub's notes before first period.
  • Grade the stay-home work, and set a firm due date for the travellers' version.
  • Write down which lessons worked as trip-day work so next year's trip plans take ten minutes.

Template 1: Trip-Day Sub Note

Copy this onto the first page of the folder and fill in the blanks.

> Thank you for covering my Agriculture classes. I'm away with students at ______________________ (event) from ________ to ________. > > Teacher: ______________ Room: ______ Today's folder: Trip Day ____ > > Some students are absent for this trip. They're on the field trip list, so please mark them as school-activity absences using the office's procedure, not as regular absences. A list of who is travelling is in this folder (Class Split page). > > Today is a paper-only lesson. Students stay in this classroom for the whole period. Students do not: > - go to the barn, greenhouse, shop, lab or outside > - handle animals, feed, plants, tools, equipment or chemicals > - "check on" animals or "water quickly." Animal and plant care is already arranged. > > If a student says "we always do this," the answer is still no. I've told them this in advance. > > The plan: see the timed plan inside the folder. Collect every paper at the end of each period. > > Questions during the day: ______________ (teacher), Room ______, ext. ______ > Office ext.: ______ Nurse ext.: ______ > Students who can help with handouts: ______________________ > > Please leave a short note on how each period went. Thank you!

Template 2: Class Split

Fill in one row per period so the sub can tell at a glance who is missing on purpose.

> Period ____ Class: ______________ > Travelling with teacher (school activity): ______________________________ > Expected in class: about ____ students > Today's work for students in class: ______________________________ > Make-up work given to travellers: ______________________________ Due: ________

Make a copy for the attendance clerk too if your school wants one. That cuts down on confused calls home about "absent" students who are actually at the contest.

Template 3: Animal and Greenhouse Care Handoff Card

This card goes to the approved caretaker, not to the substitute. Tape a second copy inside the barn or greenhouse door.

> Caretaker: ______________ Phone: ______________ > Dates covered: ________ to ________ > Backup contact: ______________ Phone: ______________ > > Animals: (species, number, pen or location) > ________________________________________________ > Feed: what, how much, what times: ______________________ > Water: check times and what to look for: ______________________ > Medications or special care: ______________________ > Who to call if an animal is sick or hurt: ______________ (vet or approved contact) > > Greenhouse/plants: watering schedule: ______________________ > Vents/heat: what to check and what not to touch: ______________________ > > Keys: who has them and where they go back: ______________________ > Students are not allowed in these areas while I'm away.

If you take animals to the show, write that on the card as well ("4 market lambs at the county fair; 2 remain in pen 3") so nobody panics at an empty pen.

Template 4: Event Reflection for Travelling Students

For travellers, a short written reflection on the event is a fair alternative or add-on to the stay-home handout. It also counts as real learning. Give it to them before you leave.

> Name: ______________ Event: ______________ Date(s): ________ > > 1. What contest, show class or session did you take part in or watch? > 2. Describe one thing judges, officials or speakers looked for. Use specific vocabulary from our class. > 3. What did you or your team do well? > 4. What is one thing you'd prepare differently next time? Be specific. > 5. Name one ag career you saw at the event, such as a judge, vet, extension agent or ag lender. What do you think that person's job involves? > 6. What is one question you want to ask in class when you get back?

If students fill in Question 4 honestly, it also makes a useful start for next year's practice plan.

A Sample Trip-Day Timed Plan (One 50-Minute Period)

Adjust the topic to your course. This shape works for almost any reading-and-questions lesson.

  1. 0-5 min: Attendance. Check the Class Split page. Hand out the reading.
  2. 5-10 min: Warm-up. Write one question on the board for students to answer on paper, for example: "Name five products in this room that started on a farm."
  3. 10-25 min: Reading. Students read silently, or volunteers read a paragraph each.
  4. 25-45 min: Questions. Students work on their own. Early finishers do the "extension" question at the bottom of the handout.
  5. 45-50 min: Collect. Every paper goes into the folder. The sub fills in the log.

For a 90-minute block, add a second handout in the same topic, such as a vocabulary match or a chart to complete, rather than stretching one reading.

Common Trip-Day Problems and Fixes

  • "The sub let students water the greenhouse." Put the rule in writing in the sub note and tell students yourself before you leave. Lock the door if policy allows.
  • "Half the class did the work and half didn't, and now the unit is split." Use work that doesn't depend on the unit, as described in the rule above.
  • "The sub texted me during finals at the show." Give the sub the name of a teacher in the building for routine questions.
  • "I forgot the travellers' make-up work." Give it to them before you board, along with Template 4.
  • "A 3-day convention means 3 sets of plans." Use one topic per day so each day stands alone. Then a cancelled day or a changed sub doesn't break anything.

Plan the trip days once, label the folders, and file them away. You'll reuse most of this every time you take students to a contest or show.
