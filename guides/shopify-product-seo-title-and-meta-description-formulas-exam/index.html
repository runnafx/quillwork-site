Shopify Product SEO Title and Meta Description: Formulas + Examples

What to type in the Search engine listing box on a Shopify product page. Includes fill-in formulas, examples for five product types, a pre-publish checklist and a way to update many products at once.

Open any product in Shopify, scroll to Search engine listing, and you'll find a page title, a meta description and a URL handle. If you never touch them, Shopify fills the listing from your product title and description. That's often fine for the title and poor for the description, because the opening of a description is rarely written to stand alone in a search result.

This guide gives you formulas to fill in, finished examples and a checklist. Allow about five minutes per product once you've done a few.

What these two fields do

The page title is the main candidate for the clickable headline in Google. Google can build that headline from other parts of the page too, including your main on-page heading, so keep the two consistent.

The meta description is a suggestion for the short text under the headline. Google says it builds snippets mainly from page content and uses the meta description only sometimes. Treat it as your best pitch, with no guarantee it will be shown.

Neither field guarantees a ranking. Their realistic job is to make it clear that your page is the right result, so the right person clicks.

How long should they be?

Google publishes no fixed character limit for either field. Results are cut to fit the width of the screen, so a safe length is a working rule rather than an official number.

  • Page title: aim for 60 characters or fewer, and put the important words first.
  • Meta description: aim for 155 characters or fewer, and put the main point in the first 100 or so, since phones may show less.
  • Shopify's counter: the admin shows a live count under each field. Its limit is the most Shopify will accept, which can be longer than what Google displays.

Check your theme before you count. Many themes, including Shopify's Dawn, add your store name to the end of the title automatically. Open a live product page and read the text on the browser tab. If it ends with your store name, don't type the name again. Leave room for it if you can, although a cut-off store name costs you little.

Before you write: fill in four lines

Do this for each product. Everything you need should come from facts you can confirm.

  • Search phrase: what a buyer would type (not your internal product name)
  • Identifier: the detail that separates this from similar items (material, size, fit, compatibility)
  • Variant or audience: color, size range or who it's for, if buyers search that way
  • Reason to pick you: a true and specific detail (made by hand in your studio, ships in two days, free returns)

To find the search phrase, type your product type into Google and read the autocomplete suggestions and the "related searches" at the bottom of the results. Choose the plainest phrase that fits exactly. "Stoneware pour-over dripper" is better than "coffee accessories" because it describes one thing.

The page title formulas

Pick the one that matches how people shop for the item.

  1. Material-led: [Material] [Product Type] – [Key Detail]
  2. Compatibility-led: [Product Type] for [Device or Model] – [Key Detail]
  3. Audience-led: [Product Type] for [Audience] – [Material or Style]
  4. Size-led: [Size or Capacity] [Product Type] – [Material or Color]
  5. Gift-led: [Product Type] Gift for [Recipient] – [Personalization or Detail]

Use one main phrase per title. A second phrase only earns its place if it describes the same product naturally. Skip the list of synonyms, and skip "Best" and "Top Quality", which tell a shopper nothing.

The meta description formula

Use this three-part pattern:

[What it is, including the search phrase, with its main detail]. [One or two facts that settle a buyer's doubts]. [Shop-level reason to buy: shipping, returns or where it's made].

The middle part is what most stores leave out. Ask what a shopper would otherwise have to click through to learn. For a ring, that may be sizing. For a phone case, compatibility. For a candle, burn time and scent.

Five finished examples

These products and shop details are invented for illustration. Replace each fact with your own.

Example 1: Kitchen item (material-led)

  • Title: Stoneware Pour-Over Coffee Dripper – Matte White
  • Meta: Matte white stoneware pour-over dripper that fits size 02 paper filters and brews 1–2 cups. Dishwasher safe. Free US shipping on orders over $50.

Example 2: Phone accessory (compatibility-led)

  • Title: Leather Case for iPhone 16 – Full-Grain Tan
  • Meta: Full-grain tan leather case cut for iPhone 16, with MagSafe magnets built in and open access to all ports. Ships within 2 business days. 30-day returns.

Example 3: Pet item (audience-led)

  • Title: Waxed Canvas Dog Collar for Large Dogs – Olive
  • Meta: Olive waxed canvas dog collar for necks 18–24 inches, with a solid brass buckle and welded D-ring. Sewn to order in our Ohio workshop. Ships in 5 days.

Example 4: Home item (size-led)

  • Title: 8 oz Soy Candle – Cedar and Smoke, Amber Jar
  • Meta: 8 oz soy wax candle scented with cedar and smoke, poured in an amber glass jar. About 40 hours of burn time. Hand-poured in small batches in Vermont.

Example 5: Jewelry (gift-led)

  • Title: Hand-Stamped Necklace Gift for Mom – Sterling Silver
  • Meta: Sterling silver necklace hand-stamped with up to 3 initials, on an 18-inch chain. Arrives in a gift box. Ships within 3 business days.

What to avoid

  • The same description on every product. Repeated text doesn't help a shopper compare, and Google is less likely to use it.
  • Claims you can't back up. "Waterproof", "hypoallergenic", "organic", "BPA-free" and "non-toxic" need a certificate, a test or a written supplier spec. Some may also be regulated for your product type.
  • Prices or holiday deadlines in the meta description, unless you'll update them when they change.
  • Opening with your store name. Lead with the product, since the first words are the ones most likely to survive a cut.
  • Editing the URL handle casually. The handle is the last part of the product's web address. If you change it on a live page, keep the Create a URL redirect box ticked so old links still work.

Pre-publish checklist

  • The title contains the phrase a buyer would actually type
  • The most important words come first
  • The title is 60 characters or fewer before any store name your theme adds
  • The title matches the product name shown on the page
  • The meta description is 155 characters or fewer
  • The first sentence says what the product is
  • At least one fact answers a real buyer question (size, fit, compatibility, materials)
  • Every claim is something you can prove
  • There's no outdated price, date or promotion
  • The description isn't reused on another product

How to update many products at once

You can do this in a spreadsheet instead of opening every product.

  1. Go to Products, then Export, and download the CSV. Save an untouched copy as your backup.
  2. Find the columns SEO Title and SEO Description.
  3. Add a temporary column with =LEN(cell) for each field so you can see the character counts and sort the long ones to the top.
  4. Write your titles and descriptions. Delete the temporary columns.
  5. Test first: keep the header row plus one product (all of its rows, if it has variants) and import it with Overwrite products with matching handles ticked. Check that product carefully.
  6. Import the rest, then spot-check a few live product pages.

Overwriting can replace existing product data with whatever is in your file, so leave the other columns as exported.

Which products to start with

If you have more than a handful, don't start at the top of the list. Work in this order:

  1. Products that already sell or get traffic. Your Shopify analytics will show these.
  2. Products with blank or copied descriptions.
  3. Seasonal items, early enough for the season.

Once they're published, leave them alone for a few weeks. Google needs time to recrawl your pages, and it may keep showing its own version of the snippet anyway. If you've connected Google Search Console, compare clicks on the edited pages before and after your changes.
