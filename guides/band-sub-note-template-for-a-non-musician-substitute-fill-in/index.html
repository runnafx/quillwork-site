Band Sub Note Template for a Non-Musician Substitute (Fill-In)

A copy-and-fill sub note for middle and high school band, orchestra and choir directors whose substitute can't read music. It includes a run sheet, room rules, a worked example and a backup activity with an answer key.

Most sub note templates assume the substitute can teach the subject. In a music room that often isn't true. Your sub may never have stood in front of 60 students with instruments within reach, and the room may have no desks.

A good music sub note makes three decisions ahead of time: whether instruments come out, where students sit, and how the work gets marked. The template below settles all three. It works for band, orchestra and choir, and it should fit on one page.

Before you write it: 4 decisions

  1. Instruments out or not? If you won't be there, the simplest rule is no instruments, and no singing rehearsal either. A sub can't fix a squeaky reed or tell whether a section is on task. They also shouldn't have to judge who may open which case.
  2. Where do students write? If you have no desks, say what students will use: clipboards, folders on their laps, or the floor. Name the place where clipboards and pencils are kept.
  3. What happens to the instrument storage room? Decide whether it stays locked or students can drop off cases as usual. Then state it in one line.
  4. Who marks the work? Choose work with one right answer, so the sub can mark it from a key, or collect it for you to mark on your return.

The fill-in sub note

Copy this into a document and fill in the brackets. Leave anything that changes daily blank, and complete it by hand when you know you'll be out.

Welcome, and thank you for covering [CLASS NAME] in room [ROOM]. You do not need to read music or know anything about music to run today's class.

Today in one sentence: Students complete a silent written assignment. No instruments and no singing today.

Schedule

  • Period [__]: [ENSEMBLE], [TIME to TIME], about [NUMBER] students
  • Period [__]: [ENSEMBLE], [TIME to TIME], about [NUMBER] students
  • Prep or lunch: [TIME to TIME]

Where things are

  • Attendance roster or login: [LOCATION]
  • Seating chart: [LOCATION]
  • Student work copies: [LOCATION]
  • Answer key: [LOCATION, kept away from students]
  • Clipboards and pencils: [LOCATION]
  • Finished work goes in: [TRAY OR FOLDER]

Run sheet

  • First 5 minutes: Students enter and leave cases and bags at [PLACE]. Take attendance from the seating chart.
  • Next 3 minutes: Read the directions aloud. Say: "Your director has asked for no instruments today. This is written work, done silently."
  • Next [__] minutes: Students work. Walk the room.
  • Last 5 minutes: Collect the work, then mark it from the key or leave it in the tray.

Rules for the room

  • Instrument storage room: [LOCKED / OPEN ONLY FOR CASE DROP-OFF]
  • Podium, stands, percussion and the piano are off limits today.
  • Bathroom passes: [YOUR POLICY]
  • Phones: [YOUR POLICY]

If a student says, "But we always play on [DAY]" or "We have a concert coming up" Say: "Your director left clear instructions. There's no playing today, and you can take it up with them when they're back."

If a student asks to practice in a practice room [YES, ONLY THESE STUDENTS: ______ / NO]

Students who can help Reliable students who know the routine: [2 OR 3 NAMES] These students can show you where things are. They do not run the class.

Students who need support Accommodations and medical information: [WHERE THE SCHOOL KEEPS THIS] Students needing extra help or seating needs: [NAMES AND NOTES, per school policy]

Who to call

  • Nearby teacher: [NAME, ROOM, EXTENSION]
  • Office: [EXTENSION]
  • Behavior support: [NAME OR PROCESS]
  • Emergency procedures: [LOCATION OF SCHOOL'S POSTED PLAN]

If you finish early: Use the backup activity in the folder.

Please leave me a note about: who was absent, anyone who was especially helpful, anything broken, and anything I should follow up on.

Worked example: the parts that matter most

Here is how a middle school band director with no desks in the room might fill in the key lines. The class and details are made up for illustration.

Today in one sentence: Seventh grade band completes the rhythm counting sheet in silence. No instruments and no singing today.

Where things are: Copies and clipboards are on the black cart by the door. The answer key is in the red folder in the top desk drawer.

Run sheet: Students put cases on the shelves as usual, then go straight to their normal chairs. Clipboards are passed down each row. The class works for 30 minutes, and the row leaders collect clipboards at the end.

Rules for the room: The storage room stays open for the first 5 minutes only, then please close it. Nobody touches the timpani or drum set, including percussionists.

Notice how specific those lines are. "Clipboards are on the black cart by the door" saves the sub from searching a room they've never seen, while "Materials are in the band room" doesn't.

Choose work a non-musician can actually mark

Before you leave an assignment, test it with this question: could someone who has never had a music lesson mark this from a printed key?

Usually safe:

  • Beat-value arithmetic with a right number as the answer
  • Vocabulary matching, such as tempo and dynamic terms
  • A short reading with comprehension questions
  • Instrument or voice-part sorting
  • A written reflection marked for completion only

Usually a problem:

  • Sight-reading or playing tests
  • "Listen and describe" tasks that depend on a working speaker or a login
  • Marking notes on a staff, unless the key shows exactly what to accept
  • Sectionals led by students, unless you've already trained the leaders and your school allows it
  • Movies, unless you've checked your school's media rules and confirmed the equipment works

A backup activity you can use tomorrow

Write this on the board or put it in the folder. It takes about 10 minutes and needs only pencil and paper.

Directions: Use these values to solve each problem: whole note = 4 beats, half note = 2 beats, quarter note = 1 beat, eighth note = half a beat.

  1. Two half notes + one quarter note = how many beats?
  2. One whole note + two eighth notes = how many beats?
  3. Three quarter notes + one half note = how many beats?
  4. Six eighth notes = how many beats?
  5. How many quarter notes equal one whole note?
  6. How many eighth notes equal one half note?
  7. One whole note + one half note + one quarter note = how many beats?
  8. A measure holds 4 beats. It already has one half note and one quarter note. How many more beats are needed to fill it?

Answer key (for the substitute only): 1) 5, 2) 5, 3) 5, 4) 3, 5) 4, 6) 4, 7) 7, 8) 1

For students who finish early: Write three problems of your own, with answers on the back, then swap with a neighbor.

Final check before you leave

  • The first line tells the sub they don't need musical knowledge.
  • "No instruments and no singing" appears in plain words.
  • Every location names a specific place.
  • Musical words in the sub's instructions are explained or removed.
  • The answer key is separate from the student copies.
  • The note matches your school's rules for attendance, student information and emergencies.
  • A non-musician colleague or friend can read the note and tell you what happens first.

Store one complete copy in a labeled folder in the main office, and another where your department or a neighboring teacher can find it. Update the seating charts and helper names whenever your classes change.
