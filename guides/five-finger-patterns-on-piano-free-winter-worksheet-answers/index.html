Five-Finger Patterns on Piano: Free Winter Worksheet + Answers

A free, print-friendly winter worksheet that teaches major and minor five-finger patterns (pentascales) with whole and half steps. It includes four activities, a full answer key and teaching tips for Level 1 and Level 2 piano students.

Five-finger patterns, also called pentascales, often come up in December lessons. Students have learned whole and half steps, and they are ready to build something with them. This page gives you a complete winter-themed worksheet you can copy into a document and print. It comes with a full answer key and some notes on teaching it in a 10 to 15 minute slot.

It works best for Level 1 students who already know whole and half steps and Level 2 students who are starting major and minor. If your student still mixes up whole and half steps, spend a lesson on that first.

The Two Formulas Your Student Needs

Every five-finger pattern has 5 notes and 4 "moves" between them. Each move is either a whole step (W) or a half step (H).

  • Major pattern: W W H W. Example: C D E F G.
  • Minor pattern: W H W W. Example: C D E-flat F G.

The only difference is the third note. In minor, finger 3 moves down one half step. Students find that easier to remember than two separate formulas, so say it out loud often: "Minor means finger 3 slides down."

Quick reminder for students:

  • Half step: the very next key, black or white, with no key in between.
  • Whole step: two half steps, which skips exactly one key.

Part 3: Gingerbread Detective (Major or Minor?)

Look at each gingerbread cookie's notes. Check the step pattern, then circle MAJOR or MINOR.

  1. E F-sharp G-sharp A B: MAJOR / MINOR
  2. D E F G A: MAJOR / MINOR

  5. A B C-sharp D E: MAJOR / MINOR
  6. E F-sharp G A B: MAJOR / MINOR

Part 4: Fix the Frozen Note

A snowflake froze one note in each pattern. One note in each is wrong. Circle it, then write the correct note.

  1. D major: D E F G A. Wrong note: ___ Correct note: ___
  2. F major: F G A B C. Wrong note: ___ Correct note: ___
  3. G major: G A B C-sharp D. Wrong note: ___ Correct note: ___
  4. A major: A B C D E. Wrong note: ___ Correct note: ___
  5. C minor: C D E F G. Wrong note: ___ Correct note: ___

Part 5: At the Piano (Play It)

Do these with your teacher or at home practice.

  1. Play C major with your right hand, fingers 1 to 5, going up and back down.
  2. Now play it again, but slide finger 3 down a half step. What did you make? ____________
  3. Play G major and G minor with your left hand, starting on finger 5.
  4. Choose one pattern from Part 2. Play it hands together, slowly, three times.

How to Teach This in One Lesson

Before the worksheet (3 minutes). Have the student play C major on the piano and say "whole, whole, half, whole" out loud while playing. Then have them slide finger 3 down and say "whole, half, whole, whole." If they hear the change before they see it written, Parts 3 and 4 go much faster.

Parts 1 and 2 (5 minutes). Do these in the lesson. Watch for two common mistakes:

  • Counting white keys instead of half steps. If a student writes D E F G A for D major, put their finger on E and ask, "What's the very next key?" They'll find F is right next door, so that's only a half step.
  • Using the wrong letter. Each note in a five-finger pattern uses the next letter name, so G-flat in F major should be F-sharp in D major, not G-flat. If a student writes "D E G-flat G A", point out that the letter F was skipped and G was used twice.

Parts 3 and 4 (5 minutes or as homework). These are good to send home because the student is checking rather than building. If they get Part 4 right, they understand the formula, not just the C position.

Part 5 (at the piano). Always end at the keyboard. A student can write F G A B-flat C correctly and still play B natural with finger 4, so check that their eyes and hands agree.

Making It Easier or Harder

For a younger or newer Level 1 student:

  • Do only C, G and F major in Part 2, and skip minor for now.
  • Let them use a printed keyboard diagram and mark the keys before they write letters.
  • In Part 3, tell them to look only at the third note.

For a Level 2 student who finds it easy:

  • Ask them to write all 12 major five-finger patterns, starting on each key.
  • Have them write the left-hand finger numbers above each note (5 4 3 2 1 going up).
  • Ask: "Which major patterns use only white keys?" (Answer: C, F with one black key, G. Strictly all-white: C only. All-white minor patterns are D minor, E minor... no. Check below.)

All-white five-finger patterns, for checking that bonus question:

  • Major: C major (C D E F G) is the only all-white major pattern.
  • Minor: A minor (A B C D E) and D minor (D E F G A) are all-white. E minor needs F-sharp, so it doesn't count.

A Simple Plan for the Weeks Before Break

If you want this to fit into a short December run of lessons, here is one way to space it out:

  1. Week 1: whole and half steps review, then Parts 1 and 2 (major only).
  2. Week 2: introduce minor with the "finger 3 slides down" idea, then Part 3.
  3. Week 3: Part 4 as a quick check, and Part 5 hands together.
  4. Week 4: a quick review game. Name a starting note and "major" or "minor," and the student plays it without looking at the page.

Students in group lessons can do Part 3 as a race in pairs. One student reads the notes and the other calls out "major" or "minor."

Printing Tips

  • Print in black and white. The worksheet doesn't need color, and students can draw their own mittens and snowmen in the margins once they finish.
  • Make the font large enough for younger students to write clearly, about 14 point.
  • For students who are less confident, put the answer key on the back of the homework page and ask them to check their own work at home.
