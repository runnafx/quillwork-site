Airbnb Check-In Message Templates: What to Send and When

Copy-and-paste message scripts for every stage of a short-term rental stay, from booking confirmation to checkout. There's a send-time checklist and fill-in wording for each message.

Most guest questions come down to a few things: how do I get in, where do I park, what's the Wi-Fi, and what do I do when I leave. If the right message reaches the guest at the right time, they mostly stop asking. This page gives you a complete set of messages you can paste into Airbnb or VRBO scheduled messages. Each one says when to send it and has [bracket] placeholders to fill in.

Fill in the brackets once, save each message as a scheduled message or quick reply, and you're done for every future booking.

Before you start: gather these details once

Most of the work is collecting facts, not writing. Have these ready before you fill in the templates:

  • Full street address, plus any quirks (for example, "GPS sends you to the back alley")
  • Check-in time and checkout time
  • Entry method: lockbox location and code, smart-lock code format, or key handoff
  • Parking: where to park, how many cars, and where not to park
  • Wi-Fi network name and password
  • Where your house manual or guidebook is, in the home or online
  • Checkout tasks you actually want done, kept short
  • Your response hours and how urgent issues reach you

Tip: Use a new door code for each booking if your lock allows it. Then the code goes only in the pre-arrival message, never in your listing or guidebook.

The message timeline at a glance

  1. Booking confirmation: right after the booking is confirmed
  2. Pre-arrival message: 2 to 3 days before check-in
  3. Check-in day message: the morning of arrival
  4. First-night check-in: a few hours after the check-in time
  5. Mid-stay check-in: optional, for stays of 4 nights or more
  6. Checkout reminder: the evening before checkout
  7. Thank-you and review request: a few hours after checkout

Seven messages sounds like a lot. Once they're scheduled, though, you never write them again.

1. Booking confirmation message

When: Right after the booking is confirmed. Goal: Reassure the guest and say when the details will arrive. Don't send the entry code yet.

> Hi [Guest first name], thanks for booking [Property name]! We're looking forward to hosting you from [check-in date] to [checkout date]. > > Check-in is from [check-in time] and checkout is by [checkout time]. I'll send full arrival instructions, including the door code and parking details, on [day before arrival or 2 to 3 days before]. > > If you're celebrating anything or have questions about the area, just reply here. See you soon! > [Your name]

Concise version:

> Hi [Guest first name], your stay at [Property name] is confirmed for [dates]. Check-in [time], checkout [time]. Full arrival instructions will arrive [timing]. Questions? Just reply here.

2. Pre-arrival message (the one that matters most)

When: 2 to 3 days before check-in. Goal: Give the guest everything needed to get from the car to the couch without messaging you.

> Hi [Guest first name], your stay at [Property name] is almost here! Here's everything you need for arrival. > > Address: [Full address] > [One line of directions, for example: "It's the blue house with the white fence. If GPS takes you to [wrong spot], continue to [landmark]."] > > Check-in: from [time]. [If early check-in is possible: "Early check-in is sometimes possible. Message me the day before and I'll let you know."] > > Getting in: [Pick one] > - Lockbox: The lockbox is [exact location]. Code: [code]. Please scramble the numbers after use. > - Smart lock: Press the keypad, then enter [code] followed by [the check/lock button]. Your code works from [time] on [date]. > > Parking: [Where to park, how many spots, any spots to avoid] > > Wi-Fi: Network [name], password [password] > > Guidebook: You'll find our house guide [on the kitchen counter / in the drawer by the door / at the link in your Airbnb app]. It covers the appliances, trash days and local recommendations. > > Safe travels, and message me if anything comes up. > [Your name]

Checklist before you save this one:

  • Test the directions yourself on a phone map app
  • Check the lockbox or keypad wording against the actual device
  • Make sure the code-valid time matches your check-in time
  • Read it on a phone screen. Guests read it standing at the door, so short lines win

3. Check-in day message

When: The morning of arrival. Goal: Put the key details back at the top of the thread, since guests rarely scroll up to find them.

> Good morning [Guest first name]! Just a quick reminder for today: > - Address: [address] > - Check-in from [time] > - Door code: [code] > - Parking: [one-line summary] > > I'm available until [time] today if you need anything.

This short repeat prevents the most common arrival-day message: "What was the code again?"

4. First-night check-in

When: About 2 to 4 hours after check-in time. Goal: Catch small problems early, before they turn into a complaint in the review.

> Hi [Guest first name], hope you got in smoothly! Is everything working as expected: the Wi-Fi, heating or cooling, and hot water? If anything isn't right, let me know now so I can fix it while you're here.

Keep it to one message. If they don't reply, assume all is well and leave them alone.

5. Mid-stay check-in (optional)

When: Middle of a stay of 4 nights or more. Goal: Offer help without hovering.

> Hi [Guest first name], just checking in halfway through your stay. Do you need anything, like extra towels or [coffee pods / firewood / trash bags]? Fresh supplies are in [location]. Otherwise, enjoy the rest of your time!

Skip this one for short stays. It can feel like too much.

6. Checkout reminder

When: The evening before checkout, around 5 to 7 p.m. local time. Goal: A clear, short list of what you actually need from the guest.

> Hi [Guest first name], thanks for staying with us! A quick reminder that checkout is by [time] tomorrow. Before you go, please: > 1. [Start the dishwasher / leave dishes in the sink] > 2. [Take trash to the bins at location] > 3. [Leave used towels in the bathtub / laundry basket] > 4. [Lock the door: smart lock locks with one press of the button / return the key to the lockbox] > > No need to strip the beds or do any other cleaning. We take care of that. Safe travels!

Keep the list to 3 to 5 items. Long checkout chore lists are a frequent source of guest frustration. Ask only for what matters to your cleaning turnover, and make sure anything you require matches what your listing says.

7. Thank-you and review request

When: A few hours after checkout, once you've confirmed the home is in good shape. Goal: Thank the guest and remind them to leave a review without pressuring them.

> Hi [Guest first name], thank you for staying at [Property name]! We hope you enjoyed [the area / your trip / the celebration]. If you have a moment, we'd really appreciate a review. It helps other travelers know what to expect. We'd be happy to host you again anytime. > [Your name]

What not to do: Don't offer discounts, refunds or gifts in exchange for a review, and don't ask for a specific star rating. Platform review policies prohibit these, so keep the request neutral.

Bonus: three quick replies worth saving

These handle the questions that still come in no matter how good your messages are.

"Can we check in early?"

> Thanks for asking! Early check-in depends on our cleaning schedule. I'll know by [time] on [day] and will message you then. If it isn't possible, you're welcome to [park in the driveway / drop bags at location] from [time].

"Can we check out late?"

> I'll check whether there's a booking right after yours and let you know by [time]. If the calendar is open, a [time] checkout should be fine.

"The code isn't working."

> Sorry about that! Please try this: [press keypad first / enter code then press lock button / make sure the lockbox dials line up]. If it still won't open, call or message me right away at [contact method shown in your listing] and I'll sort it out.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending the code too early. Plans change and messages get forwarded. Send it in the pre-arrival message, not at booking.
  • Burying the entry details. Put address, code and parking in the first few lines, not under a long welcome paragraph.
  • Copying messages that don't match your property. Read each template against your actual lock, parking and trash setup.
  • Forgetting time zones. Scheduled messages send at times you set. Check that 7 p.m. means the guest's evening, not yours, if you manage remotely.
  • Contradicting your listing. Check-in times, house rules and checkout tasks in your messages should match what the guest agreed to when booking.

How to set these up in about 30 minutes

  1. Fill in every [bracket] above in a single document.
  2. Read each message aloud once. Cut anything that sounds stiff.
  3. In your hosting app, create a scheduled message for each one and set the timing listed above. Both Airbnb and VRBO have scheduled or automated messaging tools. Menu names change, so search your host help center for "scheduled messages" if you can't find them.
  4. Save the three bonus replies as quick replies or saved responses.
  5. Send a test booking or preview to yourself where the platform allows it, and read every message on your phone.

Once they're scheduled, these messages cover every booking. You only need to update them when a code system, parking setup or checkout routine changes.
