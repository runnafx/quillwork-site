Steps and Skips on the Staff: Free Winter Worksheet + Answers

A free, no-prep steps and skips worksheet for beginner piano students, with snowman-themed drills, a short melody to analyze and a full answer key. You can print it as is or copy it onto staff paper in five minutes.

Reading by steps and skips (intervals) is the habit that gets a beginner off note-by-note decoding. A student who can see that the next note is "one step up" doesn't have to name every note. This page gives you a short explanation you can read aloud, four winter-themed drills and an answer key. It's built for Primer and early Level 1 students and takes about 10 to 15 minutes of lesson time.

The Rule in One Minute (Read This to Your Student)

  • Step: the next note up or down. On the staff, a step goes from a line to the next space or from a space to the next line. On the keyboard, it's the next white key. In letters, it's the next letter (C to D).
  • Skip: jump over one note. On the staff, a skip goes from a line to the next line or from a space to the next space. On the keyboard, you skip one white key. In letters, you skip one letter (C to E).
  • Repeat: the same note again. Same line or space, same key, same letter.

A quick memory hook: "Line-space is a step. Line-line or space-space is a skip."

The musical alphabet only uses A B C D E F G, then starts again at A. So G to A is a step, and G to B is a skip.

How to Use This Page

  1. Print it, or have the student write answers on any paper.
  2. For Part 1, number the staff with your student first: lines 1 to 5 and spaces 1 to 4, counting from the bottom up.
  3. Students write S for step, K for skip, or R for repeat. Using K for skip keeps it from looking like S for step.
  4. For younger students, add "up" or "down" to each answer only once they're secure with S, K and R.
  5. Check against the answer key at the bottom.

Part 1: Snowflake Staff Positions

Each snowflake moves from one place on the staff to another. Write S (step), K (skip) or R (repeat). Remember: L = line and Sp = space, counted from the bottom.

  1. Line 1 to Space 1: ____
  2. Line 2 to Line 3: ____
  3. Space 2 to Space 3: ____
  4. Line 3 to Space 3: ____
  5. Space 4 to Line 5: ____
  6. Line 4 to Line 4: ____
  7. Space 1 to Line 2: ____
  8. Line 1 to Line 2: ____
  9. Space 3 to Space 2: ____
  10. Line 5 to Space 4: ____
  11. Line 3 to Line 2: ____
  12. Space 2 to Space 2: ____

Teacher tip: If you want real notation, draw these 12 pairs on staff paper as two whole notes per box, with a small snowflake above each box. It takes about five minutes and you can reuse the sheet every year.

Part 2: Mitten Letter Pairs

Each pair of mittens has two letter names. Say the musical alphabet out loud if you need to. Write S, K or R.

  1. C to D: ____
  2. C to E: ____
  3. G to A: ____
  4. F to A: ____
  5. G to B: ____
  6. B to C: ____
  7. E to G: ____
  8. A to G: ____
  9. D to B: ____
  10. F to F: ____
  11. A to C: ____
  12. G to E: ____

Watch for: Items 3, 5, 6 and 11 cross the G-to-A "wrap-around." Students often get these wrong because they expect the alphabet to keep going to H. Items 8, 9 and 12 go down, so the student has to say the alphabet backwards.

Part 3: The Snowman Melody

Here is a short melody in letter names. Write S, K or R in each gap to show how the melody moves from one note to the next.

C __ D __ E __ E __ D __ C __ E __ G __ G

There are 8 gaps. When the sheet is done, have the student play the melody with right hand thumb on C (C position: C D E F G). Ask: "Which part moved by steps? Where did the snowman jump?"

Extension: Ask the student to write their own 6-note "snowman melody" using at least two steps, one skip and one repeat, then play it for you.

Part 4: Gingerbread Path (Write the Next Note)

Start on the given note. Follow each instruction to find the next note, and write the letter name. Each answer is the starting note for the next line. Use the treble staff: Line 2 is G.

  1. Start on G (Line 2). Step up. New note: ____
  2. From that note, skip up. New note: ____
  3. From that note, step down. New note: ____
  4. From that note, skip down. New note: ____
  5. From that note, repeat. New note: ____

For the staff version, students also write where each note sits, for example "Space 2." That links the letter names to staff positions, which is the whole point of the exercise.

Common Mistakes and Quick Fixes

  • Counting the starting note as "one step." Some students count "G is 1, A is 2, so it's a skip." Fix it by saying "a step is a move to the next note." Count the moves, not the notes.
  • Calling line-to-line a step because the lines "look next to each other." Have the student put a finger on Line 2 and slide it up. Their finger crosses a space before it reaches Line 3. That space is the note they skipped.
  • Forgetting the alphabet wraps. Write A B C D E F G A B C in a circle on a paper "wreath." Students can trace it with a finger.
  • Mixing up up and down. Before any direction work, have them point up on the staff (toward Line 5) and up on the keyboard (to the right). Both mean higher.
  • Getting the drill right but not using it in their pieces. Right after the worksheet, open their current piece and ask them to find one step, one skip and one repeat. That move to real music is where reading gets faster.

Adapting by Level

  • Primer: Do Parts 2 and 3 only, steps and repeats first, then add skips. Have them play each pair on the keyboard after writing the answer.
  • Late Primer / early Level 1: Do all four parts. Add up and down to every answer.
  • Level 1 to Level 2: Replace S and K with interval numbers: a step is a 2nd, a skip is a 3rd, a repeat is a unison. Then add 4ths and 5ths, for example "Line 1 to Space 2 is a 4th" and "Line 1 to Line 3 is a 5th."

Part 3: The Snowman Melody

C S D S E R E S D S C K E K G R G

That's 4 steps, 2 skips and 2 repeats. The melody steps up, holds, steps back down to C, then jumps up by skips to G.

Part 4: Gingerbread Path (treble staff)

  1. A (Space 2): a step up from G
  2. C (Space 3): a skip up from A, skipping B
  3. B (Line 3): a step down from C
  4. G (Line 2): a skip down from B, skipping A
  5. G (Line 2): repeat

The path ends where it started, on G. Students like noticing that.

What to Do Next

Once a student gets about 10 of 12 right in Parts 1 and 2 without counting on their fingers, move on to reading short step-and-skip patterns at the keyboard without naming the notes. Point to the first note, name it once, then have them play the rest by direction and distance only. That's where interval reading pays off in their December pieces.
