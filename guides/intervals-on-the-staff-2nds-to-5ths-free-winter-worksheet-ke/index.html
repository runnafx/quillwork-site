Intervals on the Staff (2nds to 5ths): Free Winter Worksheet + Key

A free winter worksheet for teaching beginner piano students to read 2nds, 3rds, 4ths and 5ths on the treble and bass staff. It includes a line-and-space shortcut, a lesson script, 20 questions and a full answer key.

Students who can read steps and skips are ready for intervals. An interval is the distance between two notes. At this level students only need the interval number (2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th). Interval quality (major, minor, perfect) can wait until later.

This page gives you a short teaching script, a shortcut that students remember, a three-part winter worksheet you can copy out by hand or read aloud at the lesson, and a full answer key. It suits students at about Level 1 to Level 2 in most method books.

The one rule students must learn first

Count both notes. To find an interval, start counting at the bottom note as "1" and keep counting letter names up to the top note.

  • C up to E: C (1), D (2), E (3). That is a 3rd.
  • G up to C: G (1), A (2), B (3), C (4). That is a 4th.

A common mistake is to count the gaps between the notes instead of the notes themselves, which gives an answer one too small. If a student says C to E is a "2nd", have them say the letters out loud and touch each key.

The line-and-space shortcut

Once counting makes sense, show students what each interval looks like on the staff:

  • 2nd: line to the next space, or space to the next line. The notes touch.
  • 3rd: line to the next line, or space to the next space. One line or space is skipped between them.
  • 4th: line to a space, or space to a line, with two lines or spaces between them.
  • 5th: line to a line with one line skipped, or space to a space with one space skipped.

A pattern students can check: odd intervals (3rd, 5th) are line-line or space-space. Even intervals (2nd, 4th) are line-space or space-line. If a student says a line-to-line interval is a 4th, the shortcut tells them right away that something is wrong.

A 5-minute teaching script

  1. On the keyboard: Play C, then E. Ask, "How many white keys from C to E if we count both?" (Three, so it is a 3rd.) Repeat with C–D, C–F and C–G.
  2. Fingers: In a C five-finger position, finger 1 to finger 2 is a 2nd, 1 to 3 is a 3rd, 1 to 4 is a 4th and 1 to 5 is a 5th. Students can feel the interval in their hand.
  3. On the staff: Write C–E and G–D in the treble clef. Ask whether each pair is line-line, space-space or mixed. Then teach the odd/even pattern.
  4. Ears (optional): Play a 2nd and a 5th and ask which sounds "close" and which sounds "wide and open".
  5. Worksheet: Hand out the sheet below.

Snowman Intervals Worksheet

Students can do this on paper by drawing each pair of notes on a blank staff. Or you can read the note names aloud and have students answer at the piano.

Reminder for students:

  • Treble clef lines (bottom to top): E, G, B, D, F. Spaces: F, A, C, E.
  • Bass clef lines (bottom to top): G, B, D, F, A. Spaces: A, C, E, G.

Part A: Name the interval (the snowman's buttons)

Each snowman has two buttons. Write the interval between them: 2nd, 3rd, 4th or 5th.

Treble clef

  1. E (bottom line) up to F (first space)
  2. G (second line) up to B (middle line)
  3. F (first space) up to C (third space)
  4. A (second space) up to D (fourth line)
  5. E (bottom line) up to B (middle line)
  6. C (third space) up to D (fourth line)
  7. D (fourth line) up to F (top line)
  8. G (second line) up to C (third space)

Bass clef

  1. G (bottom line) up to D (middle line)
  2. C (second space) up to E (third space)
  3. B (second line) up to C (second space)
  4. A (first space) up to D (middle line)

Part B: Build the interval (mitten pairs)

Each mitten needs a partner. Write or play the missing note.

  1. Up a 3rd from F = ____
  2. Up a 5th from C = ____
  3. Down a 4th from G = ____
  4. Up a 2nd from B = ____
  5. Down a 3rd from E = ____
  6. Up a 4th from D = ____
  7. Down a 5th from A = ____
  8. Up a 5th from G = ____

Part C: Gingerbread hop (at the piano)

The gingerbread person hops from key to key. Play each hop, then say the interval out loud.

  • Hop 1: C to G
  • Hop 2: G to A
  • Hop 3: A to D
  • Hop 4: D to F

Bonus: Make up your own four-hop gingerbread path that uses each interval (2nd, 3rd, 4th and 5th) once. Write down the letter names.

Part C

  • Hop 1: C to G is a 5th (up)
  • Hop 2: G to A is a 2nd (up)
  • Hop 3: A to D is a 4th if the student hops up (A-B-C-D). Played downward to the nearest D (A-G-F-E-D), it is a 5th. Accept either answer if the student can say which direction they hopped.
  • Hop 4: D to F is a 3rd (up)
  • Bonus: Answers will vary. To check, count the letter names of each hop, including both notes.

Common mistakes and quick fixes

  • Answer is one too small: The student is counting gaps, not notes. Have them point to each note and say "1" on the starting note.
  • Mixing up treble and bass names: Before the worksheet, have the student say the line and space names of both clefs out loud.
  • Counting the wrong way when building down: For "down a 4th from G", count backwards through the alphabet: G (1), F (2), E (3), D (4).
  • Thinking a bigger interval number means a higher note: Explain that an interval is a distance. A 3rd can go up or down.
  • Guessing on the staff: Have them check the odd/even pattern. A line-to-line answer must be a 3rd or a 5th.

How to stretch it over several lessons

  • Week 1: Teach counting at the keyboard and do Part C.
  • Week 2: Teach the line-and-space shortcut and do Part A.
  • Week 3: Do Part B. Then find and circle one 3rd and one 5th in a piece the student is already learning.
  • Week 4: Quick quiz. Play or point to ten random intervals and time the student. Next week, see if they can beat that time.

When to move on

A student is ready for interval quality (major and minor 3rds, perfect 4ths and 5ths) when they can name 2nds to 5ths on both staves quickly without counting on their fingers. They should also be able to build an interval up or down from any white-key note. Until then, keep practising interval numbers. Most later interval work builds on this skill.
