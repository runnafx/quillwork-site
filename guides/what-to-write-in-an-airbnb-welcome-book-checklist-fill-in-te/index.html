What to Write in an Airbnb Welcome Book: Checklist + Fill-In Text

A section-by-section checklist for an Airbnb or VRBO welcome book, with fill-in-the-blank wording you can copy. Turn your rough notes into a guidebook guests will actually use.

Plenty of hosts buy a nice welcome book template and then leave it half blank. The layout was never the hard part. The hard part is the writing: how the lockbox works, which switch runs the garbage disposal, where the recycling goes on Tuesdays.

This page covers that writing. Each section has a short checklist and fill-in wording you can copy into any template, a Google Doc or a printed binder. Replace the [brackets] with your details and delete anything that doesn't apply to your place.

Before you start: collect your raw notes

Don't try to write polished text straight away. First, get everything out of your head. Use your phone's voice memo app, walk through the property, and talk through:

  • How a guest gets from the street to inside the door
  • Every appliance a guest might use, and any quirks it has
  • Where the trash, recycling and spare supplies are kept
  • What you'd tell a friend who was staying there for a week

That rough recording or list is your source material. The sections below turn it into something a tired guest can read at 11pm.

1. Welcome page

Include: a short greeting, your first name, the best way to reach you, and where to find the Wi-Fi details.

Fill-in text:

> Welcome to [property name]! We're glad you're here. I'm [your name], and I look after this place myself. If you need anything during your stay, message me through [Airbnb / VRBO app] or text [phone number]. The Wi-Fi details are on the next page.

Keep this page short. Guests flip past it to find the Wi-Fi.

2. Wi-Fi and TV

Checklist:

  • Network name and password, spelled exactly as they appear (watch for capital letters, zeros and the letter O)
  • Where the router is, and what to do if the connection drops
  • How to turn on the TV and switch inputs
  • Which streaming apps are installed, and a reminder to log out at checkout

Fill-in text:

> Wi-Fi network: [network name] > Password: [password] > If the internet stops working, unplug the router [location, e.g. "on the shelf under the TV"], wait 30 seconds and plug it back in. It takes about two minutes to reconnect. > TV: Use the [brand] remote. Press [button] to switch to [input/app]. Please sign out of any personal streaming accounts before you leave.

3. Check-in and check-out

This section prevents the most messages, so write it step by step.

Check-in checklist:

  • Check-in time, and whether early check-in is possible
  • Exact address, plus any unit, gate or building details
  • Which door to use, and how to find it if it isn't obvious
  • How the lock works (code, lockbox or smart lock) and what to do if it doesn't work

Fill-in text:

> Check-in: from [time]. If you'd like to arrive earlier, ask me and I'll do my best. > 1. Park [where; see Parking section]. > 2. Go to the [front/side/back] door, which is [landmark, e.g. "the blue door next to the mailbox"]. > 3. Enter your code [code is sent on day of arrival / code] on the keypad and press [button]. > 4. If the lock doesn't open, wait 10 seconds and try again. If it still doesn't work, call me at [number].

Check-out checklist: keep the list short and reasonable. A long chore list annoys guests and can lead to complaints in reviews.

> Check-out: by [time]. Before you go, please: > - Load and start the dishwasher > - Put used towels [in the tub / in the basket] > - Take trash to [location] > - Turn off lights and lock the door [how] > That's it. No need to strip the beds.

4. House rules

Write these in plain, friendly language. Make sure they match the rules on your listing. If the book and the listing say different things, a guest will notice.

Common items:

  • Maximum number of guests
  • Quiet hours
  • Smoking and vaping
  • Pets
  • Parties or events
  • Shoes, pool or hot tub use, fireplace

Fill-in text:

> To keep the house in good shape and our neighbours happy: > - Quiet hours are [time] to [time]. > - No smoking or vaping anywhere on the property, including [porch/balcony]. > - Pets: [not allowed / allowed with approval, see listing]. > - No parties or events, and no more than [number] guests.

Local short-term rental rules vary by city and county. Check which rules apply to you, and confirm any wording you're required to post (such as an occupancy limit or permit number) with your local authority.

5. Appliance how-tos

This is the section hosts skip most often and guests need most. Only cover anything a guest can't work out in 10 seconds.

Checklist: thermostat, oven and stove, coffee maker, washer and dryer, dishwasher, shower (especially unusual taps), fireplace, hot tub, garbage disposal, blinds.

Fill-in format (repeat for each):

> [Appliance]: [one-sentence how-to]. [One quirk, if any.]

Examples:

> Thermostat: Press the up or down arrow to set the temperature. Please keep it between [x] and [y]°, and turn it to [setting] when you leave. > Shower: Pull the knob out to start the water, then turn left for hotter water. It takes about 30 seconds to warm up. > Coffee maker: Pods are in the [drawer]. Fill the water tank at the back, insert a pod and press the large button.

6. Parking and trash

Checklist:

  • Exactly where guests may park, and where they must not (a neighbour's spot, a fire lane, the street on cleaning days)
  • Any permit or pass, and where to find it
  • Where the bins are, which bin is for what, and collection day if guests will be there for it

Fill-in text:

> Parking: Park in [location]. There's room for [number] cars. Please don't park [restricted area]. [Permit is on the hook by the door; place it on the dashboard.] > Trash and recycling: Bins are [location]. Black bin = trash, [colour] bin = recycling ([what goes in]). If you're here on [day], please roll the bins to the curb the night before.

7. Emergency and safety info

Supply this information yourself and double-check it. Don't guess at numbers or locations.

Checklist:

  • Local emergency number
  • Full property address (guests in an emergency often don't know it)
  • Your phone number and a backup contact
  • Locations of the fire extinguisher, first aid kit, circuit breaker and water shutoff
  • Nearest urgent care or hospital, and nearest pharmacy (names, which you've confirmed yourself)

Fill-in text:

> In an emergency, call [local emergency number]. The address here is [full address]. > Host: [name, number]. Backup contact: [name, number]. > Fire extinguisher: [location]. First aid kit: [location]. Breaker box: [location]. Water shutoff: [location]. > Nearest urgent care: [name, address]. Nearest pharmacy: [name, address].

8. Local picks

Guests value your personal recommendations more than a long list. Aim for 10 to 15 places you've actually been to, and check that each one is still open before you print.

Suggested mix:

  • 2–3 coffee or breakfast spots
  • 3–4 restaurants at different prices
  • 1 grocery store and 1 pharmacy
  • 2–3 things to do (a walk, a park, a museum, a beach)
  • 1 rainy-day option

Fill-in format:

> [Place name]: [type], [distance, e.g. "5-minute walk"]. [One sentence on why you like it or what to order.]

> Example: [Bakery name]: café, 5-minute walk. Go early on Saturdays; the cinnamon buns sell out.

9. Guest messages to pair with the book

The book works best when your messages point guests to it at the right moments. Here are five short templates:

  1. Booking confirmation: "Thanks for booking [property], [guest name]! I'll send check-in details [x] days before you arrive. Our digital guidebook is here: [where you share it]."
  2. Pre-arrival (2–3 days before): "Your stay is coming up! Check-in is from [time]. Directions, parking and the door code are in the guidebook under 'Check-in.' Let me know your rough arrival time."
  3. Check-in day: "Welcome! Your door code is [code]. Wi-Fi details are on the first page of the welcome book on the [counter/table]."
  4. Mid-stay (morning after arrival): "Hope you're settling in well. Is there anything you need? Our local picks are in section 8 of the guidebook."
  5. Check-out (evening before): "Thanks for staying with us! Check-out is [time] tomorrow. The short checklist is in the guidebook. Safe travels."

Final check before you print

  • Read it on your phone. Many guests will see a digital copy first.
  • Have someone who has never been to your place follow the check-in steps exactly as written.
  • Check that the Wi-Fi password, door instructions and house rules match your listing.
  • Put a date on the last page and review the book whenever something changes: a new appliance, a new lock, a restaurant that has closed.
  • Keep a printed copy somewhere obvious, and keep a digital copy you can update in one place.

A welcome book doesn't need to be beautiful to work. It needs to be accurate, specific and easy to scan. Start with your voice memo, then fill in the sections above.
