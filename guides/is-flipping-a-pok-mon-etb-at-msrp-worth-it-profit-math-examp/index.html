Is Flipping a Pokémon ETB at MSRP Worth It? Profit Math + Example

A fill-in worksheet to check whether one sealed Elite Trainer Box bought at retail will make a profit on eBay after tax, fees, shipping and packing. It includes a worked example using Delta Reign ETB prices from November 2026.

When a new Pokémon set comes out, the Elite Trainer Box often sells on eBay for well above its shelf price for a few weeks. Price gaps like "$49.99 in store, $90 on eBay" make it look like easy money. But after sales tax, eBay fees, a shipping label and packing, much of that gap is gone. If prices drop while your box is listed, the rest can go too.

This page shows you how to do the math before you pay at the register. It includes a fill-in worksheet, a worked example with real numbers from one set's launch, a formula for your highest safe buy price and a go/no-go checklist.

The five numbers you need

Fill these in before you buy:

  1. Buy price before tax. Use the shelf tag or an employee's answer. Don't trust a website. Retailer product pages can show Marketplace or third-party prices that change during the day.
  2. Your sales tax rate. Use your real local rate.
  3. A realistic sale price, delivered. This is what buyers actually paid, including any shipping they were charged. Use sold listings, not asking prices.
  4. eBay fees. Use the final value fee rate for your category and the per-order fee. eBay usually charges the percentage on the buyer's total, which includes shipping and sales tax. Check eBay's current fee page for your category and account.
  5. Outbound cost. Your shipping label plus packing materials (box, bubble wrap, tape). Weigh and measure a packed box to price the label. Don't guess.

Fill-in worksheet

Copy this into a note and fill in the blanks:

  • Buy price before tax: $____
  • Tax rate: ____%
  • Landed cost (buy price × (1 + tax rate)): $____
  • Sale price, delivered: $____
  • eBay fee (sale price × (1 + buyer tax rate) × fee % + per-order fee): $____
  • Shipping label: $____
  • Packing: $____
  • Net profit (sale price − eBay fee − label − packing − landed cost): $____
  • Break-even sale price (landed cost + label + packing + per-order fee, divided by 1 − (fee % × (1 + buyer tax rate))): $____

If the net profit is under the amount you'd accept for the trip, the waiting and the risk of a return, stop here.

Worked example: Delta Reign ETB, November 2026

This example uses figures from the week the Mega Evolution Delta Reign set came out (Nov 6, 2026). They are a snapshot and will be out of date by the time you read this, so plug in your own numbers.

What the market looked like on Nov 9, 2026:

  • One sales tracker listed 26 recent eBay sales of a single, sealed, non-presale English ETB. They ranged from $77.00 to $101.74, with a 25th percentile of $85.00 and a median of about $90.
  • To get those 26, we removed presale listings, two-box lots and one sale described as "may have damage." Those would have skewed the range.
  • Prices were already falling. On TCGplayer the box sold for about $106 on Oct 31 and about $90 on Nov 9.
  • Retail prices varied a lot. One big-box store's page briefly showed $49.99 "In Store Only," then showed Marketplace prices of $99.95 to $139.99 later the same day. Another major retailer listed it at $59.99, out of stock.

Assumptions (estimates): 8% sales tax, a fee of 13.6% + $0.40 charged on the sale price plus 8% buyer tax, a $9.00 label and $1.50 for packing.

At a $49.99 buy:

  • Landed cost: $49.99 × 1.08 = $53.99
  • If it sells at $85.00: fee $12.88, so net = 85.00 − 12.88 − 9.00 − 1.50 − 53.99 = $7.63
  • If it sells at $89.99: fee $13.62, so net = $11.88
  • If it sells at $77.00 (the lowest recent sale): net = about $0.80
  • Break-even sale price: about $76.06

At a $59.99 buy:

  • Landed cost: $64.79
  • At $85.00: net = −$3.17 (a loss)
  • At $89.99: net = $1.08

At a $99.95 buy (a Marketplace price): you lose about $46 at an $85 sale.

What this shows: at MSRP the profit is real but small, roughly $8–$12 per box at these prices. A $10 difference in the buy price turns that into a loss. A box that sits unsold while prices fall can drift down to break-even. And one return or "not as described" claim can cancel the profit from several boxes.

Formula: your highest safe buy price

Work backward from the sale price you expect:

Max landed cost = sale price − eBay fee − label − packing − the minimum profit you want

Then divide by (1 + tax rate) to get the highest shelf price you should pay.

Example: you expect an $85.00 sale and want at least $5.00 profit. 85.00 − 12.88 − 9.00 − 1.50 − 5.00 = $56.62 landed. Divided by 1.08, that's $52.43 before tax. A $49.99 box passes. A $59.99 box doesn't.

How to get the sale price right

Your sale price is the number most likely to be wrong. To get it right:

  • Use eBay's "Sold items" filter, not active listings. Asking prices are often $5–$15 above what boxes actually sell for.
  • Use only the last 3 days. New-release prices can change quickly.
  • Remove listings that aren't the same item: presales, multi-box lots, Pokémon Center exclusive versions, opened or damaged boxes, and non-English versions.
  • Add any shipping the buyer paid to get the delivered total.
  • Use the 25th percentile, not the median. Sort the sales you kept from low to high and take the price one quarter of the way up. Plan on selling there, and count anything higher as a bonus.
  • Check the date. If the most recent sales are lower than older ones, assume the next sale will be lower too.

Same-day go/no-go checklist

Do this on the day you buy, before you pay:

  • [ ] The shelf price (or an employee's answer) is confirmed, and it's at or below your highest safe buy price.
  • [ ] It's the exact product: sealed English regular ETB, not a bundle, a different set or the Pokémon Center version.
  • [ ] The 25th percentile of the last 3 days of matching sold listings is at or above the sale price you used.
  • [ ] Your worksheet shows a net profit you'd still accept if the box sold at the lowest recent price.
  • [ ] You know the store's purchase limit and will stay within it.
  • [ ] You have a box and padding ready, and you've priced the label from a real weight and size.
  • [ ] You've set a stop date: if it hasn't sold by then, you'll lower the price or keep the box.

If any box is unchecked, don't buy.

Seal and condition checks before you leave the store

Buyers of sealed boxes are picky, and condition problems lead to returns.

  • Wrap: check that it's tight with clean, even seams. Loose, bunched or doubled-over film at the corners is a warning sign.
  • Corners and edges: look for dents, crushing or scuffs. Choose the cleanest box on the shelf.
  • Tears or holes in the wrap: don't buy it.
  • Receipt: keep it. It helps if a buyer questions whether the box is genuine.
  • Photos: before you list, photograph all six sides and close-ups of the seams in good light. Use only your own photos.

Listing tips that protect a thin margin

  • Title: put the set name, "Elite Trainer Box," "Sealed" and "English" up front. Make clear whether it's the regular or Pokémon Center version.
  • Condition notes: describe any marks on the wrap or box honestly. A buyer who wasn't warned about a dented corner can open a return.
  • Pricing: free shipping at a delivered price close to your 25th-percentile number usually reads more clearly to buyers than a low price with added shipping.
  • Packing: use a box, not a bubble mailer, and pad all sides. A crushed corner in transit becomes a return.
  • Brand rules: you can resell a genuine sealed product, but don't call yourself an authorized dealer and don't use official promo images.

When to skip the flip

  • You can only find it above MSRP, including from Marketplace sellers on store websites.
  • The 25th-percentile sold price is near your break-even.
  • Prices have dropped every day this week.
  • Your eBay account is new and may have selling limits or payout holds.
  • You'd have to buy several boxes to make the trip worth it.

Done carefully, a single MSRP box can make a small profit while a set is new. The point of the worksheet is to let you check that before you spend the money.
