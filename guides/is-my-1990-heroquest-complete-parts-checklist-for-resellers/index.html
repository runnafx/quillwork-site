Is My 1990 HeroQuest Complete? Parts Checklist for Resellers

A component-by-component checklist for the original Milton Bradley HeroQuest (1989 UK/EU, 1990 US). Use it to check a thrift, auction or attic copy before you bid, buy or list it.

Original HeroQuest copies turn up in estate auctions, attics and thrift stores, often in a beaten-up box with "complete?" written on the listing. The box tells you very little. Collectors care about the contents: how many figures, doors and cards are there, and whether the pieces were ever played with.

This page gives you a full count sheet for the original game, a short guide to checking condition, and a fill-in line you can paste into your listing. Print it out, or keep it open while you sort.

A note on accuracy: published inventories for the original game don't fully agree. Card totals in particular differ from source to source, and the 1990 US/Canada release differs slightly from the 1989 UK/European one. The counts below follow a commonly cited inventory of the original game. Always check them against the contents list in the copy's own instruction booklet if it's there. If your copy doesn't match, write down what you actually have, not what the list says.

Step 1: Identify which version you have

Before you count anything, work out what's in front of you:

  • Milton Bradley 1989/1990 original. The Milton Bradley (MB) logo is on the box. Games Workshop is credited for the miniatures. The miniatures are grey plastic, and some sets are partly unassembled on sprues.
  • The 2021 Hasbro/Avalon Hill reprint. It has a different box, different art and sculpts, and figures in several colours. It sells in a different market. Don't use this checklist for it.
  • An expansion box. Kellar's Keep and Return of the Witch Lord are separate products. They have their own quest books and figures, so keep them separate when you count.

Write down the copyright year and the language of the rulebook. European copies in other languages are common and attract a different set of buyers.

What to check on each figure:

  • Thin parts: weapons, staffs and other thin bits. These break or bend most easily, so check them closely.
  • Paint: painted figures should be described as painted. Some buyers want unpainted figures, others don't mind. Never call a painted figure "original condition".
  • Mixed-in pieces: look for figures from the expansions or from other games. Remove them from your count, or list them as extras.

  • [ ] Instruction booklet
  • [ ] Quest book. Check for pencil marks, ticked-off quests and torn pages.
  • [ ] Game Master screen (information screen)
  • [ ] Character sheet pad. Note roughly how many sheets are left and whether any are filled in.
  • [ ] Combat dice: 6
  • [ ] Movement dice: 2
  • [ ] Cardboard tiles: stairs, blocked squares, traps, secret doors and similar. Note whether the sheets are unpunched (tiles still in the frame) or punched.

Unpunched tile sheets and an unused character pad are the clearest signs that a copy was never played. Buyers search for those details.

Step 7: Check the "unplayed / new on sprue" claim

Many auction descriptions say a copy is "never played" or "new on sprue". Here's how to check that before you repeat it in your own listing:

  1. Sprues: are the figures still attached to their plastic frames, or are they loose with small nubs where they were cut off? If they're loose, they've been removed. That doesn't prove the game was played, but you can't call them "on sprue".
  2. Tiles: have any been punched out? One punched tile means you can't describe the copy as unpunched.
  3. Pad and quest book: look for any writing.
  4. Cards: are they still banded, or loose and shuffled?
  5. Inner trays: is the insert intact, or torn or missing?

If you're buying online and can't handle the game, ask the seller or auctioneer for photos of the sprues, the tile sheets and the character pad before the lot closes. If the answer is vague, base your maximum bid on a played copy.

Step 8: Grade the box separately from the contents

A crushed or water-damaged box doesn't make the contents incomplete, and a nice box doesn't make them complete. Describe the two separately:

  • Box: corners (crushed or split), lid tears, tape repairs, fading, odour (smoke, damp).
  • Contents: your counts from Steps 2 to 6, plus the condition notes.

Buyers who are rebuying the game they had as kids often care most about the figures and the board. Display collectors care much more about the box. Splitting the two in your listing lets each kind of buyer find what they're looking for.

Title words buyers actually type

Only use the words that are true for your copy:

  • "HeroQuest 1989" or "HeroQuest 1990 Milton Bradley"
  • "complete": only if every count above matches
  • "unpunched" or "on sprue": only if you checked them yourself
  • "unpainted"
  • "incomplete" or "for parts": say so plainly, then list what's missing

An honest "incomplete" listing with a clear missing-parts list sells to people filling gaps in their own sets. A "complete" listing that turns out to be short a goblin leads to a return.

Quick pre-bid checklist

  • [ ] Version confirmed (MB original, not the 2021 reprint)
  • [ ] Photos show the figures, furniture and doors spread out, not just the box
  • [ ] Sprue, tile and pad status confirmed, or assumed "played" in your price
  • [ ] Buyer's premium, card fees, tax and shipping added before you set your maximum bid
  • [ ] Maximum bid based on the condition you can prove, not the condition the description claims

Count first, describe second, and price on what's actually in the box.
