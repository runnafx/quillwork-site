Wedding Reception MC Script: Every Announcement, In Order

A fill-in script for every announcement a wedding MC or DJ makes, from grand entrance to send-off, in the order they usually happen. Copy it, add the names, and read it from the mic.

If someone asked you to MC a wedding, or you're a new DJ working your first receptions, you need two things. You need to know what to say and when to say it. This page gives you both. Below is a standard reception order, then a short fill-in script for each announcement.

Swap anything in [BRACKETS] for the real details. Then check the order against the couple's timeline, because every wedding is a little different.

Before You Touch the Mic: 6-Point Checklist

  1. Get the final timeline from the couple or planner and write the times on your script.
  2. Confirm name pronunciations. Write them out phonetically, e.g. "Siobhan (shi-VAWN)". Ask about the wedding party, both sets of parents and every toast-giver.
  3. Ask what to skip. Many couples skip the bouquet toss, garter toss or parent dances. Some have family reasons for skipping a dance, so don't assume.
  4. Know the venue rules: last call time, sparkler or confetti rules, and when the music has to stop.
  5. Find your cues. Ask who tells you the kitchen is ready, who brings out the cake knife, and where the couple will be waiting before the entrance.
  6. Do a mic check at the volume you'll actually use, from the spot you'll actually stand.

The Typical Reception Order

Your order may differ. This is a common starting point:

  1. Guests seated / welcome and housekeeping
  2. Grand entrance
  3. First dance (some couples do it right after the entrance, others after dinner)
  4. Blessing or grace (optional)
  5. Dinner release
  6. Toasts
  7. Parent dances
  8. Cake cutting
  9. Open dance floor
  10. Anniversary dance (optional)
  11. Bouquet / garter toss (optional)
  12. Last call
  13. Last dance
  14. Send-off

The Scripts

Keep each announcement short. Guests want to hear the couple's names, know what's happening, and know what to do next. Under 45 seconds is a good target for most of them.

1. Welcome and Housekeeping

"Good evening, everyone! On behalf of [COUPLE NAMES], welcome, and thank you for being here. My name is [YOUR NAME], and I'll be your MC tonight. A few quick notes: the bar is open [now / after the entrance], restrooms are [LOCATION], and the couple asks that you [keep phones down during dinner / share photos with #HASHTAG]. In about [X] minutes we'll introduce the wedding party, so please find your seats."

Tip: Do this 5-10 minutes before the entrance so people have time to sit down.

2. Grand Entrance

"Ladies and gentlemen, please rise if you're able, and help me welcome the wedding party!"

Then introduce each pair or person:

"Please welcome [PARENT NAMES]... [ATTENDANT 1] and [ATTENDANT 2]... the maid of honor, [NAME], and the best man, [NAME]..."

Then the couple:

"And now, for the first time as a married couple, please put your hands together for [HOW THE COUPLE WANTS TO BE INTRODUCED]!"

Ask the couple exactly how they want to be introduced, for example "Mr. and Mrs. ___", "Jordan and Sam ___", or first names only. Write it down word for word. This is the line people remember.

3. First Dance

"Everyone, please direct your attention to the dance floor. [COUPLE NAMES] will now share their first dance as a married couple, to [SONG] by [ARTIST]."

If the couple wants others to join in partway through: "[COUPLE] would love for all the couples to join them on the dance floor."

4. Blessing (Optional)

"Before we eat, [NAME, relationship to the couple] will offer a blessing. [NAME]?"

Then hand over the mic and step back.

5. Dinner Release

"Dinner is served! Our [catering team / venue staff] will release tables in order. We'll start with table [NUMBER], and I'll call each table up from there. Please wait for your table to be called so the line moves quickly."

For plated service: "Dinner is about to be served. Please take your seats and enjoy."

Tip: Rotate which side of the room goes first. Ask the couple whether a particular table, such as grandparents, should go first.

6. Toasts

"Could I have everyone's attention, please? It's time for the toasts. Please make sure you have a glass in hand. To start us off, please welcome [NAME], the [RELATIONSHIP] of [PARTNER]."

After each toast: "Thank you, [NAME]. Next up..."

After the last one: "Please raise your glasses one more time: to [COUPLE NAMES]!"

Toasts are the part most likely to run long or go off track. Agree on the speaking order and a rough time limit with each speaker beforehand. Stay near them so you can take the mic back gracefully.

7. Parent Dances

"It's now time for a very special dance. [PARTNER] and [his/her/their] [father / mother / NAME] will share a dance to [SONG]."

If both dances run back to back: "...and halfway through, [OTHER PARTNER] and [PARENT] will join them on the floor."

Always confirm the exact pairings in advance. Never improvise who dances with whom.

8. Cake Cutting

"Guests, if you'd like to grab your cameras, [COUPLE NAMES] are about to cut their cake. Please gather around the cake table."

Wait for the photographer to say they're ready before you announce it.

9. Opening the Dance Floor

"That's all the formalities! The dance floor is officially open. [COUPLE] wants to see everyone out here, so let's go!"

Play a song that's sure to fill the floor right away. Don't leave a gap of silence after this line.

10. Anniversary Dance (Optional)

"All married couples, please join [COUPLE] on the dance floor. As the song plays, I'll ask couples to leave the floor when I call out how long they've been married."

Then call it out as the song goes: "If you've been married less than 5 years, please step off... 10... 20... 30..." Keep going until one couple is left.

"Our longest-married couple tonight is [NAMES], married [X] years! [Optional: Do you have any advice for the newlyweds?]"

Tip: Only ask for advice if you're comfortable holding the mic for them and taking it back.

11. Bouquet / Garter Toss (Optional)

"All the single [ladies / guests] who want to catch the bouquet, please come to the dance floor!"

Let the couple decide who's invited. Many now use "anyone who wants to join." Don't call anyone out by name.

12. Last Call

"Just a heads-up, everyone: the bar will close in [15] minutes. This is last call. Please drink responsibly, and if you need a ride, [SHUTTLE INFO / please plan ahead]."

13. Last Dance

"We've come to the last song of the night. [COUPLE] invites everyone to the dance floor for one final dance."

Some couples prefer a private last dance: "...[COUPLE] will share this last dance on their own, so please make a circle around them."

14. Send-Off

"Thank you all for celebrating with [COUPLE NAMES] tonight! Please head [outside / to the front entrance], where [STAFF / wedding party] will hand out [sparklers / bubbles / petals]. Line up on both sides and let's send them off in style!"

Check the sparkler rules with the venue before you announce them.

Delivery Tips That Make a Big Difference

  • Get attention before you talk. Lower the music, wait a beat, then speak. Don't talk over a loud song.
  • Hold the mic about a fist's width from your mouth, pointed at your chin. Speak a bit slower than feels natural.
  • Say the couple's names often. It's their day, not your stand-up set.
  • Skip inside jokes unless the couple approved them.
  • Have a recovery line ready for delays: "We're just a few minutes away. Grab a drink and we'll be right back with you."
  • Print your script in large type, with times and name pronunciations in bold. Don't count on your phone in a dark room.
