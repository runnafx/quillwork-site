English Menu Descriptions for Taqueria Dishes: Templates + Examples

A step-by-step guide for taqueria and pupuseria owners who want English-speaking and delivery-app customers to understand their menu. Includes a simple formula, fill-in templates and ready-to-adapt examples for common dishes.

How to Write English Descriptions for Your Taqueria or Pupuseria Menu

Many customers walk past dishes they would love simply because they don't know what "huarache," "tlayuda" or "pupusa revuelta" means. On delivery apps it's worse: there's nobody at the counter to ask. A short English line under each Spanish name fixes this without changing your food or your identity.

This guide shows you how to write those lines yourself.

Rule 1: Keep the Spanish Name

Don't replace "birria" with "Mexican beef stew" or "pupusa" with "stuffed tortilla." The Spanish name is what people search for, what regulars order by, and part of what makes your place authentic. Put the English description underneath or after the name, not instead of it.

Good: Birria de Res – Slow-braised beef in a rich red chile broth, served with consomé for dipping.

Not as good: Mexican Beef Stew

Rule 2: Use the Four-Part Formula

Almost every dish description can be built from these parts, in this order:

  1. What it is (the base: tortilla, masa cake, soup, plate)
  2. What's in it or on it (main filling or protein)
  3. How it's cooked or what makes it special (grilled, slow-braised, handmade)
  4. How it's served (with salsa, rice and beans, curtido, consomé)

You don't need all four every time. Two or three is usually enough. Aim for one sentence, roughly 10 to 25 words. Delivery apps limit how much text shows before a customer has to tap "more," so check your app's settings and put the most important words first.

Fill-in Templates

Copy these and fill in the blanks for your dishes.

For tacos, by protein: [Protein, cooked how] on [corn/flour] tortillas, topped with [toppings]. Served with [salsa/lime/radish].

For filled masa dishes (pupusas, gorditas, tamales): Handmade [corn/rice] masa [cake/pocket] filled with [filling]. Served with [side].

For big plates (platillos): [Main item, cooked how] served with [rice, beans, tortillas, salad].

For soups and stews: [Broth type] soup with [main ingredients]. Served with [tortillas, lime, onion, cilantro].

For drinks: [Flavor/base] drink, [sweet/fresh/creamy], [made in-house, if true].

For something unfamiliar: [Region] specialty: [what it is in plain words], [main toppings or fillings].

Worked Examples You Can Adapt

These describe common versions of each dish. Recipes vary by family and region, so change any detail that doesn't match how you make it. Never list an ingredient you don't use.

Tacos and Proteins

  • Al Pastor – Pork marinated in chiles and spices, cooked on a vertical spit, served with pineapple, onion and cilantro.
  • Carnitas – Pork slow-cooked until tender, then crisped, topped with onion and cilantro.
  • Asada – Grilled marinated steak, chopped and topped with onion and cilantro.
  • Lengua – Beef tongue, slow-cooked until very tender and chopped.
  • Cabeza – Tender slow-cooked beef from the head, shredded.
  • Tripa – Beef tripe (intestine), cooked and grilled until crispy.
  • Buche – Pork stomach, slow-cooked and chopped.
  • Barbacoa – Slow-cooked, shredded beef, rich and tender.
  • Chorizo – Spiced Mexican pork sausage, crumbled and griddled.

Being direct about lengua, tripa and buche helps. Adventurous eaters look for them, and nobody is surprised by their order.

Masa Dishes

  • Sope – Thick handmade corn masa base with a raised edge, topped with beans, your choice of meat, lettuce, cream and cheese.
  • Huarache – Long, oval corn masa base (named for its sandal shape) topped with beans, meat, salsa, cream and cheese.
  • Gordita – Thick corn masa pocket, griddled and stuffed with your choice of filling.
  • Tlayuda – Oaxacan specialty: a large, thin, toasted corn tortilla spread with beans and topped with Oaxaca cheese, meat and vegetables.
  • Tamal – Corn masa steamed in a [corn husk/banana leaf] with a filling of [pork in red sauce, chicken in green sauce, cheese and jalapeño].
  • Quesadilla – Tortilla filled with melted cheese and your choice of filling, folded and griddled.

Salvadoran Dishes

  • Pupusa Revuelta – Handmade corn masa cake stuffed with ground pork chicharrón, beans and cheese, griddled. Served with curtido and tomato salsa.
  • Pupusa de Queso con Loroco – Masa cake filled with cheese and loroco, a mild, slightly floral edible flower bud.
  • Pupusa de Frijol con Queso – Masa cake filled with refried beans and cheese.
  • Curtido – Lightly fermented cabbage slaw with carrot, onion and oregano, served with pupusas.
  • Yuca Frita con Chicharrón – Fried cassava root topped with crispy pork pieces and curtido.
  • Plátanos Fritos – Fried sweet plantains, served with [beans and cream].

If you offer rice-flour pupusas (de arroz), say so. Customers who avoid wheat may look for them. But don't call anything "gluten-free" unless you've confirmed it, including cross-contact on your grill.

Soups, Stews and Plates

  • Birria – [Beef/goat] slow-braised in a red chile broth, served with consomé for dipping, onion, cilantro and lime.
  • Pozole Rojo – Hearty red chile soup with hominy and pork, served with cabbage, radish, oregano and lime.
  • Menudo – Traditional red chile soup with beef tripe and hominy, served with onion, oregano and lime.
  • Sopa de Res – Beef soup with chunks of vegetables like corn, carrot, potato and cabbage.
  • Chile Relleno – Roasted poblano pepper stuffed with cheese, battered and fried, topped with tomato sauce.
  • Enchiladas Verdes – Corn tortillas rolled around [chicken/cheese] and covered in green tomatillo salsa, cream and cheese.

Drinks and Desserts

  • Horchata – Sweet, creamy rice drink with cinnamon. (The Salvadoran version uses morro seed and other ingredients, so describe yours.)
  • Agua de Jamaica – Chilled hibiscus flower drink, tart and lightly sweet.
  • Agua de Tamarindo – Sweet-tart drink made from tamarind pods.
  • Tres Leches – Sponge cake soaked in three kinds of milk.
  • Elote – Corn on the cob with mayo, cheese, chile powder and lime.

Words That Help (and Words to Avoid)

Helpful, plain words: slow-cooked, grilled, handmade, crispy, tender, mild, spicy, topped with, served with, filled with, house-made (only if true).

Avoid:

  • Vague hype like "delicious," "amazing" or "the best." Customers skip over these.
  • Over-translating salsa, masa, tortilla, chorizo or quesadilla. Most English speakers already know them.
  • Health or allergen claims ("healthy," "dairy-free," "safe for allergies") unless you have checked every ingredient and preparation step. Prefer "contains cheese" or "ask us about ingredients."
  • Spice guesses. If it's hot, say "spicy." If customers can choose, say "mild or spicy salsa."

Section Headers

You can keep Spanish section names with a short English helper:

  • Tacos
  • Pupusas
  • Antojitos (Snacks & Small Plates)
  • Platillos (Plates, served with rice and beans)
  • Sopas (Soups)
  • Bebidas (Drinks)
  • Postres (Desserts)

Before-You-Publish Checklist

  • [ ] Every dish keeps its original Spanish name
  • [ ] Each unfamiliar dish has a one-sentence English description
  • [ ] Main protein and base (corn, flour, masa, rice) are named
  • [ ] Organ meats are described honestly
  • [ ] Sides are listed ("served with rice and beans")
  • [ ] Prices match your current prices exactly
  • [ ] No allergen, "gluten-free" or health claims you haven't verified
  • [ ] Every ingredient listed is actually in your version of the dish
  • [ ] The first few words carry the key information, for apps that cut text short
  • [ ] A staff member or family member has read it for accuracy
  • [ ] The same text is used on your menu board, website, Google profile and delivery apps

Where to Put It

Once it's written, paste the same descriptions everywhere customers look: your delivery app listings, your Google Business Profile menu, your website or Facebook page, and your printed or posted menu. Consistency means customers see the same dish explained the same way wherever they find you.

Start with your top 10 sellers and your most unfamiliar dishes. That alone makes a noticeable difference to someone deciding what to order for the first time.
