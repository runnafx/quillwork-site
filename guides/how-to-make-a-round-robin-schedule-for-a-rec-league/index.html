How to Make a Round Robin Schedule for a Rec League

A step-by-step method for building a fair round-robin season schedule by hand, with a worked 6-team example, home/away balancing, odd-team byes and a pre-release checklist.

How to Make a Round Robin Schedule for a Rec League (Step by Step)

If you're the volunteer stuck building this season's schedule, you need three things. Every team plays every other team. Nobody gets stuck with all the early games. Nothing lands on a field you don't have. This guide walks through a reliable pencil-and-paper method called the circle method. It works for any number of teams, and it includes a complete worked example you can copy.

Step 1: Do the math before you touch a calendar

Count your teams and your available game dates first. These numbers tell you whether a full round robin even fits.

  • Even number of teams (N): you need N − 1 rounds. Each round has N ÷ 2 games.
  • Odd number of teams: add a placeholder team called BYE. That makes N even, so you get N rounds. Each team sits out exactly once.
  • Total games in a single round robin: N × (N − 1) ÷ 2.

Examples:

  • 6 teams: 5 rounds, 3 games per round, 15 games total.
  • 7 teams: add BYE to make 8 slots. That gives 7 rounds with 3 real games per round and 21 games total.
  • 10 teams: 9 rounds, 5 games per round, 45 games total.

Now compare the rounds to your season. Say you have 10 Saturdays and 6 teams. Five rounds is one full round robin, and 10 weeks is exactly two, so a double round robin fits. You play everyone twice and flip home/away the second time. If your weeks don't divide evenly, play one full cycle and repeat as many rounds as you can. Teams will then meet some opponents more than others, so tell parents that up front.

Also check that you have enough field slots per week. Six teams need 3 games every week, so you need at least 3 slots on every game date.

Step 2: Generate the pairings with the circle method

  1. Number your teams 1 to N, adding BYE as the last number if N is odd.
  2. Write them in a list: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.
  3. Round 1: pair the first team with the last, the second with the second-to-last, and so on.
  4. For the next round, keep team 1 fixed. Move the last team into position 2 and shift everyone else one place right.
  5. Pair them up again the same way. Repeat until you have N − 1 rounds.

Worked example: 6 teams

  • Round 1 (list 1,2,3,4,5,6): 1v6, 2v5, 3v4
  • Round 2 (list 1,6,2,3,4,5): 1v5, 6v4, 2v3
  • Round 3 (list 1,5,6,2,3,4): 1v4, 5v3, 6v2
  • Round 4 (list 1,4,5,6,2,3): 1v3, 4v2, 5v6
  • Round 5 (list 1,3,4,5,6,2): 1v2, 3v6, 4v5

Check it by reading one team's line. Team 4 plays 3, 6, 1, 2 and 5, which is every other team exactly once. Do this check for every team. It takes two minutes and catches copying mistakes.

Odd teams: run the same method with BYE as a team. Whoever is paired with BYE has the week off. Because BYE appears once per round, every real team gets exactly one bye.

Step 3: Balance home and away

For a single round robin with an odd number of games per team, a perfect split is impossible. Aim for every team to be within one game of even. With 6 teams, each plays 5 games, so you want everyone at 3 home and 2 away, or 2 home and 3 away.

The simplest approach is to assign home teams round by round while keeping a running tally. Give home to whichever team in each pair has fewer home games so far. Here is a balanced version of the example, with the home team listed first:

  • Round 1: 1v6, 5v2, 3v4
  • Round 2: 5v1, 6v4, 2v3
  • Round 3: 1v4, 3v5, 2v6
  • Round 4: 3v1, 4v2, 6v5
  • Round 5: 1v2, 6v3, 4v5

Final home counts are Team 1: 3, Team 2: 2, Team 3: 3, Team 4: 2, Team 5: 2, Team 6: 3.

Watch for long streaks too. Avoid any team having four or more home or away games in a row. To fix a streak, flip a game in the middle of the run. Then flip a different game for the opponent so their count stays balanced.

Double round robin: repeat all rounds and swap every home/away assignment. Each team then ends up exactly even.

Step 4: Assign time slots and fields fairly

This is where most complaints come from. Nobody wants every 8 a.m. game.

  1. List your slots per game date, for example Field A 9:00, Field A 10:30 and Field B 9:00.
  2. Rank them from least to most desirable. In most leagues the earliest and latest slots are least popular.
  3. In each round, rotate which game gets the least desirable slot. A simple rule is to shift the order of games down one position each week.
  4. Keep a tally table with one row per team and columns for early, middle and late. Update it after each round.
  5. If a team falls more than one early game ahead of the others, swap two games within the same date. That changes the times without breaking the pairings.

If one field is worse than the others, track it the same way. Count games per team per field and spread them out.

Step 5: Apply your constraints

Now lay the rounds onto real dates and handle the exceptions.

  • Blackout dates (holidays, school events, field closures): skip that date and push the remaining rounds back one week. Don't drop a round, or the round robin breaks.
  • Coach conflicts (one coach with two teams, or a coach only available early): place those teams' games in compatible slots first, then fill in the rest.
  • Shared field limits: if a date has fewer slots than games, move whole games to a makeup date instead of splitting a round across weeks without noting it.
  • Rainouts: leave one or two open makeup dates at the end of the season if you can.

Whenever you move a game, re-check the tally tables from Steps 3 and 4.

Step 6: Run the pre-release checklist

Before you send anything to parents, confirm each item:

  • [ ] Every team plays every other team the intended number of times.
  • [ ] No team is scheduled twice at the same time.
  • [ ] No game is on a blackout date or on a field you don't have that day.
  • [ ] Every team is within one game of even on home/away.
  • [ ] Early and late slots are spread within one game per team.
  • [ ] Byes (if any) are spread across the season, not bunched at the start or end.
  • [ ] Coach conflicts are resolved.
  • [ ] Team names are spelled exactly as on registration.
  • [ ] Each team has its own one-page schedule it can print.
  • [ ] Your release email includes the date, time, field, home/away or jersey color, the rainout policy and who to contact.

Fill-in template: master schedule columns

A spreadsheet with these columns imports cleanly into most league apps and is easy to filter by team:

Date | Start Time | Field | Home Team | Away Team | Division | Round | Notes

Keep one row per game. Filter by team to create each team's printable schedule. Sort by date and field to spot double-booked slots.
