Kitchen Spanish for Managers: Phrases by Station (With Phonetics)

A free cheat sheet of short, practical Spanish phrases for English-speaking kitchen managers and sous chefs. It's sorted by station: prep, line, dish, receiving, closing and scheduling. Each phrase has a pronunciation guide written for English speakers.

If you run a kitchen where most of the crew speaks Spanish, you don't need to be fluent to give clear instructions. You need about two dozen short phrases you can say with confidence, the right numbers, and a few polite words that show respect.

This page sorts those phrases by where you'll use them. Each one has the Spanish, the English, and a pronunciation guide. CAPITAL letters show the stressed syllable.

Before You Start: Four Ground Rules

  1. Short beats perfect. "Dos más, por favor" (two more, please) works better than a long sentence you're unsure of.
  2. Keep the English kitchen words your crew already uses. Most US kitchens already say "86," "fire," "all day," "walk-in" and "expo" in English, whatever language people speak. Don't swap words that already work.
  3. Tú or usted? Most kitchen crews use the casual *tú* form with each other, and the phrases below use it. With an older employee, or someone you've just met, the formal *usted* is more respectful. If you aren't sure, ask: "¿Te puedo hablar de tú?" (teh PWEH-doh ah-BLAR deh TOO), which means "Can I speak to you informally?"
  4. Check that you were understood. Ask "¿Me lo repites, por favor?" (meh loh reh-PEE-tehs, por fah-VOR), meaning "Can you repeat that back to me, please?" It works better than asking "Do you understand?", because people often answer "yes" to that just to be polite.

Spanish varies by country and region. Your cooks may use different words for some items. When they correct you, use their word.

Prep Station

  • Corta la cebolla en cuadritos. Cut the onion into small dice. (KOR-tah lah seh-BOH-yah en kwah-DREE-tohs)
  • Rebanadas delgadas / gruesas. Thin / thick slices. (reh-bah-NAH-dahs del-GAH-dahs / GRWEH-sahs)
  • ¿Cuántos tenemos? How many do we have? (KWAHN-tohs teh-NEH-mohs)
  • Necesitamos más. We need more. (neh-seh-see-TAH-mohs MAHS)
  • Es suficiente. That's enough. (ehs soo-fee-SYEN-teh)
  • Ponle etiqueta y fecha. Label and date it. (POHN-leh eh-tee-KEH-tah ee FEH-chah)
  • Usa lo viejo primero. Use the old stock first. (OO-sah loh VYEH-hoh pree-MEH-roh)
  • Pruébalo. Taste it. (PRWEH-bah-loh)
  • La lista de preparación está en la pared. The prep list is on the wall. (lah LEES-tah deh preh-pah-rah-SYOHN ehs-TAH en lah pah-RED)

Line and Expo

  • ¿Cuánto falta? How much longer? (KWAHN-toh FAHL-tah)
  • Dos minutos. Two minutes. (dohs mee-NOO-tohs)
  • Empieza la mesa doce. Start table twelve. (em-PYEH-sah lah MEH-sah DOH-seh). Many crews simply say "fire twelve."
  • Se acabó el pollo. We're out of chicken. (seh ah-kah-BOH el POH-yoh)
  • Sin cebolla. No onion. (seen seh-BOH-yah)
  • Vuelve a hacer este plato. Remake this plate. (VWEL-veh ah ah-SEHR EHS-teh PLAH-toh)
  • Limpia la orilla del plato. Wipe the rim of the plate. (LEEM-pyah lah oh-REE-yah del PLAH-toh)
  • Más rápido, por favor. Faster, please. (mahs RAH-pee-doh, por fah-VOR)
  • Esta orden tiene alergia. This order has an allergy. (EHS-tah OR-den TYEH-neh ah-LEHR-hyah)

Safety calls to use while moving around the kitchen:

  • ¡Atrás! Behind! (ah-TRAHS)
  • ¡Caliente! Hot! (kah-LYEN-teh)
  • ¡Cuchillo! Knife! (koo-CHEE-yoh)
  • ¡Esquina! Corner! (ehs-KEE-nah)

About allergies: A phrase card does not replace your allergen procedure. For allergies, food safety training or anything serious, use written procedures your crew has confirmed they understand, and use a qualified bilingual person where you can.

Dish

  • Necesitamos platos limpios. We need clean plates. (neh-seh-see-TAH-mohs PLAH-tohs LEEM-pyohs)
  • Cambia el agua del fregadero. Change the sink water. (KAHM-byah el AH-gwah del freh-gah-DEH-roh)
  • Barre y trapea. Sweep and mop. (BAH-rreh ee trah-PEH-ah)
  • Saca la basura. Take out the trash. (SAH-kah lah bah-SOO-rah)
  • Guárdalo aquí. Put it away here. (GWAR-dah-loh ah-KEE)
  • Primero esto. This first. (pree-MEH-roh EHS-toh)

Receiving and Inventory

  • Revisa el pedido antes de firmar. Check the order before signing. (reh-VEE-sah el peh-DEE-doh AHN-tehs deh feer-MAR)
  • Falta algo. Something is missing. (FAHL-tah AHL-goh)
  • Cuéntalo, por favor. Count it, please. (KWEN-tah-loh, por fah-VOR)
  • Ponlo en el congelador. Put it in the freezer. (POHN-loh en el kohn-heh-lah-DOR)
  • Ponlo en el walk-in. Put it in the walk-in. Most crews say "walk-in." You may also hear *la cámara fría* (lah KAH-mah-rah FREE-ah).
  • Esto está mal. This is bad/wrong. (EHS-toh ehs-TAH mahl). Use it for damaged or wrong product.

Opening and Closing

  • Prende el horno. Turn on the oven. (PREN-deh el OR-noh)
  • Apaga la freidora. Turn off the fryer. (ah-PAH-gah lah fray-DOH-rah)
  • Tápalo. Cover it. (TAH-pah-loh)
  • ¿Quién cierra hoy? Who's closing today? (kyen SYEH-rrah oy)
  • Limpia la plancha. Clean the flat-top. (LEEM-pyah lah PLAHN-chah)
  • ¿Ya terminaste? Are you done? (yah tehr-mee-NAHS-teh)

Scheduling and Shift Talk

  • ¿Puedes trabajar el sábado? Can you work Saturday? (PWEH-dehs trah-bah-HAR el SAH-bah-doh)
  • ¿A qué hora entras? What time do you come in? (ah keh OH-rah EN-trahs)
  • Toma tu descanso. Take your break. (TOH-mah too dehs-KAHN-soh)
  • El horario está en la pared. The schedule is on the wall. (el oh-RAH-ryoh ehs-TAH en lah pah-RED)
  • Nos vemos mañana. See you tomorrow. (nohs VEH-mohs mah-NYAH-nah)

Days of the week: lunes (LOO-nehs) Monday, martes (MAR-tehs) Tuesday, miércoles (MYEHR-koh-lehs) Wednesday, jueves (HWEH-vehs) Thursday, viernes (VYEHR-nehs) Friday, sábado (SAH-bah-doh) Saturday, domingo (doh-MEEN-goh) Sunday.

Don't use a phrase sheet for pay disputes, discipline, injuries or anything else with HR or legal consequences. Get a qualified interpreter or put it in writing in both languages.

Numbers, Quantities and Time

Kitchens run on numbers, so learn these first.

  • 1–10: uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez (OO-noh, dohs, trehs, KWAH-troh, SEEN-koh, says, SYEH-teh, OH-choh, NWEH-veh, dyehs)
  • 11, 12, 15, 20: once, doce, quince, veinte (OHN-seh, DOH-seh, KEEN-seh, VAYN-teh)
  • media (MEH-dyah) half; un cuarto (oon KWAR-toh) a quarter; una docena (OO-nah doh-SEH-nah) a dozen
  • libra (LEE-brah) pound; onza (OHN-sah) ounce; taza (TAH-sah) cup; cucharada (koo-chah-RAH-dah) tablespoon
  • a las cuatro (ah lahs KWAH-troh) at four o'clock; en diez minutos (en dyehs mee-NOO-tohs) in ten minutes

Worked example: to say "We need three pounds of diced onion by four," say "Necesitamos tres libras de cebolla en cuadritos para las cuatro." (neh-seh-see-TAH-mohs trehs LEE-brahs deh seh-BOH-yah en kwah-DREE-tohs PAH-rah lahs KWAH-troh)

It breaks down like this: *necesitamos* (we need) + number + unit + *de* + item + how it's cut + *para las* (by) + time. You can reuse that pattern for almost any prep request.

Polite Phrases That Go a Long Way

  • Por favor / Gracias. Please / Thank you. (por fah-VOR / GRAH-syahs)
  • Buen trabajo. Good job. (bwen trah-BAH-hoh)
  • ¿Me ayudas? Can you help me? (meh ah-YOO-dahs)
  • Muéstrame. Show me. (MWEHS-trah-meh)
  • No entiendo. Más despacio, por favor. I don't understand. Slower, please. (noh en-TYEN-doh. mahs dehs-PAH-syoh, por fah-VOR)
  • ¿Cómo se dice…? How do you say…? (KOH-moh seh DEE-seh)
  • Perdón, mi español es malo. Sorry, my Spanish is bad. (pehr-DOHN, mee ehs-pah-NYOL ehs MAH-loh)

Asking your crew "¿Cómo se dice…?" can help more than any phrase sheet. People usually like teaching the boss a word, and you'll learn the terms your kitchen actually uses.

How to Make This Stick

  1. Pick one station a week. Learn its five most useful phrases before you add more.
  2. Post the phrases where you need them. Tape prep phrases above the prep table and closing phrases by the back door.
  3. Add your own menu. Make a blank card with your dishes, your sauces and the 86 items that come up most. Ask a cook to check the Spanish.
  4. Say it out loud before service. Pronunciation improves faster from practice than from reading.
  5. Accept corrections. If a cook says "aquí decimos…" (ah-KEE deh-SEE-mohs, "here we say…"), use their version from then on.

You don't need perfect Spanish to run a clear, respectful kitchen. A few phrases you say every day will get you further than a big vocabulary you never use.
