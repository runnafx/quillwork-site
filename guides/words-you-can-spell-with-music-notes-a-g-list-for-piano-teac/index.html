Words You Can Spell With Music Notes: A-G List for Piano Teachers

A sorted list of real words spelled only with the letters A to G, grouped by length and five-finger position. It also has winter fill-in sentences, an answer key and steps for making your own note-naming worksheet.

Note-name words are one of the easiest ways to make note reading feel like a game. The student names each note, the letters spell a word, and a wrong note usually produces nonsense, so the student can catch the mistake without you.

The hard part is coming up with the words. Most of the ones that come to mind contain an H, an I or an S. This page gives you a list you can use right away, sorted by how much the student has to know.

The lists are hand-picked, starting from a scan of a standard English word list, and a script confirmed that each word uses only the letters A to G and fits the group it is listed in. The page was written with AI assistance. Offensive words have been removed, but check any word you add yourself. The letters A to G can spell at least one slur, and a few innocent-looking combinations can get a laugh you did not plan for.

The master list, by length

3 letters

  • ACE, ADD, AGE, BAD, BAG, BED, BEE, BEG, CAB, DAB, DAD, EBB, EGG, FAD, FED, FEE, GAB

4 letters

  • AGED, BEAD, BEEF, CAFE, CAGE, DEAD, DEAF, DEED, EDGE, FACE, FADE, FEED

5 letters

  • ADAGE, ADDED, BADGE, CAGED, DECAF, EDGED, EGGED, FACED, FADED, GAFFE

6 letters

  • BAGGED, BEADED, BEEFED, BEGGED, DECADE, DEFACE, EFFACE, FACADE

7 letters

  • BAGGAGE, CABBAGE

For younger students, skip the words they will not recognize, such as ADAGE, EFFACE and GAFFE. A student who has never seen the word cannot use it to check their answer, and that check is the main reason to use words at all.

Words sorted by hand position

The master list assumes a student can read notes across the staff. Beginners usually can't yet, so use only words that fit the position they are playing in. Each list below stays inside five natural notes, and each letter has only one possible note, so the sheet tests exactly what the student has learned.

C position (C D E F G)

  • EGG, FED, FEE, DEED, EDGE, FEED, EDGED, EGGED

G position (G A B C D)

  • ADD, BAD, BAG, CAB, DAB, DAD, GAB

A minor position (A B C D E)

  • ACE, ADD, BAD, BED, CAB, DAB, DAD, EBB, BEAD, DEAD, DEED, ADDED, BEADED, DECADE

Skip F position for this activity. It has few usable words, and the B in that position is a B flat, which makes "name the note" confusing for a beginner.

If your student knows only landmark notes such as Treble G, Middle C and Bass F, those three letters don't spell anything. Add neighboring notes first, then use the C position list.

Note positions to copy from

Keep this next to you while you draw, or hand it to a student as a reference.

Treble clef

  • Lines, bottom to top: E, G, B, D, F
  • Spaces, bottom to top: F, A, C, E

Bass clef

  • Lines, bottom to top: G, B, D, F, A
  • Spaces, bottom to top: A, C, E, G

Middle C sits on one ledger line between the two staves. (If you teach in the UK or Australia, "staff" here means the same thing as "stave".)

Worked example: BADGE, three ways

Treble clef only

  • B: line 3
  • A: space 2
  • D: line 4
  • G: line 2
  • E: line 1 (or space 4, for a stretch)

Bass clef only

  • B: line 2
  • A: space 1
  • D: line 3
  • G: line 1
  • E: space 3

Grand staff, moving between the clefs

  • B: treble line 3
  • A: bass line 5
  • D: bass line 3
  • G: treble line 2
  • E: bass space 3

The word is the same each time, but the third version is a much harder reading task. That makes one word list reusable across several levels, and it gives a student who finishes early something more to do.

How to make the worksheet

  1. Choose the note range. Write down exactly which notes the student can read today. Don't include a note you are still introducing, unless the sheet is meant to practice that note.
  2. Pick 8 to 10 words from the matching list. Choose fewer for a student under about 7, or for anyone who tires quickly.
  3. Include look-alike words. Students often guess the word after two or three notes. Pairs that start the same way make guessing fail: BAG and BAD, FACE and FADE, CAGE and CAFE. A student has to keep reading past the first two notes to get those right.
  4. Draw one staff per word. Use whole notes with no stems or barlines, so nothing distracts from note position. Hand-drawing on blank staff paper works fine, and so does any free notation program.
  5. Leave one blank under each note. One letter per blank. Add a blank line for the whole word only if you are using the sentences below.
  6. Put the hardest words last. Save 6- and 7-letter words for the end, and let a student stop when the work stops being useful.
  7. Write the answer key as you draw. Don't try to work it out again later.

Fill-in sentences with a winter theme

Give the student the sentence and the notes. They name the notes, and the sentence tells them whether they got it right. All twelve are secular, so they work for any studio.

  1. Wrap the present and put it in a gift ___. (BAG)
  2. We warmed up with cocoa at the cozy ___. (CAFE)
  3. The snowman has a carrot in the middle of his ___. (FACE)
  4. Thread one more ___ onto the garland. (BEAD)
  5. The skater glides on the ___ of her blade. (EDGE)
  6. Scatter seed outside to ___ the winter birds. (FEED)
  7. Time for ___. The snow will still be there tomorrow. (BED)
  8. The footprints will ___ when the snow melts. (FADE)
  9. We ___ to stay out and sled a little longer. (BEGGED)
  10. Grandpa drinks ___ coffee so he can sleep. (DECAF)
  11. Don't forget your ___ at the airport. (BAGGAGE)
  12. The gingerbread house has a frosted front wall, or ___. (FACADE)

Sentences 1 to 8 use short words and suit students who are newer to reading. Sentences 9 to 12 work better for students who can read across the staff or grand staff.

Three ways to get more from the same words

Play it after naming it. Have the student play each word on the piano after writing the letters. CABBAGE is a nice one for this, because in treble clef (C, A, B, B, A, G, E) it moves around a small space and sounds almost like a tune.

Reverse the task. Give the word and a blank staff, and have the student write the notes. Writing a note on the staff is a different skill from naming one, and some students find it harder.

Limit the clue. Tell students who rush that they must fill in every letter before they say the word. If they name BAG from the first two notes, they have practiced guessing instead of reading.

Quick check before you print

  • Every note is one the student can already read, or the sheet is intended to stretch them
  • No word on the sheet is unfamiliar to this student
  • At least two words share a beginning, so guessing doesn't work
  • There is one blank per note and no accidentals
  • The answer key is written and matches the page
  • You have read every word aloud for accidental jokes
