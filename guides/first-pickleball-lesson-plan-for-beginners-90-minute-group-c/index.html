First Pickleball Lesson Plan for Beginners: 90-Minute Group Clinic

A complete, timed 90-minute plan for a group of brand-new adult pickleball players. It covers safety, rules, scoring, the serve and the return, with drills, coach cues and a fill-in planning sheet you can copy.

You're running a first session for new players, maybe at a rec center, an HOA court or a club "learn to play" night. You need a plan that gets everyone playing a real game before they leave and doesn't bury them in rules. This page is that plan, timed to the minute, with the drills, what to say and what to watch for.

*This plan was written with AI assistance. It is not from a certified professional coach. Check the rules summaries against the current official USA Pickleball rulebook before you teach them, and use your own judgment on player safety.*

What This Plan Assumes

  • One coach (a helper is a bonus)
  • 8 to 16 adult beginners, about 4 players per court
  • 90 minutes of court time
  • Loaner paddles, at least 3 balls per player, about 12 cones or flat spots, and a whistle or loud clap signal

With more than 4 players on a court, run drills in two waves: two pairs hit while two pairs wait at the sideline and rotate in every 2 minutes.

The Session Goal

By the end, every player can:

  1. Hit a legal serve into the correct box
  2. Explain the two-bounce rule
  3. Call the score in doubles
  4. Play one short game with help

Say this goal out loud at the start. Beginners relax when they know a game is coming.

Minute-by-Minute Plan

0:00-0:08 | Welcome and safety (8 min) Do names, ask who has played tennis or ping-pong, and hand out loaner paddles. Then give the safety briefing:

  • Never backpedal. Turn sideways and shuffle, or let the ball go. Stepping backward for a high ball is the classic beginner fall.
  • Call "Ball on!" and stop play when a ball rolls onto a court.
  • Don't run onto a neighboring court to chase a ball. Wait and ask for it.
  • Water breaks happen at every rotation. Tell players to sit out any time they need to.

0:08-0:18 | Warm-up and paddle feel (10 min)

  • 3 min of light movement: side shuffles along the baseline, walking lunges, arm circles.
  • 3 min of paddle bounces. Players bounce the ball up off the paddle, then down onto the floor. Cue: "Soft wrist, quiet paddle."
  • 4 min of partner tosses. One player tosses underhand to the other's forehand, and they catch it on the paddle face, then tap it back. Switch every 10 balls.

0:18-0:28 | Court tour and the big rules (10 min) Walk everyone to one court and point to each part as you name it:

  • The kitchen (non-volley zone): the 7-foot area on each side of the net. You can't volley (hit before the bounce) while standing in it or on its line. You *can* step in to play a ball that has bounced.
  • Service courts: the left and right boxes behind the kitchen.
  • The two-bounce rule: the serve must bounce, and the return must bounce. After that, players may volley or play off the bounce.
  • Faults: the ball goes out, into the net, or a player volleys from the kitchen.

Keep this to 10 minutes. Scoring comes later, once players have hit some balls.

0:28-0:45 | Drill 1: The serve (17 min) Setup: players stand at the baseline and serve diagonally to a partner standing in the opposite box. Put a cone deep in each service box as a target.

Teach the legal basics. Check the current rulebook for the full detail:

  • Underhand motion, contact below the waist
  • One foot behind the baseline at contact, neither foot touching the line
  • Serve diagonally, clearing the kitchen. A serve that lands on the kitchen line is a fault.
  • A drop serve (drop the ball, let it bounce, then hit it) is also legal and is easier for many beginners

Coach cues: "Pendulum, not a swat." "Finish high toward your target." "Aim deep, not hard." Common mistakes: contact above the waist, stepping on the baseline, and aiming short so the ball lands in the kitchen. Easier version: every player uses the drop serve, or serves from halfway between the baseline and the kitchen line. Harder version: every player has to hit the cone zone 3 times in a row before moving on.

0:45-0:50 | Water break and rotation (5 min) Mix up the pairs so players hit with someone new.

0:50-1:02 | Drill 2: Serve, return and stay back (12 min) Setup: there are two players on each side. One serves, the receiver lets the ball bounce, then returns it deep and high. Stop the point after the return bounces.

Coach cues: "Let it bounce. Let it bounce." (This teaches the two-bounce rule by repetition.) "Return deep, then walk to the kitchen line." Common mistakes: volleying the serve, returning short, and the returner staying at the baseline. Easier version: the coach feeds the "serve" by hand from the service area. Harder version: play the third shot too, and the serving team must let it bounce.

1:02-1:12 | Scoring walk-through (10 min) Have all four players on one court say the score out loud while they walk through positions:

  • In traditional doubles scoring, only the serving team scores points. Games usually go to 11, and you must win by 2.
  • The score has three numbers: server's team score, receiver's team score, server number (1 or 2).
  • The game starts at 0-0-2, so only one player on the first serving team serves before the serve goes to the other team.
  • When the serving team wins a point, the server switches sides with their partner and serves again.
  • When the serving team loses a rally, the partner serves (server 2). When server 2 loses the rally, the serve goes to the other team (side out).
  • First server of each side-out serves from the right box.

Some clubs now play rally scoring or other local formats. Tell players which one your facility uses, and check the current rulebook for the exact wording.

Run it as a walk-through with no hitting. The coach calls "Point to serving team!" or "Fault!" and the server calls the new score.

1:12-1:25 | Game play (13 min) Play short games to 7 or a time cap of 6 minutes, then rotate partners. The server must call the score before every serve. Walk between courts and only correct score calls and kitchen volleys. Save technique fixes for next week.

1:25-1:30 | Wrap-up (5 min) Ask three questions to the whole group:

  1. "What does the score's third number mean?"
  2. "When can you volley?"
  3. "Where does the serve have to land?"

Tell players what next session covers (dinking and the kitchen). Ask them to practice 20 serves at home or at open play.

Coach Tips for a First Session

  • Talk less than 25% of the time. If you've been talking for more than 2 minutes, give them a ball.
  • Correct one thing per player per drill. Beginners can't fix three things at once.
  • Use the same signal every time. One whistle means stop and hold your ball. Two means rotate.
  • Pair by experience at first. Put tennis or ping-pong players together so true beginners aren't overwhelmed. Mix them later.
  • Keep the game block even if you run late. Cut a drill short instead. Playing a real game is what brings people back next week.

If Things Go Off-Plan

  • Too many players for the courts: run a "king of the court" waiting line at the sideline, and have players waiting call the score for the court in front of them.
  • Uneven numbers: the extra player feeds balls in drills and joins the next game rotation.
  • Mixed abilities: give the confident players the harder versions of each drill and keep everyone on the same timing.
  • Bad weather or a short slot: keep the warm-up, the serve drill and the scoring walk-through, and drop Drill 2.

What Comes Next

A typical beginner sequence after this session is:

  • Week 2: dinking and the kitchen
  • Week 3: the third-shot drop and moving up to the net
  • Week 4: doubles positioning and full game play

Run each one with the same structure as this session: goal, warm-up, 2-3 drills, game play and wrap-up questions.
