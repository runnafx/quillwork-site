Battle of Hastings Cover Lesson for Year 7: No-Tech Plan + Answers

A complete, print-and-go 60-minute KS3 History cover lesson on 1066 and the Battle of Hastings. It includes a supervisor script, a reading, source questions, a mark scheme and finish-early tasks.

You need Year 7 History cover for tomorrow. The person running it may not be a historian, and the room may have no working projector. This page gives you a whole lesson on the Battle of Hastings that you can copy into a document, print and leave on the desk. It needs no laptop, no slides and no subject knowledge, and the answers are at the bottom.

Lesson question: Why did William of Normandy win the Battle of Hastings in 1066?

Fits: Year 7 units on the Norman Conquest, medieval England or "1066 and all that".

You need: One copy of the student reading and tasks per student (or one between two), lined paper or exercise books, and pens. That's all.

Lesson at a Glance (60 Minutes)

  1. 0-5 min: Settle the class, take the register, write the lesson question on the board.
  2. 5-10 min: Starter: "Who should be king?" quick-write.
  3. 10-25 min: Read the background text, then answer the comprehension questions.
  4. 25-40 min: Source work: what do two sources say about the battle?
  5. 40-55 min: Main task: rank the reasons William won and explain your top choice.
  6. 55-60 min: Exit question and collect books.

For a 50-minute lesson, cut the main task down to "explain your top reason only".

Cover Supervisor Script

You can read these lines out word for word.

  • Start: "Today you are working on the Battle of Hastings in 1066. Your teacher has set all the work, and it is on these sheets. Write the title and the question from the board in your book."
  • Starter: "You have five minutes. Read the box about the three men who wanted to be king. Write one sentence saying who you think had the best claim, and why. There is no wrong answer."
  • Reading: "Read the background text on your own or quietly with the person next to you. Then answer questions 1 to 6 in full sentences."
  • Sources: "Now look at Source A and Source B. Answer questions 7 to 10. A source is evidence from the past. Think about who made it and why."
  • Main task: "Put the five reasons in order, from most important to least important. Then write a paragraph explaining your number one reason."
  • End: "Write your answer to the exit question at the bottom of your work. Books on the end of the table, please."

If students finish early: send them to the "Finish-Early Tasks" section. If a student says "we've done this already": "Great, then you can aim for the challenge question and the extension."

Student Sheet: Starter (Who Should Be King?)

King Edward the Confessor died on 5 January 1066 with no children. Three men claimed the throne:

  • Harold Godwinson: the most powerful English earl. He said Edward named him as the next king just before he died. The English nobles chose him, and he was crowned the day after Edward died.
  • William, Duke of Normandy: a relative of Edward from northern France. He said Edward had promised him the throne years earlier. He also said Harold had sworn a holy oath on a visit to Normandy to support William's claim.
  • Harald Hardrada, King of Norway: an experienced Viking warrior. He based his claim on an old agreement between earlier kings of Norway and England.

Write one sentence: Who had the best claim, and why?

Student Sheet: Background Reading

In September 1066, Harald Hardrada invaded the north of England. Tostig, Harold Godwinson's own brother, was with him. King Harold marched his army north very quickly and beat them at the Battle of Stamford Bridge on 25 September. Hardrada and Tostig were both killed.

Only days later, on 28 September, William landed on the south coast at Pevensey with an army of knights, archers and foot soldiers. Harold had to march his tired army back south, a journey of around 250 miles, and gather more men on the way.

The two armies met on 14 October 1066 on a ridge called Senlac Hill, a few miles from Hastings. Harold's soldiers stood on top of the hill in a shield wall, a tight line of overlapping shields. His best troops were the housecarls, professional warriors with huge two-handed axes. Many of the others were men of the fyrd, part-time soldiers called up from farms and villages.

For hours the Normans attacked uphill and could not break the shield wall. At one point a rumour spread that William had been killed, and he lifted his helmet so his men could see his face. Some English soldiers chased Normans who were running away down the hill, and once they left the shield wall they were surrounded and cut down. Norman writers later claimed that William's army pretended to retreat on purpose to trick the English. Historians still argue about whether this really happened.

By evening Harold was dead, and the English army broke up. William was crowned King of England in Westminster Abbey on Christmas Day 1066.

Comprehension Questions

  1. Why was there a fight for the throne in 1066?
  2. Who won the Battle of Stamford Bridge, and when?
  3. Why was Harold's army tired by 14 October?
  4. What was a shield wall?
  5. Explain the difference between housecarls and the fyrd.
  6. Why did William lift his helmet during the battle?

Student Sheet: Source Work

Source A: a description of a scene from the Bayeux Tapestry. The Bayeux Tapestry is an embroidered cloth about 70 metres long, made within a few years of the battle. Most historians think it was ordered by Bishop Odo, William's half-brother. One scene shows Harold swearing an oath on holy objects in front of William. A later scene shows the death of Harold, and one figure near his name seems to be holding an arrow near his eye.

Source B: a summary of the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle. The Chronicle was written by English monks. One version says that William attacked before all of Harold's army had gathered, that the English fought hard, and that the French won because of the sins of the English people.

  1. Who most likely ordered Source A to be made?
  2. Why might Source A show Harold swearing an oath to William? (Hint: think about what this would make Harold look like.)
  3. According to Source B, why did the English lose?
  4. Challenge: Source A and Source B were made by different sides. Explain how this might change what each one says.

Student Sheet: Main Task (Rank the Reasons)

Copy these five reasons into your book, ordered from most important (1) to least important (5):

  • A. Harold's army was tired after Stamford Bridge and the long march.
  • B. William had a mix of troops: archers, knights on horseback and foot soldiers.
  • C. Some English soldiers left the shield wall to chase the Normans.
  • D. Harold was killed during the battle.
  • E. Harold did not wait for all his soldiers to arrive before fighting.

Then write a paragraph about your number one reason, using this frame:

  • "I think the most important reason William won was..."
  • "This is because..."
  • "For example, in the battle..."
  • "This was more important than [another reason] because..."

Exit question: In one sentence, was William's victory mostly down to skill, luck or Harold's mistakes?

Finish-Early Tasks

  • Draw your own "tapestry scene" of one moment from the battle, with a caption written from the Norman side and a second caption written from the English side.
  • Write a short diary entry as a housecarl the night before the battle.
  • Make a timeline of 1066 with at least five dates from the reading.

Answers and Mark Guide

  1. Edward the Confessor died in January 1066 with no children, so there was no clear heir and three men claimed the throne.
  2. King Harold Godwinson won, on 25 September 1066.
  3. They had fought at Stamford Bridge and then marched about 250 miles south in a short time.
  4. A tight line of soldiers with overlapping shields, which was very hard to break.
  5. Housecarls were full-time, professional, well-armed warriors. The fyrd were part-time soldiers, mostly farmers, called up when needed.
  6. A rumour spread that he had been killed, so he showed his face to stop his men panicking and running away.
  7. Most likely Bishop Odo, William's half-brother.
  8. It makes Harold look like an oath-breaker who had no right to the crown, which makes William's invasion look fair and justified.
  9. William attacked before Harold's whole army was ready, and the writer thought God let the French win because of English sins.
  10. Strong answers note that Source A comes from the Norman side and is likely to make William look rightful and Harold look guilty. Source B was written by English monks, who explain the defeat through bad timing and God's will rather than Norman skill. Both sources are useful, but each has a point of view.

Main task: Any ranking is fine if it's explained. Good paragraphs use one specific detail from the reading, such as the shield wall breaking or the march from Stamford Bridge, and compare at least two reasons. Reason D is a common top choice. Push students to explain that once Harold died the English had no leader and no claimant left to fight for.

Exit question: Accept any answer that is backed up with a reason.

Printing Checklist for Cover Managers

  • Print the student sheets double-sided: the starter and reading on one side, the sources and tasks on the other.
  • Print one copy of the script and answers for the supervisor only.
  • Write the lesson question on the board yourself, or leave a sticky note asking the supervisor to do it.
  • Add the seating plan and any SEND notes to the front of the supervisor's copy.
  • For lower-attaining groups, students can work in pairs on the reading and answer questions 1, 2, 4 and 6 only.
