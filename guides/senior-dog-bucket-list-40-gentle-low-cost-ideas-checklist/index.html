Senior Dog Bucket List: 40 Gentle, Low-Cost Ideas (Checklist)

A printable-style checklist of 40 low-cost bucket list ideas for an old or seriously ill dog, sorted by energy level. It also explains how to choose the right ones and how to adjust them on hard days.

When your dog is old or ill, many owners start thinking about a bucket list. Most lists online assume a healthy dog with plenty of energy and a big budget. This one is for the dog you have now. Most of the ideas below cost little or nothing. Many can be done from a dog bed, and they are grouped by how much energy each one needs.

This page gives no medical advice. Your veterinarian knows your dog's condition. Before you try new foods, longer outings or anything physical, ask them first.

How to Use This List

  1. Pick 3 to 5 ideas for this week, not 40. A short list you finish is better than a long one that turns into another worry.
  2. Match each idea to today's energy, not last year's. Use the energy labels below. If your dog has a good day, move up a level. On a hard day, choose something from the "resting" group.
  3. Ask your vet about anything involving food or exertion. Some conditions call for special diets, and some medications don't mix with certain foods. Even small treats can matter.
  4. Take photos or a short video, but don't let filming take over. One photo per activity is enough. Put the phone down for the rest.
  5. Write one line afterward. Note the date, what you did and how your dog reacted. Those lines often mean more later than the photos do.

Resting Ideas (Low Energy: Can Be Done Lying Down)

  • [ ] Move their bed to a sunny spot and spend an afternoon there together
  • [ ] Do a "sniff tray": hide a few treats or kibble in a towel or a muffin tin for them to find
  • [ ] Give a slow, gentle massage along the ears, chest and paws (stop if they seem uncomfortable)
  • [ ] Read them a book or the newspaper out loud. They like your voice more than the story
  • [ ] Let them nap on the couch or bed if that's normally off limits (use a ramp or lift them if needed)
  • [ ] Bring a favorite toy close and let them hold it or chew it gently
  • [ ] Watch a nature video together with the sound on low
  • [ ] Have a lazy brushing session with a soft brush
  • [ ] Sit outside together on a mild evening and just listen
  • [ ] Ask family members to each spend 10 quiet minutes with them, one at a time
  • [ ] Let them lie in the yard or on a blanket in the grass
  • [ ] Make a "comfort nest" with extra blankets and an old t-shirt that smells like you

Gentle Outings (Some Energy: Short and Slow)

  • [ ] Take a car ride with the window cracked open a little, to somewhere with interesting smells
  • [ ] Do a "sniffari": a very slow walk where your dog sets the pace and sniffs as long as they want
  • [ ] Use a wagon, stroller or dog sling to visit a park they can no longer walk to
  • [ ] Visit an old favorite spot, such as the first walk route, a beach or a friend's house
  • [ ] Go through a drive-through and get them a plain item your vet has okayed
  • [ ] Sit outside a café or on a bench and people-watch together
  • [ ] Visit a pet-friendly store and let them take in the smells
  • [ ] Stop by a calm friend or neighbor who loves them
  • [ ] Watch the sunrise or sunset from the car or a porch
  • [ ] Revisit the place you adopted them, even just from the parking lot
  • [ ] Have a picnic on a blanket in your own yard

Good-Day Ideas (More Energy: Keep It Short)

  • [ ] Play one short game of their favorite: tug, fetch on soft ground, or hide-and-seek in the house
  • [ ] Do a gentle paddle in shallow, calm water if they have always loved it and your vet agrees
  • [ ] Play in fresh snow or fallen leaves for a few minutes
  • [ ] Have a playdate with one calm dog friend they already know
  • [ ] Practice an old trick they're proud of and celebrate it
  • [ ] Learn one very simple new trick, like "touch" (nose to your hand)
  • [ ] Open a new toy, and let them "unwrap" it from paper

Food Treats (Vet Approval First)

Ask your vet before trying any of these. Some dogs with kidney, pancreas, heart or stomach conditions need to avoid certain foods.

  • [ ] A small portion of plain cooked chicken or plain cooked egg
  • [ ] A dog-safe "pup cup" or a small lick of plain, dog-safe frozen treat
  • [ ] A homemade birthday or "gotcha day" celebration with a dog-safe treat
  • [ ] A lick mat with a vet-approved spread
  • [ ] Let them try one new dog-safe food they've never had

Memory-Making Ideas (Any Energy Level)

  • [ ] Take a paw print with pet-safe ink or a clay kit (wipe the paw afterward)
  • [ ] Book a photo session, or ask a friend with a good camera to do one at home
  • [ ] Record a short video of their everyday sounds: snoring, tail thumps, the "dinner is late" bark
  • [ ] Write down their quirks, nicknames and favorite things before they fade from memory
  • [ ] Have friends and family write a short note about a favorite memory of them

How to Adjust on Hard Days

Plans will change, and that's normal. Some practical rules:

  • If they're tired, bring the activity to them. A walk can become a car window sniff, and a park visit can become a blanket in the yard.
  • Watch their signals, not the list. If they turn away, pant heavily, seem restless or want to lie down, stop. The point is their comfort, not ticking a box.
  • Shorten everything. Five good minutes count.
  • Keep a simple note of good days and hard days. Writing down appetite, sleep, mobility and mood helps you give your vet clear information at the next appointment. Bring the notes and let your vet interpret them.
  • Don't measure love by how many items you finish. Lying quietly next to your dog is enough on its own.

Ideas for Senior Cats Too

Many items above work for cats with small changes. Try sun-spot naps, a new box or paper bag, brushing, a dish of cat grass, a "window TV" bird feeder outside, gentle wand-toy play on good days, or lying together on your bed. As with dogs, check any new food with your vet first.

A Simple Fill-In Page

Copy this into a notebook or print it:

Dog's name: Age: Favorite things right now: This week's 3 bucket list picks: 1. 2. 3.

Done on (date): What we did: How they reacted: One thing I want to remember:

If You're Buying for Someone Else

If a friend's dog is nearing the end, a ready-made bucket list can be a kind gift. It gives them small, doable plans when everything feels heavy. Offer to help with one item yourself, such as driving them to a favorite park, taking photos, or simply sitting with them for an afternoon.

A Final Note

This list isn't about building perfect last days. It helps you spend time on purpose while you still have it. Pick one idea for today, and it can be a small one.
