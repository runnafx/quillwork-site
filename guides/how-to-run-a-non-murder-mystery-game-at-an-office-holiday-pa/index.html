How to Run a Non-Murder Mystery Game at an Office Holiday Party

A step-by-step plan for running a workplace-safe whodunit for 8 to 16 coworkers, in person or on Zoom. It includes an HR checklist, a 45-minute run sheet and a complete free 8-player mini mystery you can print today.

Murder mystery parties work well at office events. People talk to coworkers they don't usually talk to, quiet people get a role, and the reveal gets a big reaction. The problem is the "murder" part. Many HR teams won't approve fake deaths, poison, affairs or drinking games. That's reasonable, because those themes can make some people uncomfortable at work.

You don't need any of that. You need a missing object, a short time window, a handful of suspects and clues that point to exactly one person. This guide covers how to set it up, how to run it, and a ready-to-use 8-player mystery at the end.

Step 1: Pass the "Would HR Approve This?" Check

Before you write or buy anything, check the theme against this list. If every answer is "yes," you're probably fine.

  • The crime is harmless. Something is missing, swapped or hidden. Nobody is dead, hurt or in danger.
  • No romance, rivalry or embarrassing secrets. Keep secrets to things like "you secretly ate the last donut," not "you're dating someone."
  • No alcohol in the mechanics. No clues found "at the bar" and no drinking forfeits.
  • Characters are job roles, not real people. Use "the Facilities Lead," not "Dave from Facilities." Never put real coworkers' habits into a character.
  • Character names and roles are gender-neutral. Anyone can play anyone, so casting takes seconds.
  • No costumes required. Make them optional and keep them simple, like a hat or a name badge.
  • Nobody has to act. Players can read their lines straight off the card.
  • The culprit's motive is sympathetic. The best reveals end with "they were planning a surprise," not "they're a villain."

Step 2: Prep in Under 30 Minutes

  1. Count your players. A good mystery has 8 core roles. With 9 to 16 people, add "detectives" or put players in pairs, two people sharing one character card. Pairs work well for nervous players.
  2. Print one character card per role. Each card has a public intro (read aloud), a secret (keep hidden) and one or two clues.
  3. Print the clue cards for each round. Keep them face down, sorted by round.
  4. Make one accusation ballot per person or team. Slips of paper with "Who did it? Why?" are enough.
  5. Grab 3 to 5 props from the office. Things like a mug, wrapping paper, a sticky note or a key card. Props are optional, but they get people standing up and moving.
  6. Read the solution once yourself. You're the host, so you need to know the answer to keep things on track.

Step 3: Run It in 45 Minutes

This run sheet fits into a lunch hour or the middle of a party.

  • 0:00–0:05, Setup. Hand out cards. Explain the rules: read your intro when asked, share clues freely, and lying is only allowed if your card says so.
  • 0:05–0:12, Introductions. Each character reads their public intro aloud. Then the host reads the "case summary."
  • 0:12–0:22, Round 1. Players mingle and share the clues on their cards. Reveal the first clue card at the start of the round.
  • 0:22–0:32, Round 2. Reveal the second clue card. Now people start comparing alibis.
  • 0:32–0:37, Final questions. Each player or team may ask one direct question to any character.
  • 0:37–0:40, Accusations. Everyone fills in a ballot.
  • 0:40–0:45, Reveal. The host reads the solution, walking through each clue in order. Give a small prize to everyone who got it right.

Host tip: If a group gets stuck, read one of the clues aloud again yourself. Don't give out new information. Usually someone already has the key clue and hasn't shared it.

Step 4: Adapt for Zoom or Hybrid Teams

  • Send character cards as private messages or individual emails before the call, so nobody sees anyone else's secret.
  • Share clue cards by screen-sharing at the start of each round.
  • Use breakout rooms of 3 or 4 people for the mingling rounds. Shuffle the rooms between rounds so everyone talks to new people.
  • Collect accusations with a poll or by having people send them to the host in private chat.
  • For hybrid teams, pair each remote person with someone in the room so they don't get left out of side conversations.

Step 5: Make Sure the Mystery Is Fair

Players get frustrated with a mystery when the answer feels random. Before you run any mystery, including one you wrote yourself, check:

  • Every clue in the solution has actually been handed out. No secret information shows up only at the reveal.
  • Exactly one person fits all the clues. Draw a quick grid with suspects as rows and clues as columns, then tick each box. Only one row should be fully ticked.
  • Every innocent person is ruled out by at least one clue. Otherwise people will argue they "could have done it."

Free Mini Mystery: "The Case of the Missing Team Mug" (8 Players)

Case summary (host reads aloud): The team's prized "World's Okayest Team" mug was on the top shelf of the break room. At 2:10 p.m. it was there. At 2:20 p.m. it was gone. Somewhere on this floor is the person who took it.

Characters (Public Intro / Secret / Clue)

  1. Alex, Event Planner. *Intro:* "I've been running around all afternoon with party supplies." *Secret:* You spent 2:00 to 2:30 wrapping gifts alone in the supply room. *Clue:* "There was gold glitter on the break room shelf where the mug used to be."
  2. Jordan, Designer. *Intro:* "I was in yesterday's planning meeting and the gift-wrap station today." *Secret:* You took the mug. You're secretly filling it with candy as a thank-you gift for Riley. You may dodge questions, but you can't lie about your clue. *Clue:* "I was by myself between 2:10 and 2:20."
  3. Sam, IT Support. *Intro:* "I fixed the break room printer this week." *Secret:* You were in the server room with Riley from 2:05 to 2:25. *Clue:* "In yesterday's meeting, Quinn told us the mug was moving to the top shelf. Only people at that meeting knew it was there."
  4. Riley, Facilities Lead. *Intro:* "I keep this place running." *Secret:* You were with Sam in the server room from 2:05 to 2:25. *Clue:* "Yesterday's meeting was Jordan, Sam, Quinn and me. Nobody else."
  5. Casey, Finance. *Intro:* "I wrapped my Secret Santa gift this morning." *Secret:* You were on a video call from 2:00 to 2:30. Quinn joined at 2:12. *Clue:* "The gift-wrap station uses gold glitter paper. Alex, Jordan, Taylor and I all used it today."
  6. Morgan, Sales. *Intro:* "I live on break room coffee." *Secret:* None. *Clue:* "I refilled my coffee at 2:10 and the mug was definitely on the top shelf. I left right after."
  7. Taylor, Coordinator. *Intro:* "I helped wrap gifts after lunch." *Secret:* You were alone at your desk from 2:10 to 2:20, eating the last donut. *Clue:* "I never went to yesterday's meeting. I was out at a dentist appointment."
  8. Quinn, Team Manager. *Intro:* "That mug was my idea." *Secret:* You were on Casey's call from 2:12 onward. *Clue:* "Casey never left the call. I could see them on camera the whole time."

Clue Cards (Host Reveals)

  • Round 1: "The mug was taken between 2:10 and 2:20."
  • Round 2: "Whoever took the mug had glitter on their hands and already knew which shelf it was on."

Solution and Logic Check

The thief had to:

  • Have glitter on their hands: Alex, Jordan, Taylor or Casey (Casey's clue).
  • Know the mug's location: Jordan, Sam, Quinn or Riley (Sam's and Riley's clues).
  • Be unaccounted for between 2:10 and 2:20: Sam and Riley vouch for each other, Casey and Quinn are on the call, and Morgan left right after seeing the mug.

Only Jordan is in both of the first two groups. Here is why each of the others is ruled out:

  • Alex and Taylor weren't at the meeting.
  • Casey has a confirmed alibi.
  • Sam, Riley and Quinn didn't use the glitter paper.
  • Morgan didn't use the glitter paper and wasn't at the meeting.

Reveal (host reads): "Jordan took the mug, but not for themselves. They were filling it with candy as a thank-you gift for Riley, who keeps this office running. Jordan, please present the mug!" If you have a real mug and some candy, hand it over here. It's a nice way to end the game.

For 9 to 16 players: Pair people up on the 8 characters, or add "Detective" players who get no card and take the lead on questioning.

Quick Checklist Before Your Party

  • Theme passed the HR check in Step 1
  • One character card per role, plus extras for pairs
  • Clue cards sorted by round
  • Ballots and a pen for each team
  • You've read the solution
  • Zoom: cards sent privately, breakout rooms set up, poll ready
  • A small prize for the winners
