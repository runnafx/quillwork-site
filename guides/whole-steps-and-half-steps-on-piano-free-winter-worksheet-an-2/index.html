Whole Steps and Half Steps on Piano: Free Winter Worksheet + Answers

A simple way to teach whole and half steps (tones and semitones) to Level 1 piano students, with a printable winter worksheet in five parts and a full answer key.

Many students can name notes and still go blank when asked "is that a whole step or a half step?" This page gives you a short way to explain it, a five-minute lesson routine, and a winter worksheet you can copy or write out by hand. The answer key is at the bottom.

It's written for students at about Level 1 (late Primer to early Level 2). Some students know these as tones and semitones. Both sets of terms appear below.

The quick answer

  • A half step (semitone) goes from one key to the very next key, with no key in between. That next key can be black or white.
  • A whole step (tone) is two half steps. You skip exactly one key, black or white.

Students only need one rule: count the keys, not the letters.

The "two snowdrifts" rule for white keys

On the white keys, most neighbors are a whole step apart because a black key sits between them. There are only two places where two white keys touch with no black key between them:

  • E to F
  • B to C

Call these the two "snowdrifts" where no black key gets in the way. If a student remembers just this, they can work out every white-key pair on their own:

  • C–D, D–E, F–G, G–A, A–B are whole steps
  • E–F and B–C are half steps

How to teach it in one lesson (5 minutes)

  1. Touch every key. Starting on middle C, have the student play every key going up, black and white, and say "half, half, half…" on each move. Every one of those moves is a half step.
  2. Skip one. Go back to C. Play C, skip C-sharp, land on D. Say "whole step: I skipped one key." Repeat from F to G and from G to A.
  3. Find the snowdrifts. Ask the student to find the two places where white keys touch with no black key between them. Let them discover E–F and B–C themselves.
  4. Mix in black keys. Play E to F-sharp: it skips F, so it's a whole step. Then play B-flat to B: no key in between, so it's a half step. This is where real understanding shows up.
  5. Move to the staff. Show that line-to-space (or space-to-line) is always a step on the staff. The staff can't tell you whether it's whole or half, though. The keyboard decides.

Printable worksheet: "Snowy Steps"

Copy each part onto a page, or write it on the board for a group class. Students write W (whole step) or H (half step) unless the part says otherwise. A keyboard picture at the top of the page helps, but the activities work without one.

Part A: White-key snowballs

Write W or H for each pair.

  1. C to D
  2. E to F
  3. G to A
  4. B to C
  5. D to E
  6. F to G
  7. A to B
  8. C to B (going down)

Part B: Mittens with sharps and flats

Write W or H. Find each key on the piano before you answer.

  1. C to C-sharp
  2. E to F-sharp
  3. B to C-sharp
  4. A to B-flat
  5. F-sharp to G-sharp
  6. B-flat to C
  7. E-flat to E
  8. D to E-flat
  9. A-flat to B-flat
  10. F to F-sharp

Part C: Steps on the staff

Each pair is a step on the staff. Decide whether it's a whole step or a half step. If you only have text, draw the notes on a staff for the student, or have them draw the notes first.

Treble clef

  1. E (bottom line) to F (first space)
  2. G (second line) to A (second space)
  3. B (middle line) to C (third space)
  4. D (fourth line) to E (top space)

Bass clef

  1. B (second line) to C (second space)
  2. E (third space) to F (fourth line)
  3. A (top line) down to G (top space)

Part D: Build a step

Write the note that answers each question. Use a sharp or flat if you need one.

  1. A whole step up from C is ____
  2. A whole step up from E is ____
  3. A whole step up from B is ____
  4. A whole step up from G is ____
  5. A half step up from E is ____
  6. A half step up from B is ____
  7. A half step up from G is ____
  8. A whole step down from D is ____
  9. A whole step down from F is ____
  10. A whole step down from C is ____

Part E: Snowman tunes

Write W or H between each pair of notes. Then play the tune.

Snowman Climb: G – A – B – C – D (four answers: G–A, A–B, B–C, C–D)

Mitten March: E – F – G – F – E – D (five answers: E–F, F–G, G–F, F–E, E–D)

Bonus question: Play C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C. Write the pattern of W and H. Where do the two half steps fall?

Part E

  • Snowman Climb: W, W, H, W
  • Mitten March: H, W, W, H, W
  • Bonus: W, W, H, W, W, W, H. The half steps fall between E–F (notes 3–4) and B–C (notes 7–8). This is the major scale pattern. Older students can save it for later lessons.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Counting letters instead of keys. A student sees E and F as "next-door letters" and says whole step. Ask: "Is there a key in between?"
  • Thinking black keys are always involved. E–F and B–C are half steps with only white keys.
  • Thinking a whole step always means white to white. E to F-sharp and B-flat to C are whole steps that use black keys.
  • Counting the starting key. Some students count "one" on the key they start on and get every answer wrong by one. Teach them to count the *moves*, not the keys they land on.
  • Trusting the staff. Line-to-space looks the same for E–F and G–A. The staff shows a step. The keyboard shows what kind.

Adapting the worksheet

For a student who is still in Primer: Use only Part A and the Snowman Climb. Let them play every answer before they write it.

For a student moving into Level 2: Add the Part E bonus and ask them to build the same W-W-H-W-W-W-H pattern starting on G. They'll find they need F-sharp to make it work, which is a natural introduction to the G major scale.

For group lessons: Turn Part A into a snowball game. Call out a pair, and students hold up a W card or an H card. Anyone who answers wrong walks to the piano and checks it.

For UK and Australian students: Replace W and H with T (tone) and S (semitone). Every answer stays the same.

How long it takes

Most Level 1 students can finish Parts A and B in one lesson with the keyboard in front of them. Part C fits well as homework the next week. Part D is the real check: a student who gets F-sharp for "a whole step up from E" without help understands the idea and isn't just remembering pairs.
