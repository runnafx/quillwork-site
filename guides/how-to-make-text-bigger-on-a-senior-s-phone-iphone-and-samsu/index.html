How to Make Text Bigger on a Senior's Phone (iPhone and Samsung)

A step-by-step checklist for making an older relative's iPhone or Samsung Galaxy easier to read and hear: bigger text, bold letters, larger icons, louder ringing and a screen that stays on longer. It ends with a short test you can do with them and a card to fill in once the settings are done.

If your parent or grandparent squints at their phone, holds it at arm's length, or misses calls because they can't read who is ringing, you can usually fix most of it in about 15 minutes with settings already on the phone. You don't need to buy an app.

This guide covers iPhone and Samsung Galaxy. Menu names change a little between software versions. If a step doesn't match what you see, open Settings, tap the search bar at the top and type the setting's name, such as "Text Size" or "Font size".

Before you start:

  • Sit next to the person and change the settings with them, not for them. Let them hold the phone and tell you which size is comfortable.
  • Have them wear the glasses they normally use for the phone.
  • Make one change at a time and ask "Is that better or worse?" Bigger is not always better. Very large text can push words off the screen and make apps harder to use.

Part 1: iPhone

1. Make the text bigger

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Display & Brightness.
  3. Tap Text Size.
  4. Drag the slider to the right until the person can read the sample text comfortably.

This changes the text in Settings, Messages, Mail, Contacts and other apps that support it. Some apps ignore it.

2. Go even bigger if needed

If the largest size from step 1 still isn't enough:

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility.
  2. Tap Display & Text Size, then Larger Text.
  3. Turn on Larger Accessibility Sizes.
  4. Drag the slider to the size they want.

Tip: Look at Messages and the Phone app after this change. At the very largest sizes, some screens get crowded. If they do, move the slider back one or two notches.

3. Turn on bold text

Thin letters are often harder to read than small ones.

  1. Open Settings, then Display & Brightness.
  2. Turn on Bold Text.

4. Make icons and buttons bigger

  1. Open Settings, then Display & Brightness.
  2. Tap Display Zoom.
  3. Choose Larger Text (on some versions it's called Zoomed). Tap Done, then confirm.

The phone switches to the larger view. Everything on screen gets bigger, including the keyboard. Not every iPhone model has this option.

5. Increase contrast

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size.
  2. Try turning on Increase Contrast. Also try Reduce Transparency, which gives some menus a solid background so text is easier to read.

Keep each one only if the person says it helps.

6. Keep the screen on longer

If the screen goes dark while they're still reading:

  1. Open Settings, then Display & Brightness.
  2. Tap Auto-Lock and choose a longer time, such as 2 or 5 minutes.

A longer time uses a bit more battery, and the phone stays unlocked longer if it's left lying around.

7. Make it louder

  1. Open Settings, then Sounds & Haptics.
  2. Drag the Ringtone and Alerts slider all the way up, or as high as is comfortable.
  3. Tap Ringtone and pick one that's easy to hear. Lower-pitched, steady tones are often easier for people with hearing loss than high chimes. Let them choose.

8. Use the iPhone as a magnifying glass

The iPhone has a built-in Magnifier app for reading medicine labels, menus and small print. Search for "Magnifier" on the Home Screen. If you plan to use it often, add it to the Home Screen or Control Center.

One setting to be careful with: Accessibility > Zoom magnifies the whole screen when you double-tap with three fingers. It's useful for some people, but if it gets switched on by accident the screen looks "stuck" and enlarged, which confuses many people. Only turn it on if you teach them how to turn it off: double-tap again with three fingers.

Part 2: Samsung Galaxy

1. Make the text bigger and bold

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Display.
  3. Tap Font size and style.
  4. Drag the Font size slider to the right.
  5. Turn on Bold font.

2. Make icons and buttons bigger

  1. Open Settings, then Display.
  2. Tap Screen zoom and drag the slider to the right.

Screen zoom controls the size of icons and on-screen controls. Font size controls the size of words. Most people do best with both turned up partway rather than one turned up all the way.

3. Try Easy mode

Samsung's Easy mode gives the phone a simpler Home screen with larger icons and text, and adds a longer touch-and-hold delay so taps aren't misread as presses.

  1. Open Settings, then Display.
  2. Tap Easy mode and turn it on.

Good to know: Screen zoom usually disappears while Easy mode is on. Font size and style still work. Easy mode also rearranges the Home screen. If the person already knows where their apps are, ask them before you switch it on. You can turn it off the same way.

4. Increase contrast

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility.
  2. Look for Visibility enhancements (called Vision enhancements on some versions).
  3. Try High contrast fonts, which adds a dark or light outline around text. Keep it only if it helps.

The same menu usually has Magnification options. As with iPhone Zoom, only turn them on if you show the person how to turn them off.

5. Keep the screen on longer

  1. Open Settings, then Display.
  2. Tap Screen timeout and choose 2 or 5 minutes.

6. Make it louder

  1. Open Settings, then Sounds and vibration.
  2. Tap Volume and turn up Ringtone and Notifications.
  3. Go back and tap Ringtone to choose one that's easy to hear.

On Samsung, the side volume buttons may only change media or ringtone volume, depending on settings. Changing it in Settings means the ring volume is set properly.

Part 3: The 5-Minute Check With Them

After making the changes, ask the person to do each of these on their own while you watch:

  1. Read a text message out loud without bringing the phone closer to their face.
  2. Find a contact by scrolling the contacts list and tap it.
  3. Read who is calling. Call their phone from yours and see whether they can read the name before picking up.
  4. Type a short reply. Check that the keyboard letters are big enough to hit.
  5. Hear the phone ring from another room or from across the room.

If anything fails, change only the setting for that task. For example, if they can read texts but miss the keys, turn up Display Zoom (iPhone) or Screen zoom (Samsung) instead of making the text even bigger.

Part 4: Common Problems and Fixes

  • "The words are cut off" or "There are dots (...) everything." The text size is too large for that app. Move the slider down one step.
  • "The screen suddenly got huge and I can't do anything." Zoom or Magnification probably got turned on. On iPhone, double-tap the screen with three fingers. On Samsung, open Settings > Accessibility and turn magnification off.
  • "An app is still tiny." Some apps don't follow the phone's text size. Check that app's own settings for a text size option, or use pinch-to-zoom where it's allowed, for example in web pages and photos.
  • "It went back to small." Changes usually don't undo themselves. Check whether someone else uses the phone, or whether a different phone or tablet is being used.
  • "It's still hard to see in sunlight." Turn the brightness up. On iPhone, swipe down from the top-right corner. On Samsung, swipe down from the top. Auto-brightness can lower it again, so leave it on or off based on what the person prefers.

Part 5: Fill-In Card (Copy This and Leave It by the Phone)

Write this down so the next helper, or you on your next visit, knows what was changed.

MY PHONE SETTINGS

  • Phone type: ______________________ (iPhone / Samsung model)
  • Text size: set to step ____ of ____
  • Bold text: ON / OFF
  • Display Zoom / Screen zoom: ______________________
  • Easy mode (Samsung): ON / OFF
  • Screen stays on for: ____ minutes
  • Ringtone name: ______________________
  • Magnifier: on Home Screen? YES / NO
  • Zoom / Magnification: ON / OFF (if ON, how to turn off: ______________________)
  • Set up by: ______________________ Date: ____________
  • Who to call for phone help: ______________________

Print the card in large type. At least 18 point in a plain font like Arial or Verdana, with black text on white paper, is a good starting point for many readers.

A Few Last Tips

  • Write steps down in their words. If they call it "the green phone button", write that rather than "Phone app".
  • Change one thing per visit if they're anxious. A phone where everything looks different can feel like a new phone.
  • Check again after big software updates. Your settings usually stay, but menus and layouts can move, and that confuses people even when the text size is fine.
