How to Write Emergency Sub Plans for Ag Class (No-Lab Checklist)

A step-by-step checklist and fill-in template for agriculture and FFA teachers who need sub plans a non-ag substitute can run safely. Includes a sample one-day plan on soil science that you can copy and adapt.

Being out sick is hard enough for any teacher. In an agriculture classroom it brings extra problems: there is a greenhouse, a shop, maybe animals, and a lot of equipment that a substitute shouldn't touch. Often you are the only ag teacher in the building, so nobody down the hall can step in.

This guide shows you how to build an emergency sub folder that any substitute can run safely with no ag background. Use the checklist, fill in the templates, and copy the sample day.

The Golden Rule: Plan for Zero Hands-On Work

Assume your substitute has never been in an ag room. Emergency plans should involve no tools, no animals, no chemicals, no greenhouse, no shop and no outdoor work. Students are often disappointed by that, but it's the right call. A sub who doesn't know your procedures can't supervise a shop safely, and your district's safety rules almost certainly require a trained adult for that work. Check your district and school policies to confirm what applies to you.

Good emergency plans are:

  • Paper-based or reading-based. They work even if the Wi-Fi or the projector is down.
  • Self-explanatory. Students can start without a mini-lesson.
  • Tied to your content. Ag students can tell when a day is just busywork.
  • Collectible. You can grade or check the work when you get back.

Step-by-Step: Build Your Ag Sub Folder

  1. Pick a physical spot. Use a clearly labeled binder or folder in a place you can describe over the phone, such as "red binder, top drawer of the desk by the door." Give a copy to the front office too.
  2. Write a one-page cover note. Use the template below. Keep it to one page, because subs skim.
  3. Write the safety rules out clearly. List every space and item that is off-limits.
  4. Prepare 3 to 5 stand-alone days. Each day should work in any order, at any point in the year, for any of your sections.
  5. Copy handouts in advance. Make a full class set for your largest section, plus a few extras, and label each set by day.
  6. Add rosters and seating charts. Print them, since a sub may not have a login for your attendance system.
  7. Name a go-to adult. Give the name of a nearby teacher or administrator who knows your room.
  8. Cover your animals and plants separately. Arrange with an administrator, a colleague, or approved FFA officers (whatever your school allows) for feeding, watering and greenhouse checks. This is not the substitute's job. Put it in writing.
  9. Review the folder each semester. Update rosters, refill copies and remove anything that no longer applies.

Fill-In Template: Sub Cover Note

Copy this onto one page and fill in the blanks.

Welcome! Thank you for covering [Teacher Name]'s Agriculture classes.

Room: [Room #] Office phone: [ext.] Go-to teacher nearby: [Name, Room #]

Today's plan is in: [location of folder/binder]

SAFETY — Please read first:

  • Students must NOT enter the shop, greenhouse, barn, animal area or storage rooms today, even if they say they "always do."
  • Do NOT allow use of any tools, equipment, chemicals or lab supplies.
  • No outdoor work or outdoor class today.
  • Students should not feed or handle animals. Animal care is covered by [Name].
  • If a student is injured or feels sick, contact [office/nurse ext.].

Bell schedule: [periods and times]

Class list by period:

  • Period [ ]: [Course name] – [# students] – Handout set [A/B/C]
  • Period [ ]: [Course name] – [# students] – Handout set [A/B/C]

Students who need support: [Initials or "see sealed list in folder"; follow your school's privacy rules]

Helpful students: [names of reliable students who can answer routine questions such as where the pencils are]

When students finish early: [extra activity, e.g., "word search in back pocket" or "silent reading"]

At the end of the day: Please collect all work in the tray labeled [ ] and fill out the log on the back.

Fill-In Template: Attendance and Behavior Log

Print this on the back of the cover note.

  • Period: ____ Absent: ______________________
  • Students who were especially helpful: ______________________
  • Problems or concerns: ______________________
  • Did students finish the work? All / Most / Some / Few
  • Anything I should know? ______________________
  • Substitute name (optional): ______________________

Fill-In Template: One-Period Day Plan

Write each day so a sub can read it aloud from top to bottom.

Day [#]: [Topic] — about [50] minutes

  1. (5 min) Attendance. Take roll using the seating chart.
  2. (2 min) Read aloud to class: "Today you'll work on [topic]. This is graded. Work [alone / with your table partner]. Turn it in at the end of class."
  3. (10 min) Warm-up. Hand out Sheet 1. Students answer [3–5] quick questions.
  4. (25 min) Main task. Hand out Sheet 2: a reading passage with questions.
  5. (8 min) Wrap-up. Hand out Sheet 3 as an exit ticket, then collect everything.

If finished early: [option]

Worked Example: Day 1, Soil Science Basics

Here is a complete sample day you can adapt. It needs only paper and pencils.

Warm-up (Sheet 1), "What Do You Already Know?"

  1. Name three things you think soil is made of.
  2. Why do farmers care about soil? Give two reasons.
  3. Have you ever seen soil that was very sandy, or very sticky? Describe it.

Main task (Sheet 2), short reading to write yourself:

Write a one-page passage covering these accurate basics:

  • Soil is a mix of mineral particles, organic matter, water and air.
  • The mineral part is grouped by particle size: sand (largest, feels gritty), silt (medium, feels smooth or floury) and clay (smallest, feels sticky when wet).
  • Soil texture is the proportion of sand, silt and clay. A soil with a balanced mix is called a loam.
  • Texture affects drainage and water holding. Sandy soils drain quickly but hold less water. Clay soils hold more water but can drain slowly and become compacted.
  • Organic matter (decayed plants and animals) helps soil hold water and nutrients and supports soil life.
  • Soil forms slowly through weathering of rock and the buildup of organic material, which is one reason soil conservation matters in agriculture.

Questions:

  1. List the four main components of soil.
  2. Put sand, silt and clay in order from largest to smallest particle.
  3. What is a loam?
  4. A farmer's field floods after every rain and stays muddy. Which particle is probably most common in that soil? Explain.
  5. Why might a farmer add compost or manure to a field?
  6. In your own words, why is soil conservation important?

Exit ticket (Sheet 3):

  • One thing I learned today: ______
  • One question I still have: ______
  • Circle one: Sand / Silt / Clay is the particle I'd want most in a garden, because ______

Grading tip: Score the main task for completion and accuracy. For the exit ticket, a quick check-mark is enough.

Ideas for More No-Lab Ag Sub Days

Build more days in the same Warm-up / Reading / Exit-ticket format:

  • Plant parts and photosynthesis in crops. Label a diagram, then match each part to its job.
  • Livestock species, breeds and by-products. Sort products by the animal they come from.
  • Farm-to-table supply chain. Trace one food from field to grocery store and list each step and job along the way.
  • Ag careers. Read short career profiles and fill out a "which career fits me" chart.
  • Ag vocabulary review. Do a crossword or matching sheet built from your current unit.

Final Checklist Before You Need It

  • [ ] Folder labeled and location shared with the office
  • [ ] One-page cover note with safety rules
  • [ ] Attendance and behavior log
  • [ ] 3–5 stand-alone day plans with timings
  • [ ] Class sets of handouts copied and labeled
  • [ ] Current rosters and seating charts
  • [ ] Go-to adult named
  • [ ] Animal and greenhouse care arranged separately, in writing
  • [ ] Early-finisher activity included
  • [ ] Reviewed this semester

Put this together on a calm afternoon and you won't have to write plans at midnight with a fever. Your sub will have a safe day and your students will keep learning.
