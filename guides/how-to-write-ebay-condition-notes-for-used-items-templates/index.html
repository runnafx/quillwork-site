How to Write eBay Condition Notes for Used Items (Templates)

A step-by-step way to fill in eBay's condition box honestly and clearly, with fill-in templates for each condition grade and a worked example. Built to cut down on "not as described" returns and repeat buyer questions.

When you list a used item, eBay gives you two separate places to describe its condition. One is the condition dropdown (New, Used, For parts or not working, and so on, depending on the category). The other is an optional condition description box that sits under it. Many sellers leave the box empty or type "see photos". That leaves buyers guessing, and buyers who guess are the ones who send questions, lowball, or open "not as described" returns.

This guide shows you what to put in that box, in what order and with what wording. It also gives you templates to copy.

First, know the two fields

  • The condition dropdown is the structured field. eBay search filters (for example "Used" or "For parts") read it. eBay's developer documentation says listings show up in condition searches based on the structured condition, not on what you write in the description. So always set the dropdown correctly. Never pick a better grade and "explain" it in the text.
  • The condition description box is free text. Sellers widely report a limit of about 1,000 characters, and the box shows a counter as you type. On mobile, some sellers say it shows up near the top as "Seller notes" with a "Read more" link. That makes it one of the first things a phone shopper reads.
  • Your main description is a separate field. You can repeat the key condition facts there. The condition box is the short, scannable version.

The 5-part condition note formula

Write the note in this order. Every part is one short sentence or a short list.

  1. Overall grade in plain words. "Used, good working condition with light wear."
  2. What you tested and how. "Powers on, all buttons tested, charges from the included cable."
  3. Every flaw, with location and size. "2 cm scratch on the back lower-left; small scuff on the top edge."
  4. What is included and what is not. "Includes unit and cable. No box, no manual, no charger brick."
  5. A pointer to the photos. "Flaws are shown in photos 6-8."

That's it. Leave out sales talk, words like "mint" unless it truly is, and "as is, no returns" warnings in this box. Put your return policy in the return settings, where it actually applies.

Rules that prevent most condition disputes

  • Describe flaws with location + size + type. "Scratch on screen" is vague. "Hairline scratch, about 1 cm, top-right of screen, not visible when on" is checkable.
  • Use measurable words, not mood words. Write "light", "moderate" or "heavy" and add a size. Avoid "great", "amazing" and "barely used".
  • Say what you did NOT test. "Not tested with a disc" or "battery life not measured" is honest. It also protects you more than staying silent does.
  • Match the photos to the text. Every flaw you mention should appear in a close-up photo, and every flaw in a photo should appear in the text.
  • Mention smells, pets and smoke if they apply. Buyers return items over these, and you can't show them in a photo. If your home is smoke-free and pet-free, you can say so. If it isn't, say that too.
  • Mention missing parts in the note, not just the description. Missing battery covers, lids, cords, cases, pieces and inserts are common "not as described" triggers.
  • Never guess at authenticity or age. Write "tag reads...", "marked..." or "model number on label: ..." and let the buyer decide.

Fill-in templates by condition

Copy the one that fits, fill in the brackets and delete anything that doesn't apply. Keep the final text under the character counter.

Template A: Used, excellent / like new

Used, excellent condition. [Item] has been [used briefly / opened but unused]. Tested: [what you checked, e.g. powers on, all functions work]. Cosmetic: no visible scratches, marks or wear found under bright light, except [any tiny note or "none"]. Includes: [list]. Not included: [list or "nothing else"]. All angles shown in photos 1-[x].

Template B: Used, good (normal wear)

Used, good working condition with normal wear. Tested: [what you checked and how]. Wear: [flaw 1: type, size, location]; [flaw 2: type, size, location]. These are shown in photos [x-y]. Works fully as far as tested; [anything not tested]. Includes: [list]. Not included: [list].

Template C: Used, fair (heavy wear, works)

Used, fair condition. Works, but shows heavy wear. Tested: [what you checked]. Wear: [list each significant flaw with location and size]. Functional notes: [e.g. battery holds a short charge, one button is stiff but works]. Includes: [list]. Not included: [list]. Please review all photos; flaws in photos [x-y].

Template D: For parts or not working

Sold for parts or repair. Not working as described below. What happens: [e.g. does not power on with a known-good charger / powers on but no display]. What I checked: [steps you tried]. Not tested: [anything]. Physical condition: [cracks, missing parts, corrosion: location and size]. Includes only what is pictured.

Use the "For parts or not working" dropdown for this. Don't list a non-working item as Used.

Template E: Clothing and textiles

Used, [good] condition. Flaws: [stain/hole/fade/pilling] at [location], about [size]. Tag: [size on tag, brand, any care or style code shown]. Measurements (laid flat): [pit to pit], [length], [sleeve or inseam]. [Smoke-free / pet-free home, if true]. Flaws shown in photos [x-y].

For clothing, measurements matter more than tag size. Put them in both the condition note and the main description.

Worked example: a used handheld game console

Here is how the formula turns rough notes into a finished condition note.

The seller's rough notes: "works fine, some scratches on back, screen ok, no charger, has case."

What the seller checked before writing: They turned it on, tested every button in a game, charged it with their own USB-C cable, looked at the screen with the backlight off under a lamp, and photographed the back.

Finished condition note:

Used, good working condition with normal wear. Tested: powers on, charges via USB-C, all buttons, sticks and triggers respond in-game, Wi-Fi connects. Screen: no scratches seen with the screen off under bright light. Wear: several light surface scratches across the back (largest about 3 cm), and a small scuff on the lower-left corner. See photos 5-7. Battery life not measured. Includes: console and a soft carry case. Not included: charger, cable, box, games. Factory reset completed.

That's about 470 characters, well under the limit, and it answers the questions buyers usually send: does it work, how bad are the scratches, what's in the box, and is it reset.

Pre-publish checklist

Run through this before you click List.

  • [ ] Condition dropdown matches reality (don't upgrade it)
  • [ ] Note starts with a plain overall grade
  • [ ] Note says what you tested, and how
  • [ ] Note says what you did not test
  • [ ] Every flaw has a type, location and approximate size
  • [ ] Every flaw in the text has a close-up photo, and every flaw in a photo is in the text
  • [ ] Included and not-included items are both listed
  • [ ] Smoke/pet status mentioned if relevant (and true)
  • [ ] For electronics: reset or account-removal status stated
  • [ ] For clothing: flat measurements included
  • [ ] Note is under the character counter and has no sales talk
  • [ ] Key condition facts are repeated in the main description

Common mistakes to avoid

  • "See photos" as the whole note. Photos back up your words. They don't replace them, especially for buyers on a small phone screen.
  • "Untested" when you could test it. Buyers often read "untested" as "doesn't work" and price it that way. If you can plug it in, do.
  • Hiding a flaw in photo 12. If it matters to the buyer, put it in the text and in an early photo.
  • Mixing up condition and returns. "No returns" in a condition note doesn't change your return settings. It also doesn't override eBay's protections for items that aren't as described.
  • Copying a note from another listing. Each unit has its own flaws. Write the note for the item in front of you.

A clear condition note takes about five minutes once you have the item in hand and a flashlight. It's one of the cheapest ways to get fewer questions, fewer lowball offers and fewer returns.
