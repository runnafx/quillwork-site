Airbnb Winter Guest Messages: 6 Copy-Paste Templates for Hosts

Six fill-in winter messages for Airbnb and VRBO hosts covering snow, driving, heating, frozen pipes and checkout. It also includes a checklist of what to send and when, and notes on keeping messages within Airbnb's house-rules policy.

Winter stays bring questions that summer guests never ask. Does the driveway get plowed? Can the car make it up the hill? Why is the bedroom cold? What do I do if the water stops? If you answer these before guests ask, your inbox stays quieter and guests are less likely to get stuck or do something that harms the house.

This page gives you six winter messages with blanks to fill in. It also says when to send each one and covers one rule you need to know before sending any of them.

First: messages are for information, not new rules

For Airbnb reservations booked on or after November 20, 2026, Airbnb's house-rules policy says rules must appear in the house-rules section of your listing when the guest books. You can't add new rules later by message, by a sign in the home or in person. Hosts generally can't charge fines, and they can't ask guests to do significant chores.

For winter messages, that means:

  • If something is a requirement, put it in your listing's house rules now. An example is "keep the thermostat at 55°F or above." The message can then remind guests of it.
  • Anything else should be written as information or a polite request. For example: "Please leave the cabinet doors open on very cold nights. It helps protect the pipes."
  • Don't threaten fines, and don't make shoveling or de-icing a guest's job. Say who handles snow removal and what you've provided if guests want to clear a path themselves.

Airbnb may change its policy, so read the current help article on house rules before you rely on this. This is a practical writing guide, not legal advice. VRBO and local rules are separate, so check them too.

Before you write: collect these winter facts

Fill this in once. Every message below draws from it.

  • Who clears snow, the trigger depth (e.g. "after 2 inches") and roughly how long it takes after snow stops
  • Where guests should park so the plow can get through
  • Whether the road or driveway is steep, and whether you recommend AWD/4WD, snow tires or chains
  • Where the shovel, ice melt and spare blankets are
  • Heating type (furnace, heat pump, baseboard, wood stove, fireplace) and the thermostat setting you recommend
  • Where the main water shutoff is, and what to do if the water stops
  • Where the carbon monoxide and smoke detectors are
  • Who to call for heat or water problems, and when it's OK to call at night
  • What to do if a road closure stops guests leaving on checkout day

If a blank applies to your property and you can't fill it in, find out the answer before winter starts.

When to send each message

  1. Booking confirmation (winter dates): Message 1, driving and arrival expectations
  2. 3 to 5 days before arrival: Message 2, winter arrival details
  3. Check-in day: Message 3, heating and staying warm
  4. When a hard freeze is forecast: Message 4, protecting the pipes
  5. When a storm is forecast during the stay: Message 5, storm heads-up
  6. The evening before checkout: Message 6, winter checkout

You can schedule Messages 1, 2, 3 and 6 in your messaging tool. Send Messages 4 and 5 yourself when the forecast calls for them.

Message 1: Booking confirmation, driving and arrival

> Hi [Guest name], thanks for booking [Property name] for [dates]! Since you're visiting in winter, here's a heads-up on getting here. > > The last [distance] to the house is [describe: a steep gravel road / a county road that's plowed first / a shared private drive]. In snow, we recommend [AWD or 4WD / snow tires / carrying chains]. [If relevant: Some phone map apps route guests via [road name], which isn't maintained in winter, so please use [route] instead.] > > [Name or company] clears the driveway after about [X] inches of snow, usually within [time frame] after it stops. We'll send full arrival details a few days before you come. Questions anytime!

Message 2: Winter arrival details (3 to 5 days out)

> Hi [Guest name], we're looking forward to having you on [day]! A few winter notes for arrival: > > - Parking: Please park [exact spot] so the plow can reach the driveway. > - Entry: The [door/lock] is at [location]. [If the steps can ice over: We salt the steps before arrival, but please take care, as ice can form after sunset.] > - Snow gear: A shovel and ice melt are [location] if you'd like to clear a path. Snow removal is otherwise handled by [name]. > - Weather: Check the forecast for [area] before you set out. If conditions look bad on your travel day, message us and we'll talk through options. > > Your door code is [code] and is active from [time].

Message 3: Check-in day, heating and staying warm

> Welcome, [Guest name]! A quick guide to keeping warm: > > - Thermostat: It's in [location]. [Explain anything unusual, e.g. "It's a heat pump, so it warms the house gradually. Turning it way up won't heat things faster."] A comfortable setting is about [X]°F. > - Leaving the house: [If this is in your listing's house rules: "As listed in our house rules, please keep the heat at [X]°F or higher even when you're out."] [If not: "We'd appreciate it if you leave the heat at [X]°F or higher when you go out. It protects the pipes."] > - Fireplace / wood stove: [How to use it, where the wood is, damper open or closed. Or: "The fireplace is decorative only and isn't for use."] > - Extra blankets: [location] > - Safety: Carbon monoxide and smoke detectors are in [locations]. If an alarm goes off, get everyone outside first, then call [emergency number] and us. > > If any room stays cold or the heat seems off, message or call [number]. Don't wait until morning.

Message 4: Hard freeze forecast, protecting the pipes

> Hi [Guest name], tonight's forecast is around [temperature], which is cold enough to freeze pipes. A couple of small things would really help: > > - Please keep the heat at [X]°F or higher overnight. > - Leave the cabinet doors open under the [kitchen/bathroom] sink so warm air reaches the pipes. > - [If you want faucets dripping: Let the [faucet] drip slowly overnight.] > > If the water slows or stops, please don't use a heat gun or open flame on the pipes. Message or call us at [number] right away. The main water shutoff is at [location] in case you ever see water leaking. Thanks so much!

These are worded as requests. If you need guests to follow any of them, add them to your listing's house rules before guests book.

Message 5: Storm forecast during the stay

> Hi [Guest name], a storm is forecast for [day/time], with about [amount] of snow expected. Here's what to expect: > > - [Name] will plow once snowfall reaches about [X] inches, usually within [time frame] after it stops. > - Please move your car to [spot] by [time] so the plow can get in. > - If the power goes out: flashlights are [location], [backup heat source, if any, and how to use it safely]. Never run a grill or generator indoors. > - Local road conditions: [where to check, e.g. the state DOT road report or the county's road line]. > > If roads are closed on your checkout day, message us before [time] and we'll work out a plan together.

Message 6: Winter checkout

> Hi [Guest name], we hope you had a cozy stay! A few notes for tomorrow's [time] checkout: > > - Please leave the thermostat at [X]°F. Please don't turn it off. > - [Close the fireplace glass doors / leave the wood stove to burn out. Never take ashes out of the house.] > - Lock the door by [method]. > > If snow or ice makes leaving unsafe, message us before checkout and we'll sort something out. Safe travels!

The checkout list is short on purpose. Airbnb's policy doesn't allow significant chores, so keep checkout steps to things like locking up and leaving the heat on.

Checklist: before you turn on your winter messages

  • [ ] Any winter requirement (minimum thermostat, no wood stove use, parking spot) is in the listing's house rules, not only in messages
  • [ ] No message mentions fines, penalties or guest chores beyond simple courtesy requests
  • [ ] Every [blank] is filled in. Search your text for "[" before sending
  • [ ] Snow removal contact and timing are confirmed with the person who does it
  • [ ] The main water shutoff is labeled and easy for a guest to find
  • [ ] Carbon monoxide and smoke detector locations are correct and the detectors have been tested
  • [ ] Your phone number is in Messages 3, 4 and 5
  • [ ] A friend who has never visited the property read Message 2 and understood how to get in and where to park
  • [ ] Your printed guidebook in the home matches what the messages say

Keep it consistent

Guests get confused when your messages, listing and printed guidebook don't agree. Once your winter facts are settled, use the same thermostat number, parking spot and contact number everywhere. Check them again at the start of each winter, because plow contractors and phone numbers change.
