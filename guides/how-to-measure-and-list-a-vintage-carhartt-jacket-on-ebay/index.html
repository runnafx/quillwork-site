How to Measure and List a Vintage Carhartt Jacket on eBay

A step-by-step guide to measuring a used Carhartt work jacket, reading its tags, describing wear honestly and writing a title buyers search for. Includes a fill-in listing template and a worked example.

Vintage Carhartt jackets sell to buyers who know exactly what they want. They search by model, color and size, and then they check measurements because old tag sizes don't match modern fit. A listing with no measurements, no tag photo and a vague title like "Carhartt coat used" tends to sit or sell cheaply. The same jacket, measured and described properly, competes with the best listings in its search.

This page covers the whole process for one jacket: what to check, how to measure, how to word the condition, and a fill-in title and description you can copy.

What You Need

  • A flat, hard surface such as a table or a clean floor
  • A soft tape measure in inches (a metal tape works but is awkward)
  • A phone camera and a plain background (a sheet or a wall)
  • Daylight or a bright white lamp, because duck canvas color looks very different under yellow light
  • A notepad, or the template at the bottom of this page

Step 1: Read and Photograph Every Tag

Before you measure, collect what the jacket tells you about itself. Look in three places:

  1. The neck tag. Note the brand label style, the size, and whether it says "Made in USA" or names another country.
  2. The care or content tag, usually in a side seam or inside pocket. It often gives the style number (for example a code starting with J), the fabric content and sometimes a color code.
  3. Any extra labels, such as union labels, "Blanket Lined" or "Quilt Lined" text, or a size tag that says "Tall" or "Regular".

Copy the style number exactly as printed. Don't guess a model name from memory. If the tag is faded, cut out or missing, say so in the listing. Buyers will forgive a missing tag, but they won't forgive a model name you guessed wrong.

Take a sharp, close photo of each tag. On eBay the tag photo is often the second thing a serious buyer looks at, right after the front view.

Step 2: Identify the Style From Its Features

If the tag doesn't give a style number, describe what you can see instead of naming a model. Check for:

  • Collar: corduroy collar or a plain canvas collar
  • Lining: blanket lining (striped or plain wool-blend), quilted lining, sherpa, or unlined
  • Closure: zipper, snaps or buttons, and whether the cuffs have snaps or knit
  • Pockets: chest pockets, hand-warmer pockets, inside pockets
  • Length: waist-length with a waistband (the Detroit style) or hip/thigh length (chore coat and coat styles)

A waist-length duck jacket with a corduroy collar and a waistband is the shape most buyers mean when they search "Detroit jacket". If yours has those features but no readable tag, a safe title phrase is "Detroit Style". Then let the photos and description back it up.

Lining matters for price. Lined and unlined versions of similar jackets can sell at quite different prices, so check sold listings for your exact lining type before you set a price.

Step 3: Measure Flat, the Same Way Every Time

Lay the jacket face up, zipped or snapped closed, sleeves out to the sides. Smooth it with your hands but don't stretch it. Measure in inches and round to the nearest half inch.

  1. Pit to pit (chest): straight across the front, from the bottom of one armpit seam to the other. This is the number most buyers use first.
  2. Length: from the top of the shoulder where it meets the collar (or the center back below the collar) straight down to the bottom hem. Say which method you used.
  3. Sleeve length: from the shoulder seam down the outside of the sleeve to the end of the cuff.
  4. Shoulder width: straight across the back from one shoulder seam to the other.
  5. Waistband / hem width: straight across the bottom, laid flat. This is useful for waist-length styles because the waistband shapes the fit.

Write down flat measurements and don't double them. Label them clearly, for example "Pit to pit: 24 in (laid flat)". Many buyers double the number themselves, and doubling it for them causes confusion.

Take a photo of the tape across the chest. It shows you measured, and it answers the most common buyer question before anyone asks.

Step 4: Inspect and Name the Wear Honestly

Work in good light from top to bottom, then inside. Check:

  • Collar: corduroy worn bald at the fold, fraying or holes
  • Cuffs and hem: fraying, frayed-through edges, sun fading
  • Zipper: does it run smoothly the whole way, does the pull stay locked, any missing teeth
  • Snaps and buttons: all present, all closing
  • Pockets: torn corners, holes in pocket bags
  • Seams: open seams under the arms and at the shoulders
  • Lining: tears, pilling, stains, separated seams
  • Stains: paint, oil, grease. Note size and location
  • Repairs: patches or restitching, and whether they look professional or homemade
  • Odor: smoke, mildew, storage smell

Use plain words and locations, for example: "Small paint spot on left sleeve, about the size of a dime. Fraying on both cuffs. Collar corduroy worn at the fold. Zipper works smoothly." Photograph every flaw you mention.

Workwear buyers often want honest wear. Fading and fraying can be a selling point for some of them. What causes returns and bad feedback is wear they didn't know about.

Step 5: Clean Carefully, or Not at All

If the jacket is dirty, decide if you can improve it without risk:

  • Brush off loose dirt outside with a stiff brush once the jacket is dry.
  • Spot-clean marks with mild soap and cold water, and test on a hidden area first.
  • Air it out for a day or two if it smells stale.
  • If you machine-wash, use cold water and a gentle cycle, then hang dry. Heat can shrink duck canvas and damage old linings.

If you're unsure, list it as-is and say so ("Not washed. Sold as found."). Some buyers prefer to clean it themselves. Always measure after cleaning, not before.

Step 6: Photograph in a Set Order

Buyers scroll fast, so give them the shots in the order they want them:

  1. Front, laid flat or on a hanger, full jacket in frame
  2. Back
  3. Neck tag close-up
  4. Care/style tag close-up
  5. Collar close-up
  6. Lining (open the front)
  7. Tape across the chest (pit to pit)
  8. Tape for length
  9. Each flaw, one photo per flaw
  10. Cuffs and hem

Use the same background for every shot and don't use filters. If the color looks off on screen, retake it in daylight. Color is part of what buyers search for.

Step 7: Write a Model-First Title

eBay titles allow up to 80 characters. Put the words buyers type first, then the details that narrow the search.

Title formula: Vintage Carhartt [Style or Model] Jacket [Style No. if known] [Color] [Lining] [Made in USA if true] Men's [Size]

Rules of thumb:

  • Put the exact style number in the title only if it's on the tag.
  • Write the size the way the tag shows it, and add "Fits like" only in the description, supported by measurements.
  • Don't use words like "rare" or "grail" unless you can back them up. They take up characters and don't help search.
  • Add a flaw word (such as "Distressed" or "Faded") if the wear is obvious. It sets expectations and matches what some buyers search for.

Fill-In Description Template

Copy this and fill in the brackets:

Vintage Carhartt [style / "Detroit Style"] Jacket, [color], [lining type]

Tag size: [size as printed] Style number: [as printed / "tag faded, not readable"] Made in: [country as printed / "not shown"]

Measurements (laid flat, inches):

  • Pit to pit: [ ]
  • Length (top of shoulder at collar to hem): [ ]
  • Sleeve (shoulder seam to cuff): [ ]
  • Shoulders (seam to seam): [ ]
  • Hem / waistband across: [ ]

Condition: [Overall: e.g., well-worn with honest fading]. [List each flaw with location.] [Zipper/snap function.] [Lining condition.] [Odor: none noticed / describe.]

Cleaning: [Washed cold and hung dry / spot-cleaned / not washed, sold as found.]

Please compare the measurements to a jacket you own, because vintage sizing runs differently from current sizing. All flaws I found are listed and photographed.

Worked Example

Here's a fictional jacket used only to show the format. The measurements are made up and are not a size chart.

Title: Vintage Carhartt Detroit Style Jacket Tan Duck Unlined Distressed Men's M

Description: Vintage Carhartt Detroit style work jacket in tan duck canvas, unlined.

Tag size: M. Style number: tag faded, not readable. Made in: tag not readable.

Measurements (laid flat, inches): pit to pit 23, length 25, sleeve 24, shoulders 19.5, waistband 20.

Condition: Well-worn with even fading across the body. Corduroy collar worn bald at the fold. Fraying on both cuffs and along the bottom edge of the right hand pocket. Small dark stain on the lower back, about one inch across. Zipper runs smoothly and holds. All seams closed. No odor noticed.

Cleaning: Brushed and washed cold, hung dry.

Before You Click List

  • Did you photograph every tag and every flaw you mention?
  • Did you label all measurements as flat, in inches?
  • Does the title use only facts you can see on the jacket or its tags?
  • Did you check recent sold listings (Sold + Used filters, your size and lining) before setting a price?
  • Did you weigh the jacket after packing it? Heavy canvas jackets can push you into a higher shipping tier.

If all five are yes, the listing is ready.
