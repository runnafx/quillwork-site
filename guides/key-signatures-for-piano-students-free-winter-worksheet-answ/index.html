Key Signatures for Piano Students: Free Winter Worksheet + Answers

A no-prep winter worksheet that teaches Level 2 piano students to read and write the key signatures for C, G, D, F and B-flat major. It includes two memory tricks, five short activities and a full answer key.

Students around Level 2 often learn to play in G major and F major before they understand why the sharp or flat is printed at the start of every line. The result is a lot of F naturals where an F-sharp should be. This page gives you a short, secular winter worksheet that fixes this in one or two lessons. You can read it aloud, copy it onto staff paper or print it as is.

It covers the five keys most beginner method books use first: C, G, D, F and B-flat major. The answer key is at the bottom.

What students need to know first

Check these before you start. If a student is unsure of any of them, review that first.

  • The names of the white keys, and the fact that a sharp is the next key up and a flat is the next key down
  • Whole steps and half steps (tones and semitones)
  • That a major scale follows the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half

The one big idea

A key signature is a shortcut. It tells you, "Every time you see this note, in any octave, play it sharp (or flat), for the whole piece." That holds until the key signature changes or an accidental (a sharp, flat or natural written next to a single note) says otherwise for one bar.

Say it in a way that sticks: the key signature is like a winter coat. You put it on at the start of the line and it stays on for the whole line.

Two memory tricks

Order of sharps: F C G D A E B A winter version students like: "Frosty Cats Go Down And Eat Biscuits."

Order of flats: B E A D G C F This is the order of sharps backwards. Many teachers use "BEAD, Greatest Common Factor", or let students make up their own winter sentence.

Finding the key from sharps: go up a half step from the last sharp.

  • One sharp (F-sharp): up a half step is G, so the key is G major.
  • Two sharps (F-sharp, C-sharp): up a half step from C-sharp is D, so the key is D major.

Finding the key from flats: the second-to-last flat is the name of the key.

  • Two flats (B-flat, E-flat): the second-to-last flat is B-flat, so the key is B-flat major.
  • One flat is the exception. One flat (B-flat) = F major. Students just memorize it.

No sharps or flats = C major.

At this level, treat every key signature as a major key. Relative minors can come later.

Where the sharps and flats sit on the staff

If your students will draw key signatures, show them these positions:

  • Treble clef, sharps: F-sharp on the top line, C-sharp in the third space
  • Bass clef, sharps: F-sharp on the fourth line, C-sharp in the second space
  • Treble clef, flats: B-flat on the middle line, E-flat in the top space
  • Bass clef, flats: B-flat on the second line, E-flat in the third space

Key signatures always come right after the clef and before the time signature, and they are repeated at the start of every line.

The Worksheet: Snow Day Key Signatures

Copy the parts below onto staff paper, or use them as spoken questions during the lesson. Each part takes about 3 to 5 minutes.

Part 1: Snowman Name Tags (name the key)

Each snowman is wearing a key signature. Write the name of the major key on his name tag.

  1. No sharps or flats
  2. One sharp: F-sharp
  3. One flat: B-flat
  4. Two sharps: F-sharp, C-sharp
  5. Two flats: B-flat, E-flat
  6. Bonus: Three sharps: F-sharp, C-sharp, G-sharp

Part 2: Pack the Mittens (write the key signature)

Write the sharps or flats each key needs, in the correct order. If you have staff paper, draw them on both a treble and a bass staff.

  1. G major
  2. F major
  3. D major
  4. B-flat major
  5. C major

Part 3: Fix the Gingerbread Scales

A gingerbread baker wrote out these scales but forgot the key signatures. Circle each wrong note and write the correct one.

  1. G major: G A B C D E F G
  2. F major: F G A B C D E F
  3. D major: D E F G A B C D
  4. B-flat major: B-flat C D E F G A B-flat

Part 4: Gift Tag Detective (which key is it?)

Each gift tag lists the notes in a short melody and the note it ends on. Use the sharps or flats to name the key.

  1. Notes used: G, A, B, C, D, F-sharp. Ends on G.
  2. Notes used: F, G, A, B-flat, C. Ends on F.
  3. Notes used: D, E, F-sharp, G, A, C-sharp. Ends on D.
  4. Notes used: B-flat, C, D, E-flat, F. Ends on B-flat.

Part 5: Sharp or Natural? (reading in a key)

The piece is in the key given. Write what the student should actually play for each note: sharp, flat or natural.

  1. Key of G major. The note on the staff is F (written with no sign, in a high octave).
  2. Key of F major. The note on the staff is B (written with no sign).
  3. Key of D major. The note on the staff is C (written with no sign).
  4. Key of D major. The note on the staff is G (written with no sign).
  5. Key of G major. The note on the staff is F with a natural sign in front of it.

Part 3

  2. F major: the B should be B-flat (F G A B-flat C D E F)
  3. D major: the F should be F-sharp and the C should be C-sharp (D E F-sharp G A B C-sharp D)

Check Part 3 on the keyboard using the whole-whole-half-whole-whole-whole-half pattern. Students can then hear why each scale needs its sharps or flats.

Part 4

  1. G major (one sharp, F-sharp, and it ends on G)
  2. F major (one flat, B-flat, and it ends on F)
  3. D major (F-sharp and C-sharp, and it ends on D)
  4. B-flat major (B-flat and E-flat, and it ends on B-flat)

Part 5

  1. F-sharp. The key signature applies in every octave, not just on the line where the sharp is printed.
  2. B-flat
  3. C-sharp
  4. G natural. D major has no G-sharp.
  5. F natural. The natural sign cancels the key signature for that note until the end of the bar.

Teaching Tips

  • Start at the keyboard, not the page. Have the student build G major from G using whole and half steps. When they get to F-sharp, they find out for themselves why the key needs it. Then show them the key signature as the shortcut that saves writing it every time.
  • Do the "every octave" check out loud. Question 1 in Part 5 is the one students most often get wrong. Before they play a new piece, ask: "Which notes are wearing a coat in this piece? Show me every one on the keyboard."
  • Highlight before playing. For the first few pieces in a new key, let the student lightly circle every F (or B) in pencil before playing. Stop once they no longer need it.
  • Keep it short. One part per lesson as a warm-up works better than all five parts in one sitting.
  • UK students: key signatures work the same way. Only the rhythm names differ (crotchet, minim), and this worksheet doesn't use them, so you can use it as is.

Where this fits in a December plan

This worksheet works well in week 3 or 4 of December lessons. By then most Level 2 students are polishing a recital or holiday piece in G or F major, and they can find the key signature in that piece right after finishing the worksheet. Asking "What key is your recital piece in, and how do you know?" is a quick check that the lesson worked.
