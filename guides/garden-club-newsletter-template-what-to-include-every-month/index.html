Garden Club Newsletter Template: What to Include Every Month

A fill-in-the-blanks template for volunteer garden club newsletter editors, with a checklist and a worked example that turns rough meeting notes into a finished issue.

Garden Club Newsletter Template: What to Include Every Month

You said yes to being newsletter editor, or nobody else did, and now an issue is due. This page gives you a structure you can reuse every month, fill-in templates for each section, and a worked example that shows how a pile of messy notes becomes a finished newsletter.

You don't need to be a writer. You need a repeatable layout and a short list of questions to ask your officers before each issue.

The core sections (in this order)

Most garden club newsletters work well with five to seven short sections. Readers skim, so put the most time-sensitive information first.

  1. Header: club name, month and year, issue number if you use one
  2. Next meeting: date, time, place, program and speaker
  3. President's note: a short, friendly paragraph (optional but popular)
  4. Upcoming events: plant sales, garden tours, workdays, deadlines
  5. Member news: welcomes, thank-yous, get-well wishes, milestones
  6. Seasonal garden tasks: what to do in the garden this month in your region
  7. Club business and contacts: dues reminders, committee sign-ups, who to contact

If space is tight, cut the president's note and shorten the garden tasks. Keep the meeting details and events.

Before you write: the 10-minute information checklist

Send this list to your president or secretary a week before your deadline. Most editing headaches come from missing details, not bad writing.

  • Next meeting date, start time and exact location (room name, parking notes)
  • Program title and speaker name, spelled correctly
  • Whether refreshments are assigned, and to whom
  • Events coming up in the next two months, with dates and contact person
  • New members to welcome (and whether they agreed to be named)
  • Anyone to thank: hosts, volunteers, donors
  • Get-well or sympathy notes the member wants shared
  • Dues, ballot, or sign-up deadlines
  • Any correction to last month's issue
  • Photos, and permission to use them

Tip: Never guess a date, time or name. If something is unconfirmed, put it in brackets, like [time TBC], and fix it before sending. A wrong meeting time causes more trouble than a missing one.

Fill-in templates for each section

Copy these and replace the bracketed parts.

Header

[Club Name] Newsletter [Month Year] | Issue [number]

Next meeting

Next Meeting: [Day, Month Date] at [Time] Location: [Venue name, room, address] Program: "[Program title]" with [Speaker name], [one line about who they are]. Refreshments: [Names], thank you! [Optional: Guests welcome. Parking is available at [location].]

President's note

Hello, gardeners! [One sentence about the season or recent weather.] [One sentence about a recent club success or activity.] [One sentence pointing to the most important upcoming item.] See you on [date]. [Name], President

Upcoming events

[Event name]: [Date], [time], [location] [One or two sentences on what it is and what members should do: volunteer, bring plants, RSVP.] Contact: [Name, phone or email].

Repeat for each event. List them by date, soonest first.

Member news

Welcome to our newest members: [Name] and [Name]. Say hello at the next meeting! Thank you to [Name] for [specific contribution]. Get well soon to [Name]. [Only include if the member or family has agreed.]

Seasonal garden tasks

In the Garden This [Month]

  • [Task 1: planting, sowing or dividing]
  • [Task 2: maintenance, such as pruning, mulching or watering]
  • [Task 3: pest or disease watch]
  • [Task 4: planning or ordering for next season]

Keep it to four to six bullets. Base timing on your own region and frost dates, not generic national advice. If your club has a local expert or extension office guidance you trust, lean on that.

Club business and contacts

Dues reminder: [Amount] due by [date]. Pay to [Name] or [method]. Volunteers needed: [Committee or task], contact [Name]. Newsletter submissions for next month are due [date] to [editor name and contact].

Worked example: from raw notes to finished sections

Here is what an officer might actually send an editor. This is an invented example for illustration.

The raw notes:

> mtg is 2nd tues as usual, 7pm, church hall. Linda doing talk on dividing perennials. plant sale may 17 need potters, Bev coordinating. 2 new ppl - Tom Reyes and Anita (last name?). thank Marge for hosting board mtg. dues $25 by end of april. Carl had knee surgery, doing ok, says hi

Step 1: Pull out the facts. Meeting: second Tuesday, 7 p.m., church hall, Linda, perennial division. Event: plant sale May 17, potters needed, Bev. Members: Tom Reyes, Anita (surname missing). Thanks: Marge. Dues: $25, end of April. Carl: knee surgery, recovering.

Step 2: Flag what's missing. The actual meeting date, Linda's last name, the church name, Anita's last name, Bev's contact details, and whether Carl is comfortable being mentioned.

Step 3: Write it up.

> Next Meeting: Tuesday, [April date] at 7:00 p.m. > Location: [Church name] Fellowship Hall > Program: "Dividing Perennials" with [Linda's last name]. Bring your questions about crowded clumps! > > Spring Plant Sale: Saturday, May 17 > Our plant sale is coming up, and we need volunteers to help pot up divisions beforehand. Contact Bev [last name, phone/email] to sign up. > > Welcome to new members Tom Reyes and Anita [last name]! > Thank you to Marge for hosting our board meeting. > Get well wishes to Carl, who is recovering from knee surgery and sends his hello. [Confirm Carl is happy to be mentioned.] > > Dues reminder: Annual dues of $25 are due by April 30.

Notice that nothing was invented. Every gap is in brackets, so a quick reply from the president fixes everything before it goes out.

Formatting tips that save time

  • Use one template file. Duplicate last month's issue and update it, rather than starting fresh.
  • Bold the dates. Readers scan for "when."
  • Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences. Many members read on phones.
  • Make two versions: a print or PDF version for members who want paper, and a plain-text version pasted into the email body. Some members won't open attachments.
  • Put the next submission deadline at the bottom of every issue so material comes to you.
  • Proofread names twice. Misspelled names are the error members notice most.

A simple monthly editor timeline

  1. Two weeks before sending: Email the information checklist to officers.
  2. One week before: Collect replies; draft using your template.
  3. Five days before: Send the draft with bracketed questions to the president.
  4. Three days before: Make corrections, add photos, final proofread.
  5. Send day: Email the plain-text version, attach the formatted file, print copies if needed.
  6. After sending: Save a copy in your archive and note anything to carry into next month.

Ideas when news is thin

Some months are quiet. Fill space with something useful rather than padding:

  • A member's favorite plant and why (ask one member for three sentences)
  • A short recap of last month's program
  • A "what's blooming now" list from members' gardens
  • A reminder of club history or a founding date, if you have it on record
  • A photo from a past event with a caption

Quick checklist before you hit send

  • Meeting date, time and place are confirmed and correct
  • Every name is spelled correctly
  • No brackets or placeholders left in the text
  • Event contacts are included
  • Personal news has the member's permission
  • Next submission deadline is listed
  • Plain-text version reads cleanly in an email

Save this page and reuse the templates each month. After two or three issues, the routine gets much faster.
