How to Test a Used New 3DS XL Before Reselling It (Checklist)

A 20-minute bench test for a used New Nintendo 3DS XL: check the model and region, test every input, camera, speaker and screen, spot modded units, then write the condition notes for your listing.

If you bought a New Nintendo 3DS XL at an estate auction, a thrift store or in a lot, the selling price depends on two words: "tested, working." Buyers who search by exact model expect you to have checked everything. If you miss a dead camera or a drifting Circle Pad, you get a return.

This checklist takes about 20 minutes. You need a charger, a game cartridge, a Wi-Fi network and a phone to take photos. Work through it in order and write notes as you go, because those notes become your listing description.

Step 1: Confirm it's really a "New" 3DS XL

Sellers and auctioneers often mix up the models. The "New" model sells for more than the original 3DS XL, so check before you title anything.

  • Model number: look on the back or under the battery cover area. New 3DS XL is RED-001. Original 3DS XL is SPR-001. New 2DS XL is JAN-001. Original 3DS is CTR-001. The small New 3DS is KTR-001.
  • C-Stick: the New 3DS XL has a small rubbery nub above the A/B/X/Y buttons. The original XL does not.
  • ZL and ZR buttons: the New model has two extra shoulder buttons on top, inside the L and R buttons.
  • Start and Select: on the New model they sit on the right, below the A/B/X/Y buttons. On the original XL they sit below the bottom screen with the Home button.
  • Cartridge slot: on the New model it's on the front bottom edge, next to the stylus. On the original XL it's on the back.

If any of these don't match, title the item as whatever model it really is.

Step 2: Check the region before anything else

3DS consoles are region-locked, so a Japanese or European unit won't play most US cartridges. That matters to US buyers.

  1. Power on and open System Settings.
  2. Read the version number in the top corner of the top screen, for example "Ver. 11.x.x-xxU".
  3. The last letter is the region: U = Americas, E = Europe/Australia, J = Japan.

If it isn't U and you sell in the US, put the region in the title and the first line of the description.

Step 3: Physical inspection (before you charge it)

Do this under a bright light. Take photos as you go.

  • Hinge: open and close it fully. Look for cracks at both hinge corners and feel for looseness or grinding.
  • Top screen: XL models often get scratches on the top screen from the buttons or bottom screen when closed. Tilt it against the light to find them.
  • Bottom screen: check for scratches and for any screen protector that's peeling or bubbled.
  • Battery bulge: set the console flat on a table. If it rocks or the back plate bulges, the battery may be swelling. Don't keep charging it. Sell it as needing a battery, or replace the battery first.
  • Shell: cracks, missing screws, stickers, sticker residue and engraving. Missing rubber feet under the top screen are also worth noting.
  • Ports: look into the charge port and the headphone jack for debris or bent pins.
  • Stylus: check whether it's present. The stylus lives in the bottom edge, next to the cartridge slot.
  • Back plate: the New 3DS XL holds its microSD card under the back plate, behind two small captive screws. Open it if you can and confirm the card is there. Note its size.

Step 4: Power and charging

  1. Plug in the charger. The orange charge light near the hinge should come on.
  2. Power on. Both screens should light evenly, and the blue power light should come on.
  3. Wiggle the charger plug gently. If the charge light flickers, note that the port is loose.

You can't measure battery health on the console. Just note whether it holds a charge through your whole test session. Don't claim anything about how long the battery lasts unless you've actually timed it.

Step 5: Input test (every button, every stick)

Most returns start here, so be thorough.

  • Circle Pad: go to System Settings, then Other Settings, then Circle Pad. Run the calibration screen. Let go of the pad and watch whether the dot drifts away from center. Then roll it in full circles and check that it reaches every edge.
  • C-Stick: it barely moves by design (it senses pressure). Test it in a game that uses it, or in any menu that responds to it.
  • Touch screen: go to System Settings, then Other Settings, then Touch Screen. Calibrate it, then tap the corners and edges. Dead spots usually show up near the edges.
  • Buttons: press A, B, X, Y, the D-pad in all four directions, L, R, ZL, ZR, Start, Select and Home. You should get a clean click and a response every time. Shoulder buttons often wear out and need a hard press, so press each one 10+ times.
  • Power button and volume slider: slide the volume all the way down and up while sound plays.
  • 3D slider: slide it up from off. The top screen should switch into 3D with no flicker. The New 3DS XL tracks your face with the inside camera to keep the 3D stable. Move your head side to side a little and check that the 3D stays clear.
  • Wireless switch: the New XL has no physical Wi-Fi switch. Check Wi-Fi in Step 7.

Step 6: Cameras, speakers, microphone

  • Cameras: open the Nintendo 3DS Camera app. Take a photo with the inside camera. Then take a 3D photo with the outer cameras, which uses both lenses. Look for black images, heavy blur or dust spots.
  • Speakers: play sound in the Nintendo 3DS Sound app with the 3D effect off, and listen to the left and right speakers separately. Crackling or one silent side is a defect you must disclose.
  • Headphone jack: plug in earbuds and check that sound switches over cleanly.
  • Microphone: record a short clip in the Sound app and play it back.

Step 7: Cartridge, Wi-Fi and amiibo/NFC

  • Cartridge slot: insert a known-good 3DS game and launch it. If you can, test an original DS game too. Play for a few minutes.
  • Wi-Fi: go to System Settings, then Internet Settings, and run a connection test on your network. Afterwards, delete your network.
  • NFC (amiibo): the reader is under the bottom screen. If you have an amiibo, open System Settings, go to amiibo Settings and tap it on the bottom screen. If you don't have one, list NFC as "not tested." Don't claim it works.

Note for buyers: Nintendo ended 3DS eShop purchases in 2023 and online play in 2024. Don't suggest in the listing that a buyer can buy new digital games.

Step 8: Check for modding

A modded 3DS runs custom firmware. Many resellers don't sell them, and if you do, you must disclose it. Signs to look for:

  • Apps on the Home Menu like "FBI," "Homebrew Launcher," "Anemone," "Checkpoint," or any app you don't recognize.
  • A custom boot screen or custom Home Menu theme before you've done anything.
  • The version text in System Settings mentions "Luma."
  • Turn the power off, then hold Select while pressing power. If a text configuration menu appears instead of a normal boot, the console runs custom firmware.

If you find any of these, either describe it accurately in the listing or don't sell it as a stock console. Never hide it.

Step 9: Wipe it (last)

Do this only after testing, because formatting removes any games installed on the system.

  1. Write down any pre-installed digital games first. Mention them in the listing only if you're keeping them installed and will say what's on the console.
  2. Remove any parental controls PIN. If the previous owner set one and you don't know it, say so. Removing it may need Nintendo's help.
  3. To wipe it, go to System Settings, then Other Settings, then Format System Memory. This also removes the previous owner's linked Nintendo Network ID from the console.

Fill-in condition notes for your listing

Copy this, then fill in only what you actually checked:

  • Model: New Nintendo 3DS XL (RED-001), color: ______, region: ______
  • Tested: powers on, charges, both screens, all buttons incl. ZL/ZR, Circle Pad (no drift / drift: ____), C-Stick, touch screen, 3D, cameras (3), speakers, mic, headphone jack, cartridge slot, Wi-Fi
  • Not tested: ______ (e.g., NFC/amiibo)
  • Cosmetic: top screen ______, bottom screen ______, hinge ______, shell ______
  • Included: charger (yes/no), stylus (yes/no), microSD ____GB (yes/no), games: ______
  • Software: factory reset, not modded (or: describe mods)

Quick pass/fail summary

  • Sell as "tested, working": every item in Steps 4-7 passes, it isn't modded, and any cosmetic flaws are photographed and described.
  • Sell with a disclosed defect: small issues, such as a stiff shoulder button, light scratches or no stylus. Price it below clean examples.
  • Sell "for parts or not working": won't power on, dead screen, cracked hinge, swollen battery, or a failed cartridge slot.

Photograph both screens powered on, plus the hinge, the back and every included item. Clear photos and accurate notes like these prevent most "not as described" disputes.
