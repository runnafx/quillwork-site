No-Tech Band & Choir Cover Lesson a Non-Musician Can Run

A complete one-period cover plan for a band, orchestra or choir class, with a cover note template, a worksheet and a full answer key. The cover teacher does not need to read music or play anything.

When an ensemble teacher is off, the usual music cover sheets often don't fit. Most of them are about a composer or a genre. Your class is used to rehearsing, and the cover teacher can't run a rehearsal. Letting students get instruments out with nobody qualified in charge puts equipment at risk, and it can end with a noisy room and a complaint from next door.

This page gives you everything to leave for one period: a cover note you can fill in, a minute-by-minute plan, a rhythm worksheet, and an answer key that someone with no music training can mark. It works for band, orchestra or choir at KS3 (roughly ages 11 to 14) and needs no audio, no screens and no instruments.

Before you leave: the 5-minute checklist

  • Lock or label the instrument store. Put a note on the door saying instruments stay put today, so the cover teacher doesn't have to decide.
  • Print the worksheet below, one per student plus five spares, and leave a pack of pencils. Ensemble rooms often have no pens to hand.
  • Leave a seating plan or a register with any known pairings to keep apart.
  • Write the cover note using the template below and put it on top of the pile.
  • Tell students in advance if you can: "If I'm out, it's a written lesson and it counts."

Cover note template (copy and fill in)

Class: ________ Period/time: ________ Room: ________

What this group usually does: This is a [band / orchestra / choir] class. Normally they rehearse. Today they will not use instruments or sing. It is a written lesson.

Instruments: Please do not allow anyone to take instruments out, including their own. The store is [locked / labelled].

What you need to know: You don't need to read music. Everything you need to mark is on the answer sheet.

Plan: Follow the timings on the next page. Collect all worksheets at the end and leave them [on my desk / in the tray marked ____].

Students who can help: ________ (reliable for handing out sheets, not for running the lesson)

Students to seat apart: ________

If there's a problem: Contact ________ in room ________.

The lesson plan (for a 50 to 60 minute period)

  1. Minutes 0 to 5: Register and settle. Students sit in the seating plan, not their usual ensemble seats. This makes clear that today is not a rehearsal.
  2. Minutes 5 to 10: Read the starter aloud. The cover teacher reads the "Note values" box (below) to the class. No musical knowledge is needed. It's just reading.
  3. Minutes 10 to 30: Part A, rhythm counting. Students work on their own. The cover teacher can mark a few using the answer key while walking round.
  4. Minutes 30 to 45: Part B, how an ensemble works. These are written questions about their own group.
  5. Minutes 45 to 55: Part C, the extension task, for anyone who finishes early. Others keep going with Part B.
  6. Final 5 minutes: Swap and mark Part A. The cover teacher reads the answers aloud from the key, students mark a partner's sheet, then all sheets are collected.

For a shorter period, drop Part C. For a longer one, give Part C to everyone.

Student worksheet

Note values (read this first)

In most music your ensemble plays, the beat is counted in groups of 4. Each note lasts a set number of beats:

  • Semibreve (whole note) = 4 beats
  • Minim (half note) = 2 beats
  • Crotchet (quarter note) = 1 beat
  • Quaver (eighth note) = half a beat
  • Semiquaver (sixteenth note) = a quarter of a beat

Rests (silences) follow the same values. A crotchet rest is 1 beat of silence and a minim rest is 2 beats of silence.

A bar (measure) in 4/4 time holds exactly 4 beats.

Part A: Rhythm counting

Work out the total number of beats in each line.

  1. Minim + crotchet + crotchet
  2. Crotchet + crotchet + crotchet + quaver + quaver
  3. Semibreve + minim
  4. Quaver + quaver + quaver + quaver + minim
  5. Minim rest + crotchet + crotchet rest
  6. Semiquaver × 4 + crotchet + minim
  7. Crotchet + minim + quaver

Each of these is meant to be one full bar of 4/4. Write "complete" if it adds up to 4 beats. If not, write how many beats it is short or over.

  1. Minim + minim
  2. Crotchet + crotchet + crotchet
  3. Minim + crotchet + crotchet + crotchet
  4. Quaver × 6 + crotchet
  5. Semibreve rest

Fill the gap with one note so the bar adds up to 4 beats:

  1. Minim + crotchet + ______
  2. Crotchet + quaver + quaver + ______
  3. Quaver + quaver + minim + ______

Part B: How an ensemble works

Answer in full sentences. Think about your group.

  1. Name the sections in your ensemble. (For example: woodwind, brass, percussion, strings, or soprano/alto/tenor/bass.)
  2. Which section are you in? Describe one job your section does that no other section does.
  3. Why does an ensemble need a conductor or director? Give two reasons.
  4. What should a player or singer do in the first two minutes of a rehearsal, before the conductor starts?
  5. Describe something that goes wrong when one person doesn't count their rests.
  6. A concert is in two weeks and one section keeps coming in early. Write three things that section could do to fix it.
  7. What makes a good ensemble member, apart from being good at their instrument or voice?

Part C: Extension

Write your own 4-bar rhythm in 4/4 using words (for example: "crotchet, crotchet, minim"). Every bar must add up to exactly 4 beats. Use at least one rest and at least one quaver. Under each bar, show your working (for example 1 + 1 + 2 = 4).

Answer key (for the cover teacher)

You don't need to understand the music. Just compare each answer with the list.

Part A, Questions 1 to 7 (total beats)

  1. 4
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 4
  5. 4
  6. 4
  7. 3½ (three and a half)

Questions 8 to 12 (complete, short or over)

  1. Complete
  2. Short by 1 beat
  3. Over by 1 beat
  4. Complete (6 quavers = 3 beats, plus 1)
  5. Complete

Questions 13 to 15 (fill the gap)

  1. Crotchet (or crotchet rest)
  2. Minim (or minim rest)
  3. Crotchet (or crotchet rest)

Accept a rest wherever a note of the same value is correct.

Part B

There's no single right answer, so mark for effort and full sentences. Good answers might say:

  • Q3: The conductor keeps everyone in time, sets the speed, balances loud and quiet sections, and shows when to start and stop.
  • Q4: Get the music in order, sit or stand ready, tune or warm up quietly if the teacher allows it, and watch the conductor.
  • Q5: That person comes in at the wrong time, so the parts clash or the section falls out of sync.
  • Q6: Count rests aloud or in their head, mark entries in pencil, watch the conductor's cue, or practise that passage as a section.

Part C: Check that each bar adds to 4 using the note values box. Bars that add up and include a rest and a quaver are correct.

Tips that make cover go smoothly

  • Say "it's marked" and mean it. When you're back, go through the Part A scores in the next lesson, even briefly. Students take cover work more seriously when it's followed up.
  • Keep a cover set ready all term. Print a class set now and leave it in a labelled folder. Then a sudden absence needs nothing from you except the date on the note.
  • Swap the content each time. Use the same structure (counting, ensemble questions, extension) with new questions, so a second cover lesson doesn't repeat the first.
  • Don't plan sectionals without you. Even confident student leaders can't be supervised properly by a non-specialist in practice rooms. Keep everyone in one room.

If you're often away for trips, concerts or assessments, it's worth building five of these in advance, each on a different topic, so the class never gets the same cover lesson twice.
