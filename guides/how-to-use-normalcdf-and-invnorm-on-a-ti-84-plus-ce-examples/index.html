How to Use normalcdf and invNorm on a TI-84 Plus CE (Examples)

Exact keystrokes for normalcdf and invNorm on the TI-84 Plus CE, with worked examples you can check yourself and the mistakes that cost points on finals and AP Statistics. Covers "between," "less than," "greater than," sample means and percentile cutoffs.

Most normal-distribution questions on a stats final come down to two calculator commands. normalcdf gives you a probability, which is an area under the curve. invNorm goes the other way: you give it an area and it returns a cutoff value. This page covers the exact keystrokes, five worked examples with answers you can check, and the errors students run into most often.

Keys are written in brackets. [2nd] [VARS] means press the blue 2nd key, then the VARS key. That opens the DISTR menu printed above VARS.

Before you start: check two settings

1. Stat Wizards on or off. Press [MODE] and scroll down to STAT WIZARDS.

  • ON means choosing normalcdf opens a fill-in screen with the labels lower, upper, μ and σ. This is the easiest way to work, and the rest of this page assumes it.
  • OFF means the calculator pastes a bare `normalcdf(` and you type the numbers yourself, separated by commas, in this order: lower, upper, μ, σ.

2. The negative key. Negative numbers need the [(-)] key at the bottom of the keypad, next to [ENTER]. The [−] subtraction key will not work and gives ERR: SYNTAX.

The keystrokes

normalcdf (finding a probability)

  1. Press [2nd] [VARS] to open DISTR.
  2. Choose 2: normalcdf(. Either arrow down and press [ENTER], or press [2].
  3. Enter lower, upper, μ (the mean) and σ (the standard deviation). Use the down arrow to move between fields.
  4. Highlight Paste and press [ENTER], then press [ENTER] again to calculate.

invNorm (finding a cutoff from an area)

  1. Press [2nd] [VARS], then choose 3: invNorm(.
  2. Enter area, μ and σ.
  3. On current CE operating systems there's also a Tail option: LEFT, CENTER or RIGHT. LEFT is the default. With LEFT, "area" means the area to the left of the cutoff.
  4. Highlight Paste, then press [ENTER] twice.

How to type "infinity." The calculator has no infinity key, so use a huge number: 1E99.

  • Positive: [1] [2nd] [,] [9] [9]. The "E" is EE, printed above the comma key.
  • Negative: [(-)] [1] [2nd] [,] [9] [9]

Worked examples

All five examples use the same setup: adult heights are normally distributed with mean 170 cm and standard deviation 8 cm.

Example 1: "Between" (P(160 < X < 180))

  • lower: 160
  • upper: 180
  • μ: 170
  • σ: 8

Screen after pasting: `normalcdf(160,180,170,8)` Answer: about 0.7887, meaning about 78.9% of adults are between 160 and 180 cm.

Example 2: "Greater than" (P(X > 185))

There's no upper limit, so use 1E99.

  • lower: 185
  • upper: 1E99
  • μ: 170
  • σ: 8

Screen: `normalcdf(185,1E99,170,8)` Answer: about 0.0304

Example 3: "Less than" (P(X < 150))

There's no lower limit, so use −1E99, typed with the [(-)] key.

  • lower: −1E99
  • upper: 150
  • μ: 170
  • σ: 8

Screen: `normalcdf(-1E99,150,170,8)` Answer: about 0.0062

Example 4: Percentile cutoff with invNorm ("How tall to be in the top 10%?")

Top 10% means 90% of the area lies to the left of the cutoff.

  • area: 0.90
  • μ: 170
  • σ: 8
  • Tail: LEFT

Screen: `invNorm(0.9,170,8)` Answer: about 180.25 cm

If your OS shows the Tail option, you can also enter area 0.10 with Tail set to RIGHT and get the same answer.

Middle 50% (the quartiles): use invNorm(0.25,170,8) ≈ 164.60 and invNorm(0.75,170,8) ≈ 175.40. Alternatively, set Tail to CENTER with area 0.5 and the calculator returns both cutoffs.

Example 5: Sample means ("A random sample of 16 adults. P(x̄ > 173)?")

When the question asks about a sample mean, the spread is the standard error, σ/√n, not σ.

  • Standard error = 8 / √16 = 2
  • lower: 173
  • upper: 1E99
  • μ: 170
  • σ: 2 (you can also type 8/√(16) straight into the σ field)

Screen: `normalcdf(173,1E99,170,2)` Answer: about 0.0668

Using z-scores (standard normal)

If you leave out μ and σ, the calculator assumes the standard normal (μ = 0, σ = 1).

  • `normalcdf(-1.96,1.96)` → about 0.9500
  • `invNorm(0.975)` → about 1.96

This is a quick way to check critical values from a z-table.

Common mistakes

  1. Using normalpdf to get a probability. normalpdf (option 1) gives the height of the curve, not an area. For "what proportion" or "what probability" questions, use normalcdf.
  2. Entering the variance instead of the standard deviation. If the problem says "variance 64," then σ = 8.
  3. Forgetting σ/√n for sample means. If the question mentions a sample of size n and asks about x̄, divide σ by √n.
  4. Typing the minus key for negatives. This gives ERR: SYNTAX. Use [(-)].
  5. Writing 1E99 as "1 × 10^99" by hand on the calculator. That can work, but it's slow and easy to mistype. Use the EE key.
  6. Mixing up left and right area in invNorm. With Tail set to LEFT, a "top 5%" question needs area 0.95, not 0.05.
  7. Entering an area of 0 or 1 or more in invNorm. This gives ERR: DOMAIN. The area must be strictly between 0 and 1. If you typed a percentage like 90, change it to 0.90.
  8. Lower bound bigger than upper bound. normalcdf(180,160,170,8) returns a negative number. If you get a negative probability, swap the two bounds.

Getting full credit on written answers

Teachers and AP Statistics free-response scoring usually expect more than bare calculator syntax. A line like "normalcdf(160,180,170,8)" alone may not get full credit. To be safe, write three things:

  1. Name the distribution and its parameters, for example "X ~ Normal(μ = 170, σ = 8)."
  2. State the probability you're finding, for example "P(160 < X < 180)."
  3. Give the answer in context, for example "About 78.9% of adults are between 160 and 180 cm tall."

If your teacher wants it, add the z-scores: z = (160 − 170)/8 = −1.25 and z = (180 − 170)/8 = 1.25.

Speed tips for exam day

  • Edit instead of retyping. Press [2nd] [ENTER] (ENTRY) to bring back your last command, arrow to the number you want to change, and press [ENTER].
  • Leaving wizards on is fine. If you know the argument order, typing directly with wizards off can be faster, but the labeled fields help you avoid mixing up the order.
  • Do a sanity check. A probability must be between 0 and 1. A cutoff for the "top 10%" has to be above the mean. If your answer breaks either rule, check your tail and your bounds.
  • Check your OS. If your screen doesn't show a Tail option for invNorm, your operating system is older. Use the left-area method shown in Example 4, which works on every version.

Quick reference card

  • P(a < X < b): normalcdf(a, b, μ, σ)
  • P(X > a): normalcdf(a, 1E99, μ, σ)
  • P(X < b): normalcdf(−1E99, b, μ, σ)
  • Cutoff with left area p: invNorm(p, μ, σ)
  • Top p cutoff: invNorm(1 − p, μ, σ)
  • Sample mean: replace σ with σ/√n
