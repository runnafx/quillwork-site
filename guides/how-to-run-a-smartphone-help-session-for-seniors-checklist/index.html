How to Run a Smartphone Help Session for Seniors (Checklist)

A step-by-step plan for activity directors and senior center coordinators running resident phone help sessions. It covers prep, room setup, a 60-minute agenda, large-print handout rules, and a sample one-page guide you can copy.

Resident tech-help sessions are among the most requested programs in many senior living communities. They are also among the hardest to run well. Residents bring different phones and different skill levels, and their questions often have nothing to do with the topic on the flyer. This page gives you a plan you can use next week. It includes a prep checklist, a room setup, a 60-minute agenda, rules for handouts residents can actually read, and a sample one-page guide.

Step 1: Find out which phones your residents actually use

Do this before you plan any content. Steps on an iPhone and steps on an Android phone are different, and steps on a Samsung often differ from steps on a Google Pixel. A session that tries to cover "smartphones" in general usually leaves half the room lost.

Put a short sign-up sheet at the front desk or in the activity calendar. Use this template:

Phone Help Session Sign-Up

  • Name: ____________
  • Room / apartment: ____________
  • What kind of phone do you have? (iPhone / Samsung / other / not sure): ____________
  • What is the ONE thing you most want to learn? ____________
  • Do you use reading glasses or a hearing aid? (yes / no): ____________
  • Can a family member help you bring your charger and passcode? (yes / no): ____________

If a resident writes "not sure," that is normal. You can check at the session. On an iPhone, go to Settings, then General, then About. On most Android phones, go to Settings, then About phone.

Tip: If your sign-ups show two main phone types, plan your handouts for those two. Handle any others one-on-one.

Step 2: Pick one topic per session

One topic per session works better than a tour of the whole phone. These topics usually work well as standalone sessions:

  1. Making and answering calls, including calling favorites
  2. Sending and reading text messages
  3. Video calls with family (FaceTime, WhatsApp or Google Meet)
  4. Looking at photos and sending a photo to someone
  5. Making text bigger and sounds louder
  6. Spotting scam calls and scam texts

Start with "making text bigger and louder" if you are unsure. It often makes every later session easier, because residents can then read their own screens.

Step 3: Prep checklist (the day before)

  • Confirm the topic and post it clearly: "Tuesday 2pm: Make your phone's text bigger."
  • Print handouts for each phone type, plus a few spares.
  • Ask residents to bring their phone, charger, glasses, hearing aids and passcode. A forgotten passcode can stop a session cold, so remind residents the day before.
  • Check the Wi-Fi in the room and write the network name and password in large letters on a whiteboard or card.
  • Line up helpers. One helper for every three to four residents is ideal. Volunteers, staff, family members or local student volunteers all work.
  • Charge a demo phone, if you have one, for each main phone type.
  • Put out power strips near seats for low batteries.

Step 4: Set up the room

  • Seat residents at tables, not in rows, so helpers can lean in.
  • Group residents by phone type: iPhone users at one table, Android users at another.
  • Seat residents with hearing loss closest to the presenter and away from noise such as kitchens, TVs and air vents.
  • Make sure the lighting is bright and avoid glare on screens. Do not seat people facing a sunny window.
  • Keep a "parking lot" sheet on the wall for off-topic questions you will answer at the end or one-on-one later.

Step 5: A 60-minute session agenda

  • 0–10 min: Settle in. Help everyone unlock their phone and connect to Wi-Fi if needed. Hand out the guide for their phone type.
  • 10–15 min: Show the goal. Demonstrate the finished result first, for example "Here's what your text will look like after today."
  • 15–35 min: Do it together, one step at a time. Read one step aloud, wait until every resident has done it, and then move on. Helpers check screens.
  • 35–45 min: Repeat without help. Ask residents to undo the change and do it again using only the handout. This is when the learning sticks.
  • 45–55 min: Parking lot questions.
  • 55–60 min: Wrap up. Remind residents to keep the handout near where they charge their phone. Announce the next topic.

Facilitation tips that make a real difference:

  • Say "tap" instead of "click," and show what a tap looks like.
  • Say the exact words that appear on screen, such as "Tap the word Settings." Avoid "go into your settings."
  • Never take the phone out of a resident's hands to "just do it quickly." Guide their finger instead.
  • Expect phones to look slightly different from your demo. Menu names vary between software versions and manufacturers. When something doesn't match, say so: "Yours may say it a little differently."

Step 6: Make handouts residents can actually read

Manufacturer help pages are written for everyone, so they are often too small, too long or too broad for a group session. Use these rules for your own handouts:

  • Font size 18 point or larger. Use a plain font such as Arial or Verdana.
  • One task per page. "Send a text" gets its own page. It does not share a page with "send a photo."
  • One phone type per page. Do not mix iPhone and Android steps on the same sheet.
  • Numbered steps, one action each. Write "1. Tap Settings," not "Open Settings and find Display."
  • Bold the exact on-screen words residents need to look for.
  • Black text on a white background with high contrast. Avoid colored paper and light gray text.
  • Leave white space. A short page is easier to follow than a crowded one.
  • Add a "Notes" line at the bottom where residents can write family names, numbers or reminders.
  • Put the date and phone type at the top. Phone software changes, so you'll know when a sheet may need updating.

Sample handout: Make the text bigger (iPhone)

You can copy this sample and print it at 18pt or larger. Check it against a current iPhone before printing, because Apple sometimes moves or renames menu items in updates.

Make the Words on Your iPhone Bigger

  1. Find the gray gear picture labeled Settings and tap it.
  2. Scroll down and tap Display & Brightness.
  3. Tap Text Size.
  4. You will see a slider at the bottom. Put your finger on the dot and slide it to the right.
  5. Stop when the words are easy to read.
  6. Press the Home button or swipe up from the bottom to go back.

Need it even bigger? Go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, then Larger Text. Turn on Larger Accessibility Sizes and slide to the right.

Notes: ______________________

Sample handout: Make the text bigger (Samsung Galaxy)

Menu names on Samsung phones can vary by model and software version. Test these steps on a resident's phone before printing.

Make the Words on Your Samsung Phone Bigger

  1. Find the gear picture labeled Settings and tap it.
  2. Tap Display.
  3. Tap Font size and style.
  4. Slide the Font size dot to the right.
  5. Stop when the words are easy to read.
  6. Tap the back arrow or swipe to go home.

Want everything bigger, not just words? In Display, look for Screen zoom and slide it to the right.

Notes: ______________________

Step 7: After the session

  • Write down which steps confused people, and fix those lines on the handout.
  • Use the parking lot questions to plan your next topic.
  • Keep a master copy of each handout and re-check it after major phone updates. Apple and Google typically release one major update each year.
  • Offer brief drop-in help for residents who missed the session or need a repeat.

Quick checklist to print

  • [ ] Sign-up sheet collected phone types and top questions
  • [ ] One topic chosen
  • [ ] Handouts printed per phone type, 18pt+
  • [ ] Residents reminded: phone, charger, glasses, passcode
  • [ ] Wi-Fi details posted large
  • [ ] Helpers lined up (about one per three to four residents)
  • [ ] Tables grouped by phone type
  • [ ] Parking lot sheet on wall
  • [ ] Notes taken afterward for next session
