How to Rewrite Manufacturer Product Descriptions: Template + Example

A step-by-step method for turning copied supplier text into your own product descriptions for Shopify, Etsy or WooCommerce. It includes a fact sheet, a fill-in template and a full before-and-after example.

If you resell or dropship, your product pages may still carry the supplier's text. That same text probably sits on dozens of other stores. It wasn't written for your customers, and it often reads like a spec sheet translated twice.

Google doesn't punish a store just for having duplicate text. But when many pages say exactly the same thing, search engines usually show one of them and filter out the rest, and your page may not be the one shown. Shoppers who compare tabs also notice when your page matches everyone else's word for word.

This guide shows you how to rewrite one product in about 20–30 minutes. You don't have to invent anything. You only reorganize and translate facts you already have.

Step 1: Pull the facts out before you write anything

Don't edit the supplier's paragraph line by line. You'll end up with the same text in a new order. First, take the facts out of it and put them into a plain list. Then close the original.

Fact sheet (copy this for each product):

  • Product name (your name for it, not the SKU):
  • What it is, in five words or fewer:
  • Materials:
  • Dimensions / sizes / weight:
  • Colors or variants:
  • What's included in the box:
  • Care or use instructions:
  • Who it's for (one specific person, not "everyone"):
  • The problem it solves or the moment it's used in:
  • Anything you've checked yourself (handled it, measured it, used it):
  • Things you're NOT sure about (leave these out of the copy until confirmed):
  • 3 search phrases a buyer might type:

The "not sure about" line matters. Supplier text often claims things like "waterproof," "BPA-free," "hypoallergenic" or "eco-friendly." If you can't back a claim up with a certificate, a test, or the supplier's written spec, leave it out. A false claim on your page is your problem, not the supplier's.

Step 2: Pick one buyer and one moment

Generic copy happens when you write for "customers." Pick one person and one situation:

  • "A renter who wants plants on a windowsill but has no outdoor space"
  • "Someone buying a gift for a coworker under $30"
  • "A parent packing school lunches every morning"

Everything in the description should make sense to that person in that moment.

Step 3: Turn features into benefits (the "so that" test)

Take each fact from your sheet and finish the sentence "...so that you can..." If you can't finish it, the fact goes in the specs, not the main copy.

  • "Stainless steel lid" → so that it doesn't stain or hold smells
  • "12 cm diameter" → so that it fits a standard windowsill
  • "Drainage hole with removable plug" → so that you can choose between drainage and a drip-free shelf

Step 4: Fill in the description template

Here's a structure that works for most physical products. Aim for 120–180 words in the main description. That's long enough to answer the real questions and short enough to read on a phone.

Fill-in template:

  1. Opening line (1 sentence): Who it's for + the moment they'll use it. "For [buyer] who [situation], this [product] [main result]."
  2. What makes it work (2–3 sentences): Your top two or three benefits, each tied to a real fact from your sheet.
  3. What it's like to use (1–2 sentences): A concrete detail. How it feels, sits, opens, cleans, or fits.
  4. Practical reassurance (1 sentence): Care, size guidance, or what's included.
  5. Closing line (optional, 1 sentence): A plain nudge, like "Pairs well with..." or "A good fit if...". Skip fake urgency.

Then add:

  • 5 benefit bullets. Each starts with the benefit and ends with the fact. "Fits most windowsills — 12 cm wide, 11 cm tall."
  • Specs block. Dimensions, materials and what's included as a plain list. Shoppers scan for this.

Step 5: Write the SEO title and meta description

On Shopify, open the product and scroll to "Search engine listing." There you can set a page title and meta description separately from the on-page product title. WooCommerce usually needs an SEO plugin for this. On Etsy, the listing title and tags do the job instead.

  • SEO title: keep it to roughly 60 characters or fewer so it's less likely to be cut off in search results. Format: Main search phrase + key detail + brand or shop name.
  • Meta description: roughly 155 characters or fewer. Say what it is, who it's for, and one concrete detail. Google sometimes writes its own snippet anyway, but a good one gives it something useful to use.
  • Etsy note: Etsy titles can be up to 140 characters and you get 13 tags. Put the clearest description first and use the tags for the other phrasings buyers search for.

Use your 3 search phrases naturally: once in the SEO title, once in the first sentence, and once in a bullet or the meta description. Repeating a phrase more than that doesn't help and makes the copy harder to read.

Worked example: before and after

This example uses an invented product, so you can see the whole method from start to finish.

Before (typical supplier text):

"High quality ceramic flower pot. Modern design suitable for home office decoration. Perfect for succulents cactus plants. Durable and beautiful. Size: 12x11cm. Color: matte white. Drainage hole. Bamboo tray included. Great gift for friends."

Fact sheet, filled in:

  • What it is: small ceramic plant pot with tray
  • Materials: ceramic, matte glaze; bamboo tray
  • Size: 12 cm wide, 11 cm tall
  • Variant: matte white
  • Included: pot, bamboo tray
  • Drainage: one hole in the base
  • Buyer: renter with a windowsill or desk, growing succulents
  • Not sure about: "durable" (no test data), so it's left out
  • Search phrases: small succulent pot with drainage, ceramic pot with bamboo tray, white windowsill planter

After: description (about 130 words):

For anyone growing succulents on a windowsill or desk, this small ceramic pot gives one plant a tidy home that won't leave water rings behind.

The drainage hole in the base lets extra water run out, so roots aren't left sitting in a soggy pot, which is one of the most common ways succulents fail. The bamboo tray underneath catches the runoff and protects your sill or shelf.

At 12 cm wide and 11 cm tall, it fits most windowsills and leaves room on a crowded desk. The matte white glaze keeps the focus on the plant and goes with most rooms.

To water, soak the soil, let it drain, then empty the tray. Wipe the ceramic with a damp cloth when needed.

A good fit for a first succulent, or as a small gift with a plant already potted.

Bullets:

  • Roots stay out of standing water — drainage hole in the base
  • Protects surfaces — bamboo tray included
  • Fits small spaces — 12 cm wide, 11 cm tall
  • Goes with most decor — matte white glaze
  • Easy to keep clean — wipes down with a damp cloth

SEO title (52 characters): Small Succulent Pot with Drainage & Bamboo Tray

Meta description (about 140 characters): Matte white ceramic succulent pot, 12 cm wide, with drainage hole and bamboo tray. Sized for windowsills and desks.

Look at what changed. Nothing was invented. "Durable" and "high quality" are gone because nothing backed them up. Every remaining sentence traces back to a line on the fact sheet.

Final checklist before you hit publish

  • [ ] Every claim traces back to a fact you can support
  • [ ] No unverified words like "waterproof," "non-toxic," "eco-friendly," "medical-grade" or "best"
  • [ ] The first sentence names who it's for or what moment it's for
  • [ ] Main description is roughly 120–180 words
  • [ ] 5 bullets, each with a benefit and a fact
  • [ ] Specs listed separately (size, material, what's included)
  • [ ] SEO title around 60 characters or fewer; meta description around 155 or fewer
  • [ ] Main search phrase used naturally, not stuffed
  • [ ] Sizes and units match your variant settings and size chart
  • [ ] Read it aloud once. If a sentence sounds like a robot or a catalog, rewrite it

How to handle a whole catalog

If you have 20–50 products, don't rewrite them all at once. Start with your best sellers and the products you advertise, since those pages get the most visits. Make a fact sheet for each one in a spreadsheet, one row per product and one column per field. Gaps show up right away, and filling in the template goes much faster.

Products from the same supplier often share a lot of facts. Write the shared parts (material, care, what's included) once and reuse them. Make each opening line and benefit section specific to the product, so no two pages read the same.

Summary

Before you rewrite anything, fill in a fact sheet. Then write for one buyer and one moment, link every benefit to a fact you can support, and keep the SEO title and meta description short. That's how you get product pages that read as your own and still don't promise anything you can't stand behind.
