How to Reply to a 1-Star Google Review: Templates for Local Shops

A step-by-step method for answering negative Google reviews calmly and specifically, with fill-in templates for auto shops, salons, barbers, cafés, groomers and boutiques. Includes a worked example and a pre-posting checklist.

How to Reply to a 1-Star Google Review (Without Making It Worse)

A bad review with no owner response stays on your listing, and every future customer who scrolls down can read it. You can't always get a review removed, but you can decide what sits next to it. A calm, specific reply shows readers how you treat people when something goes wrong.

This page gives you a repeatable method, templates you can fill in, and a worked example. It is written for owner-run local businesses.

Before You Type Anything

  • Wait until you're calm. A reply written while you're angry usually reads as defensive. Waiting a few hours is fine.
  • Read the review twice. Find the one or two concrete complaints, such as a long wait, a price surprise, a haircut that wasn't what they asked for, or a car returned with the same problem.
  • Check your records. Look for the visit, the ticket or the booking. You don't need to mention what you find. It helps you reply accurately.
  • Remember who you're writing for. The reviewer may never come back. The people reading your reply later are deciding whether to book with you.

The 5-Part Reply Structure

Most good replies to negative reviews follow this order. Keep the whole thing to about 3 to 6 sentences.

  1. Thank them or acknowledge them, using their name if it's shown. For example: "Hi Dana, thanks for taking the time to write this."
  2. Name the specific issue in neutral words. This proves you actually read the review. For example: "I'm sorry your oil change took over two hours when you were quoted 45 minutes."
  3. Take ownership where it's fair, without arguing. For example: "That's longer than it should have been, and we should have updated you while you waited." If you disagree with part of the review, leave that part out, or state facts briefly and politely. Don't argue in public.
  4. Say what you're doing or have done, if it's true. For example: "We've changed how we give time estimates on busy mornings." Only include this if you really did it.
  5. Invite them to continue offline. Use a real contact route and name a person. For example: "If you're open to it, please call the shop and ask for Mike. I'd like to make this right."

What Not to Put in a Public Reply

  • Private details. Don't mention what they bought, their appointment history, their vehicle details, their pet's health or anything else they didn't post themselves.
  • Blame. Avoid lines like "If you had arrived on time…" or "Most customers love us." Readers see it as excuse-making.
  • Copy-paste sameness. Ten identical "We're sorry for your experience, please contact us" replies read as automated and careless.
  • Offers tied to changing the review. Don't offer a discount or refund in exchange for editing or removing a review. Google's policies prohibit incentivizing reviews. You can offer to fix a problem. Just don't attach it to the review.
  • Arguments about whether they're a "real" customer. If you truly can't find them, say so neutrally. For example: "I couldn't find your visit in our records. Could you call us so we can look into it?"
  • Legal threats or accusations. These rarely help and often get screenshotted.

If a review looks fake, spammy or abusive, you can flag it through your Google Business Profile. Google decides whether to remove it, and removal isn't guaranteed. Write a calm reply anyway in case the review stays up.

Fill-In Templates

Replace the brackets. Then change at least one sentence so it sounds like you.

Template 1: Long wait or slow service

"Hi [Name], thank you for the feedback. I'm sorry you waited [time] for [service]. That's longer than we aim for, and we should have kept you updated. [What you've changed, if anything.] If you're willing, please reach out to [person] at [phone/email]. I'd like to hear more and make it right."

Template 2: Result wasn't what they wanted (haircut, color, grooming, repair)

"Hi [Name], I'm sorry the [cut/color/groom/repair] didn't turn out the way you hoped. Getting [specific thing they mentioned] right matters to us. We'd like the chance to look at it and talk through options. Please contact [person] at [phone/email] when it suits you."

Template 3: Price surprise or billing complaint

"Hi [Name], thanks for letting us know. I'm sorry the final bill was a surprise. You should have had a clear estimate before we started. I'd like to go over the charges with you directly. Please reach out to [person] at [phone/email]."

Template 4: Rude or unhelpful staff

"Hi [Name], I'm sorry about how you were treated during your visit. That isn't how we want anyone to feel here. I've shared your feedback with the team. If you're open to it, I'd appreciate hearing more. You can reach me directly at [phone/email]."

Template 5: Can't find the customer, or the details don't match

"Hi [Name], thank you for posting this. I wasn't able to find your visit in our records, and I'd like to understand what happened. Could you contact us at [phone/email] with the date of your visit so we can look into it?"

Template 6: Star rating with no text

"Hi [Name], thanks for the rating. I'm sorry we didn't meet your expectations. If you're willing to share what went wrong, we'd like to hear it. Please reach out at [phone/email]."

Worked Example

The review (2 stars): "Brought my car in for brakes. They said it'd be ready by 3, didn't call, I showed up at 3:30 and it wasn't even started. Guy at the desk acted like it was my fault. Won't be back."

A reply that makes it worse: "We were extremely busy that day and told you it MIGHT be ready by 3. Our staff is always professional. Sorry you feel that way."

This reply argues the timeline, defends the staff without acknowledging anything, and uses "sorry you feel that way," which reads as dismissive.

A reply that helps: "Hi Chris, thank you for the honest feedback. I'm sorry your car wasn't started when you came at 3:30, and that nobody called to tell you about the delay. That's on us. You also shouldn't have been made to feel it was your fault. We now call customers as soon as we know a job is running behind. If you're willing, please call the shop and ask for Dave. I'd like to talk this through."

Why it works:

  • It names both complaints, the delay and the attitude.
  • It accepts responsibility without groveling.
  • It describes a concrete change. Only write a line like this if it's true.
  • It gives a named person and a clear next step.
  • It reads well to a stranger deciding whether to book a brake job.

Matching Your Shop's Voice

Templates are a starting point. Adjust them so the replies sound like the person who runs the place.

  • Café or boutique: warmer and more casual. "So sorry about that, we really appreciate you telling us."
  • Auto repair: plain and practical, focused on timelines, estimates and what was fixed.
  • Salon, barber or groomer: personal, with an offer to see them again or talk through options.

Sign with your first name if you're the owner. "– Maria, owner" makes a reply feel human.

Clearing a Backlog of Unanswered Reviews

If you have several old negative reviews with no reply:

  1. Sort your reviews by lowest rating in your Google Business Profile.
  2. Start with the most recent and most detailed negative reviews. These are the most likely to be read.
  3. Reply to older ones too. A late reply is still visible to future readers. You can open with "Sorry for the slow response" and then use the same structure.
  4. Vary your wording so the replies don't look pasted.
  5. Answer positive reviews as well when you have time. A short, specific thank-you is enough.

Pre-Posting Checklist

Before you hit "Reply," check each item:

  • Used their name, if one is shown
  • Named the specific complaint
  • No blame, sarcasm or "sorry you feel that way"
  • No private customer details
  • No discount or refund offered in exchange for changing the review
  • Any change you mention is actually true
  • Includes a real contact person and method
  • About 3 to 6 sentences
  • Read aloud once, and it sounds like you
  • Proofread for typos

You can edit your reply later from your Business Profile if you spot a mistake. It's still better to get it right before posting, since people may see the first version.
